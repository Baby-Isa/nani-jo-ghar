## The answer in short

- **Every word group the child needs has a home, but the homes don't yet hold A2.** Counted word by word (Part 2), the settings and games as planned can carry about **650-700 of the ~1,000 A2 words**: a solid A1, about 300 short of A2.
- **Where the shortfall is:** describing words, verbs, numbers above ten, time words, and the health half of feelings.
- **How to close it:** later visits that add items and levels (the template's "later visits"), and the story library (idea 62). It isn't more settings: the average game carries about 15-20 words, and A2 needs about 25.
- **Little of it is playable today.** Most homes are new, designed or parked, and only about 100 of the ~1,000 target words are heard in a family take. The plan is sound; Mum's words and the builds are the work.
- **Verbs:** 30 of the top 40 have a game where the verb decides a move (Part 3). The missing 13 are mostly "mind" verbs (know, think, remember) and need their own moments.

## How to read it

**Status letters:**
- **B** built on the shared kit, in the labs
- **G** greybox: a parked mode's own engine; moving it onto the kit is a rebuild (R7)
- **D** designed in a doc, not built
- **N** new (31 Oct)
- **P** parked

**Rules for every game** (R6):
1. The heard word decides the move (non-negotiable 4).
2. **Two words per move from level 2:** who and where, which and how many.
3. **English placeholders are a leak for readers** until Mum records the words. A game counts as teaching only with its deciding words recorded.
4. A miss gets a different funny reaction, never a frown (non-negotiable 7).

The **Leak** column names the leak the research found and the fix.

Word groups go by name, not number: the word-groups map and the wordlist research number them differently from group 31 on (R10).

## Part 4: what it shows

1. **Holds A1, short of A2 by about 300 words** (Part 2). Building every game here reaches about 650-700 words.
   - **Biggest shortfalls:** describing words (35 short), time (20), numbers (18: the tens), feelings and health (15), family (10), places (10), school (15), toys (7).
   - **Most of these close cheaply with a later visit's extra items.** The rest come from the story library, which is A2 listening by design.
2. **Engines, skins and games.** Counting engines once:
   - **Built engines on the kit:** Cook's kit, the heal-game pattern, Conversations, the window ride, the door, the book.
   - **Greybox (a rebuild each):** Snap, put it in its place (Tidy), Find it, Who did it?, Dress up, Monsoon.
   - **Designed:** pack the bag.
   - **New:** give-to, "do it" (statues, the clean-up, getting ready), the blackboard, the clock, fill-and-stack.

   About 18 engines carry the 45 games above. Most new games are skins of six of them.
3. **Recording is the real blocker.** About 100 of the ~995 target words are heard in a family take today. The deciding words each new game needs are its first job (Part 5).
4. **Imbalances:**
   - numbers and colours ride everywhere with almost no clips
   - the body lives only in the clinic
   - play verbs live only at the party
   - "careful" now has its homes: the foot, the ear, the stir
5. **Against Table A's 30 games:**
   - split: the clinic into four, the party into six, the farm into six, the beach into four, the school into three
   - restored: the eggs station and the beach's stages (R4)
   - parked, not dropped: Monsoon, hic, hair, tummy
   - folded: the breakfast skin into the eggs station
   - added: musical statues, look after the guests
