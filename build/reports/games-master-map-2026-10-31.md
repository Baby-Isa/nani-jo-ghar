## Part 4: what the grids show

**Coverage.** Of the 34 word groups still in:
- 20 are **covered** (a main home and others).
- 8 have **one home**: body, home and rooms, weather, wild animals, pets, places in town, transport and school. Each has a game made for it, so that's fine; only body is thin for its size (about 25 words, all in the clinic).
- 3 are **woven** (colours, shapes and sizes, describing words): no game of their own, by Zafar's choice.
- Jobs are dotted by design, sports are thin (a sport's bag), and music is left.
- Nothing the child needs at A2 is without a home, except days of the week (left) and shapes (waiting on Mum's words).

**Imbalances:**
1. **Numbers and colours are everywhere**: numbers in 10 settings, colours in 7. Numbers decide most heal-game moves, yet no number and only one colour (red) has a clip of its own. Recording numbers 1-10 and the basic colours unlocks more games than any other words.
2. **The body sits in one setting, and the clinic teaches it mainly through the diagnosis.** Body words should ride elsewhere too: the school's getting ready (wash your face), musical statues (touch your nose), the adoption centre (the bee's wings, the dog's paw).
3. **Verbs:** hands-on verbs are everywhere, while play verbs (catch, hide, chase, win) live only at the party. Hide and seek could carry "hide" and "find"; the safari "run, climb, swim".

**Build once, use everywhere** (the audit found these coded several times over):
- one **give it to the right person** (the party, the farm, Who did it?)
- one **tap the named thing** (the pantry, the bag, Find it, Snap, the ride)
- one **"say it, and Ali does it"** speaking moment (six copies today)
- **put it in its place** with the relations checker
- **openables with a reveal**
- **Cook's order card and kit**

New engines needed:
- "do it to the thing" (the clean-up, the school's getting ready)
- the blackboard
- the clock
- a small pose picker (statues)
- the Dress up rebuild (sewing)

**Weak on outside evidence: prototype small, then decide.**
- the wake-up clock (a lab test first)
- litter fishing (one stage of the boat day)
- clean-up day (only if the house reacts like a toy)
- musical statues (plus an off-screen round)
- the school routine (the order said, not known)

**The real blocker is still Mum's words:** body (about 20), feelings, the house's places and containers, home verbs, the places in town, numbers 1-10 and colours. Each design should list its words first, so her next round can be planned from the designs.

**Proposed design order** (strongest evidence and most reuse first):
1. hide and seek

3. the party: look after the guests, gifts to the right person, introductions

## Part 5: open questions for Zafar

1. "A part the diagnosis can name must be treatable": does that mean any part it can name (we're 7 short: hand, finger, elbow, shoulder, neck, chest, tummy) or only the part said as the sore one (we already meet it)?
2. The nose game: is taking something out of the nose safe to show (a child may copy it)? The clinic already avoids pins for this reason.
3. Two parked heal games' stories mention a wedding (tummy: too many sweets; foot: dancing all night). Do they move to the birthday, like the Eid lines?
4. Did "the lice game was a joke" mean the parked hair game is out?
5. Musical statues: what music (or a clapping beat)?
