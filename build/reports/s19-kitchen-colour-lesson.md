# Why the polished kitchen came out colder (11 Nov)

Zafar: the original (the open-door kitchen) looks like a beautiful home; the polished one "looks sad, clinical, cold".

**Measured** (CIELAB, the same regions): overall warmth b* 16 -> 8; the wall from #d8b18c (b* 24, peach) to #ddd0c1 (b* 9, grey
cream); the cabinets cooler and greener (a* -10 -> -15); the marble the same; overall contrast the same (std L* 18.1 both). So the
loss is warmth and light direction, not brightness or contrast.

**Fable (blind, which is better): the original**, for its warm upper-left sun and wall gradient, one warm white balance, its depth
(the open door, the lit floor) and an olive sage lit by the room. Its flaws: the wall too peach (near skin tone) and an acid
yellow-green garden.

**The cause: the orchestrator's polish brief gave a scene the cut-out pieces' rules.** "Soft even daylight, no hard window
shadows" took the key light out (it contradicts the art bible's day light); "marble with cool grey veins" split the white balance;
"hazy muted garden" and "nothing competes with the faces" flattened it; naming Ecru and one sage gave flat swatches, not lit
surfaces.

**The colour book is right for pieces, wrong as the governor of a scene.** For items and UI that must sit together, named colours
from one family work (the S19 items). A scene is governed by its light and white balance, a value plan and a chroma ceiling, with
the book naming local colours only. Written into the art bible ("Scenes: light first").
