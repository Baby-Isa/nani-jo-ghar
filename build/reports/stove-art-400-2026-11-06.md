## 3. The size rule

**My opinion: Zafar is right, and the rule should be a little gentler at the small end, and live in the data, not the picture.**

- **Why not true sizes:** at 740x320 the whole shelf slot is 50 CSS px tall. True scale (a spice jar a third of the bottle) would
  draw the jars 17 px tall, their contents a few pixels: unreadable, and below any tap-target guide (Apple 44 pt, Material 48 dp).
- **What practice does:** icon sets keep one optical size and let shape vary only a little: Material's system icons fit a 24 dp grid
  with keylines of 18 (square), 20 (circle) and 20 by 16 (rectangles), so siblings differ by at most about 10-20%, never by real
  size. Picture-word books for small children do the same: one object per cell, all about the same size. And people's preferred
  drawn size of an object grows with the **logarithm** of its real size (Konkle and Oliva, 2011, "Canonical visual size for
  real-world objects"): the steps between small, medium and large should be equal ratios, and small.
- **Why 85 rather than 80 at the bottom:** the small class is the spice jars, the ones he worried about. A jar's contents scale with
  its area: at 85% they keep 72% of the large's area, at 80% only 64%; at 740x320 that is 42.5 against 40 CSS px tall. 100, 92, 85
  are even steps (each about 8%, a step the eye sees side by side), so the order reads without any item becoming hard to see.

**The rule.** Classes L 100%, M 92%, S 85% of the slot's height, by real size (over 20 cm, 10-20 cm, under 10 cm); W for wide things
(width at most the slot's width less 12 design px, and at most 1.2 times the slot's height; its height then follows). Measured on the
cut-out's opaque height (alpha over 60, as `St.opaqueSpan`) against the slot's height (chai: `St.shelfFit`'s room, 155 design px:
50 CSS px at 740x320, 68 at 930x430, 91 at 1280x665, 146 at 1920x1080). Top-down: the same steps against the station's well,
on the opaque box's longer side. The order never inverts in one row. Station pieces and long tools are outside the rule.
**The class is data** (`size` per item in `data/cook.json`), and the art draws everything the same height, so the steps are exact.

**How it is checked.** (1) `artjudge.py`: every cut-out fills at least 70% of its cell's height (or width for W), so resolution
is never the limit. (2) A new review check, `sizecheck` (a short page script): at chai L1-L4 and the four sizes, each shelf item's
opaque height on screen divided by the slot's room equals its class within 3%; no S taller than an M or M than an L in a row; the
S floor at 740x320 at least 42 CSS px. (3) `artjudge.py`'s batch check (the colour tool, tonight's other task) measures the lid and
gloss across a sheet against FA01.
