# Games at a glance (1 November 2026)

How each game plays, in plain words, to read beside the ChatGPT mock-ups.

**How to use it:** find the day, then the game. Open the pictures named under each game (each is saved as `mockup-<name>.png`, so `hide-and-seek-1` is `mockup-hide-and-seek-1.png`). Then read the lines below them. A picture often shows a harder level or a later visit; the line after its name says which moment it is.

## The eleven days and their games

1. **Kitchen:** eggs at breakfast, baking.
2. **Clinic:** where does it hurt?, the scrape, the hand splinter, the blocked nose, the drinks.
3. **A day at home:** hide and seek, pick it up and put it back, the clean-up.
4. **Someone's birthday:** pass the balloon, how are you?, musical statues, the cake, Snap the guests, the party presents, who ate the cake?
5. **School day:** the wake-up clock, getting ready, pack the school bag, the blackboard.
6. **Town and shops:** the town map, serve at the fruit stall, Nani's picky shopping, pay, Big Ma's cloth.
7. **Sewing with Big Ma:** the cloth stall, make, mend, the fitting.
8. **The farm:** feeding time, collect the eggs, the photo shoot, the corn stall, home at dusk, pick, plant and water.
9. **The pet hotel:** look after the animals, going home.
10. **The safari:** Snap from the jeep, who's hiding?
11. **The beach (and the boat):** shells, the sandcastle, the three stalls, the picnic, the ride on the water, litter fishing.

## The same in every game

So each game below can leave these out:
- Someone on screen speaks first. Then a picture card pops up and flies into the list on the left, one row per ask.
- The first time, one thing glows gold to show what to tap. After that the child is on their own.
- A wrong tap gets a funny reaction, never a frown. The line is said again and the child tries again straight away.
- A row ticks gold when it's right first time, and plain after a miss. Only right-first-time rounds move the child up a level.
- A count ticks the moment the right number is reached, and an extra tap doesn't land. Half ticks at half.
- Snap is an instant camera: each right shot prints and ticks at once.
- Paying is always the same: count out the coins and hand them over, with ready-made stacks for small prices.
- No timers, no Done buttons. Each level adds one thing to what is heard; what you tap stays the same.
- For now the prototypes play the family recordings that exist and the device voice for the rest.

## 1. Kitchen (Nani)

### Eggs at breakfast

**The pictures:** `cook-baking-eggs-1` (the crack), `cook-baking-eggs-4` (breakfast served)

**What you're doing:** it's breakfast at Nani's, and each person at the table wants their egg boiled or fried.

**What you tap:**
1. An egg from the bowl, then the pot or the pan.
2. The egg when its ring is in the green, then that person's plate.
3. The tray, to carry the plates to the table.

**What you hear and what decides it:** "I want a boiled egg." The eggs all look the same, so only the cooking word picks the pot or the pan. Later, who it's for picks the plate.

**When it's done:** each plate ticks when it holds the right eggs, and the tray carries them over. Nana taps his boiled egg in its egg cup, "tok tok", and slices the top off. A plate with the wrong egg shakes and comes back.

**The fun bit:** the crack: one knock, the yolk slides out and wobbles, then the sizzle. And the spare egg, which Nani claims for herself.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: one person, one egg; boil or fry.
- L2: two people, how many eggs, "with chilli" or "no chilli".
- L3: Nani passes on every order with no faces, so the name picks the plate.
- L4: soft or hard: "take it out now" or "leave it a bit longer".

**Words it teaches:** egg, boil, fry, crack, pot, frying pan, for Nana, breakfast.

### Baking

**The pictures:** `cook-baking-eggs-2` (the pour with no line), `cook-baking-eggs-3` (the oven), `cook-baking-eggs-5` (Simba's sneeze)

**What you're doing:** in the golden evening you bake dinner's naan or bread (or the party cake) with Nani.

**What you tap:**
1. Press and hold a jar or bottle to pour into a clear cup with no lines; let go.
2. Tap the cup to tip it into the bowl, then tap the bowl to knead.
3. At the oven, tap the door when Nani says it's ready.

**What you hear and what decides it:** the amount: "a little water", "a whole cup of flour". The cup has no marks, so the heard amount is the only ruler. At the oven, "take it out now" or "leave it a bit longer".

**When it's done:** the row ticks the moment the amount is reached (half at half); more doesn't go in. Let go too early and nothing happens; just hold again. The bake comes out and Nana tears the naan: "aah". Nothing ever burns.

**The fun bit:** the level climbing the glass with a rising sound. A full cup of flour goes in with a puff, and Simba sneezes. The naan puffs up in bubbles behind the oven glass.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: a little or a whole cup; one bake.
- L2: numbers of cups, "no sugar"; two bakes, each with its own word.
- L3: "a lot", "heaped", "a bit more"; whose bake comes out now.
- L4: half a cup, two and a half; salt beside sugar; three bakes.

**Words it teaches:** a little, a whole cup, a lot, half, more, flour, water, knead, oven, take it out now.

## 2. Clinic (the doctor)

### Where does it hurt?

**The pictures:** `clinic-every-part-1` (the body chart)

**What you're doing:** a patient sits on the bed and tells the doctor what hurts; you find the part.

**What you tap:**
1. The part on the patient's body.
2. For the face, tap the head first: it zooms in. Then tap the eye, ear, nose or mouth.
3. Later the hand and the foot zoom too, for fingers and toes.

**What you hear and what decides it:** "My knee hurts." The body shows nothing, and the patient looks at the doctor, never at the sore part, so only the body word picks.

**When it's done:** a right tap gets "ow!" and a soft red glow. Then the pharmacy brings the cure and one of the four cure games below starts. A wrong tap gets a giggle, a toe wriggle or a shrug, and the word again.

**The fun bit:** the face zoom: the camera glides in, the face fills the screen, and a right tap gets "ow!", then a relieved "there".

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: seven big parts.
- L2: sixteen parts; sometimes two in one line ("my elbow and my hand").
- L3: left or right (the doctor first lifts the patient's left hand, then the right, once), or only the reason: "I've got a cough" means the chest.
- L4: the small parts: finger, thumb, toe, cheek, chin.

**Words it teaches:** knee, hand, head, ear, tummy, chest, left, right, cough, it hurts.

### The scrape

**The pictures:** `clinic-every-part-2` (two scrapes and the cold-water splash)

**What you're doing:** the patient fell over and scraped an arm or a knee; you clean it and put a plaster on.

**What you tap:**
1. The water bowl, then the scrape.
2. The cream, then dab the scrape as many times as the doctor says.
3. The plaster of the colour said, then the scrape.

**What you hear and what decides it:** what comes first and what comes next, how many dabs, which colour plaster. Later, which scrape first, by its body word.

**When it's done:** the dabs tick at the right number, and another dab doesn't land. The plaster smooths on, and the patient waves the mended arm proudly.

**The fun bit:** the cold-water gasp and shiver, and the zip of the plaster's backing peeling off.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: one scrape, one or two dabs counted aloud, one plaster.
- L2: two scrapes, up to three dabs, "the blue one".
- L3: "clean the one on the elbow first, then the hand"; the scrapes look alike.
- L4: three scrapes, two-colour plasters, finger beside thumb.

**Words it teaches:** clean, cream, plaster, first, then, elbow, arm, knee, blue, red.

### The hand splinter

**The pictures:** `clinic-every-part-5` (splinters on winding paths)

**What you're doing:** the patient was picking up sticks and has splinters in their hand; you soak it and pull them out.

**What you tap:**
1. The jug of hot or cold water, then pour it, as many jugs as said.
2. Drag each splinter out along its winding path with your finger.
3. A plaster on each spot.

**What you hear and what decides it:** hot or cold, how many jugs, and later which finger first.

**When it's done:** each splinter pops out with a tiny "plink" into the tray. Touch the edge of the path and it slides back with an "ow" and "careful!". At the end the patient wiggles their fingers.

**The fun bit:** the different winding paths, and "ow … careful … phew".

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: one straight splinter.
- L2: splinters in named fingers, in order.
- L3: big or small too: "first the big one, in the thumb".
- L4: narrower paths and a fourth splinter.

**Words it teaches:** hot, cold, water, careful, thumb, middle finger, little finger, big, small.

### The blocked nose

**The pictures:** `clinic-every-part-3` (the tissue arrives)

**What you're doing:** the patient has a cold and a blocked nose: drops first, then a tissue.

**What you tap:**
1. The dropper, then the nose, once per drop.
2. The tissue box, then the nose.
3. The tissue, then the bin.

**What you hear and what decides it:** the order (drops first, then the tissue), how many drops, and later which side.

**When it's done:** the drops tick at the right number, and another drop doesn't land. The breathing clears little by little, and the sneeze comes when the tissue arrives. Nothing ever goes inside the nose.

**The fun bit:** the tickle, the build-up, then a big honking sneeze into the tissue and a giggle.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: one or two drops, counted aloud.
- L2: two or three drops; the cream sits on the tray as a trick.
- L3: "two drops on the left side".
- L4: both sides, with different counts.

**Words it teaches:** nose, drops, tissue, first, then, left, right, cold.

### The drinks

**The pictures:** `clinic-every-part-4` (the counter with six ingredients)

**What you're doing:** one patient ate too much at the party; another has a cough. You make the family's drink that helps.

**What you tap:**
1. The stethoscope, then the tummy or chest: you hear a grumble or a cough.
2. Each ingredient, then the cup; then stir.
3. The cup, then the patient.

**What you hear and what decides it:** which things go in ("ginger and water"), later how many spoons, then what it's for ("the drink for his cough").

**When it's done:** the patient sips, and the rumble or the cough quietens.

**The fun bit:** a tiny polite burp after the ginger, then a giggle.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: one drink, named by its two things.
- L2: counts: "two spoons of honey".
- L3: named by what it's for (tummy, cough, throat); two drinks in order.
- L4: halves ("half a cup of milk"); lemon beside a lime.

**Words it teaches:** ginger, honey, milk, turmeric, lemon, water, spoon, cough, tummy, throat.

## 3. A day at home (Nani, Simba and Zazu)

All three games happen in Nani's house, drawn as a two-storey doll's house: the kitchen and sitting room downstairs, the bathroom, Nani's bedroom and Ali's room upstairs.

### Hide and seek

**The pictures:** `hide-and-seek-1` (the first find), `hide-and-seek-2` (a funny empty spot), `hide-and-seek-2b` (a look at Nana only, not a game moment), `hide-and-seek-3` (the big cat in the small box, the hardest level), `hide-and-seek-4` (bedtime with the torch, a later visit)

**What you're doing:** Simba and Zazu are hiding in Nani's bedroom, and Nani tells you where to look.

**What you tap:**
1. Nani's hands, to count to five while the cats hide.
2. The place Nani names: it opens.
3. At the end, the places Nani asks you to close.

**What you hear and what decides it:** "Where's Simba? Look in the wardrobe." Every place looks closed and alike, and no tail peeks out, so only the place word picks.

**When it's done:** the cat leaps out, runs to Nani, and its row ticks. At the end both cats curl up purring in Nani's lap.

**The fun bit:** the reveal: a beat, two green eyes blink, then a "mrrp" and a leap. An empty spot has its own joke: a sock on a spring, a pigeon on the sill, towels slumping out.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: five places, one cat at a time; the place glows as Nani says it.
- L2: seven places; both cats hide, so who and where. A cat moves after two empty opens.
- L3: other ways of saying it ("I think he's under the bed"); you ask Nana "Where's Zazu?"
- L4: big and small boxes side by side; the size word decides.

**Words it teaches:** wardrobe, cupboard, under the bed, behind the curtain, box, where, look, close, big, small.

### Pick it up and put it back

**The pictures:** `pick-it-up-put-it-back-1` (the doll's house and two pairs of glasses), `pick-it-up-put-it-back-2` (put it away), `pick-it-up-put-it-back-3` (Simba's paw, later), `pick-it-up-put-it-back-4` (the spice box, later)

**What you're doing:** Nana can't find his glasses or his cup. Nani says where they are, and you fetch them through the house. Then you put back what Simba knocked over.

**What you tap:**
1. A room in the doll's house: the camera goes in.
2. The right one of two identical copies: it flies through the house to Nana.
3. Later, a thing on the floor, then one of the dotted rings (on or under).

**What you hear and what decides it:** "Nana's glasses are under the table." There's a pair on the table and an identical pair under it, so only on or under decides. Later the room is heard too.

**When it's done:** Nana puts his glasses on and reads; the ball rolls under the table and Zazu curls up beside it. A wrong copy shakes, and Zazu's nose pops out of the wrong cup.

**The fun bit:** Nana's eyes going huge behind his glasses.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: the room is lit for you; on or under one table.
- L2: the room is heard too; three rooms, two pieces of furniture.
- L3: Ma and Ali ask too; "this side" or "that side"; Simba knocks things off and you fix them by ear; you tell Ali where.
- L4: big and small copies; "my" or "your".

**Words it teaches:** on, under, table, chair, bed, glasses, cup, ball, bag, kitchen, bedroom.

### The clean-up

**The pictures:** `clean-up-day-1` (the bathroom, the tap just off), `clean-up-day-2` (the bathroom at dusk), `clean-up-day-3` (Ali's room, a later visit: push the toy car), `clean-up-day-4` (Nani's bedroom at night, telling Ali, a later visit)

**What you're doing:** the camera zooms into the bathroom, and Nani asks you to tidy it: switch off the light, turn off the tap, close the curtains.

**What you tap:**
1. The thing Nani names: it flips (on to off, open to closed).
2. To clean: the broom or the cloth, then the dirty spot.
3. To put away: the soap, then its home.

**What you hear and what decides it:** at first, the thing's name. From L2 there are two of each, one tap running and one off, so the doing word (on or off, open or close) decides.

**When it's done:** each thing reacts like a toy and its row ticks. A wrong thing does its joke, then flips back by itself. At the end Nani switches the light back on and Simba curls up on the clean bath mat.

**The fun bit:** the dusk: with the light off and the curtain closed, the room goes soft blue, Zazu's eyes shine in the laundry basket, and he pops up with a sleepy "mew".

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: one of each thing; off, close and sweep only.
- L2: pairs in opposite states, so on or off decides; the soap.
- L3: the reason first ("it's dark in here, switch on the light"); Simba undoes two things; you tell Ali.
- L4: "the big tap", "all the lights", "first close the curtains, then switch on the light".

**Words it teaches:** switch on, switch off, turn off, tap, light, open, close, curtains, sweep, wipe.

## 4. Someone's birthday (Zara's family)

### Pass the balloon

**The pictures:** `party-guests-1` (three children, the balloon floating), `party-guests-2` (whose mum, whose aunt)

**What you're doing:** at Zara's party the children pass a balloon, and whoever holds it calls who gets it next.

**What you tap:**
1. Listen to the call.
2. Tap that person: the balloon floats over to them.

**What you hear and what decides it:** "Give the balloon to Zara's masi!" (her mum's sister). From L2 only the two sisters are called and they swap seats, so only the family word, mum or aunt, picks.

**When it's done:** a child catches it with a bop on the head; a grown-up bats it back to a child, who calls next. At the end Zara asks your name, then ties the balloon to your wrist.

**The fun bit:** the slow balloon bopping heads. A wrong tap gets it puffed back with a cheeky blow, batted like volleyball, or ducked.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: the children say their names, then three calls by name.
- L2: Zara's mum or aunt, Yusuf's mum or aunt; seats shuffle.
- L3: the holder says "give it to my mum!"; you introduce the sisters to Nani.
- L4: Zara's grandparents beside your own Nana and Nani.

**Words it teaches:** my name is, mum, aunt, my, your, grandfather, grandmother, hello.

### How are you?

**The pictures:** `party-guests-3` (the guests and the tea trolley), `party-guests-4` (a year older, the second visit)

**What you're doing:** the guests have been playing. Zara's mum asks you to go round, ask how each one is, and help.

**What you tap:**
1. A guest, to ask "how are you?".
2. Listen to their answer.
3. A thing on the tea trolley, then the guest.

**What you hear and what decides it:** "I'm so thirsty!" From L2 the face shows nothing until you try, and all six fixes (food, water, a chair, a fan, a shawl, a balloon) are always there, so the feeling word picks.

**When it's done:** the guest says thank you and their row ticks. A wrong fix gets a funny reaction and goes back; the face then shows the feeling, so you can fix it straight away.

**The fun bit:** every fix: gulping the water in one go, flopping into the chair and nodding off, fanning in bliss. Wrong ones too: a fan for a cold guest makes their teeth chatter.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: two guests; hungry, thirsty, tired or sad; the faces show.
- L2: all six feelings, and one guest who's fine ("no, thank you").
- L3: natural ways of saying it: "my tummy's rumbling", "I'm shivering"; which food.
- L4: two needs at once ("hot and thirsty"); "two samosas"; water beside milk.

**Words it teaches:** how are you, I'm fine, hungry, thirsty, tired, sad, hot, cold, thank you.

### Musical statues

**The pictures:** `do-it-engine-1` (the first stop), `do-it-engine-2` ("touch your nose"), `do-it-engine-3` ("now you!")

**What you're doing:** Zara and Yusuf dance while Nani claps; when she stops, she calls a pose.

**What you tap:**
1. While they dance, tap a dancer for a wiggle (just for fun).
2. When the clapping stops, tap the pose card for what Nani said.
3. When she says "dance!", tap the dance card.

**What you hear and what decides it:** "Sit!" The cards are pictures only and shuffle at every stop, so only the heard pose picks.

**When it's done:** both dancers snap into the pose with a click and hold it, then the music bursts back. About six stops make a round. Zara puts a paper crown on you. Then Nani turns to the real child, "now you!", and they dance and freeze in the room.

**The fun bit:** the freeze-click and Yusuf's wobble and giggle. Wrong cards are funny: jump instead of sit lands them in a giggling heap.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: sit and jump, then turn.
- L2: stand, stop, dance, and "jump, don't sit!".
- L3: touch your nose, head or ear; you call a pose as Nani.
- L4: stand against stop; two calls in one ("jump, then sit").

**Words it teaches:** sit, jump, turn round, stand, stop, dance, touch, nose, head, ear.

### The cake

**The pictures:** `party-gifts-1` (the candles), `party-gifts-2` (the cloth and the cake)

**What you're doing:** it's Zara's cake: you blow out the candles, lay the cloth and give everyone their slice.

**What you tap:**
1. Candles: the bundle Nani asks for, then the cake to blow.
2. The cloth (from L2): a cup, then the place in front of its owner.
3. The cake: a slice, then the person.

**What you hear and what decides it:** the number picks the bundle (no digits anywhere). Who it's for picks the person: "this is for Nana". On the cloth, whose cup it is.

**When it's done:** the flames go out one by one as Nani counts down. Each person takes a bite, "mmm", and the row ticks. A wrong person makes a "for me? no, no!" face and hands it back.

**The fun bit:** the blow-out; the baby with cream on its nose; Zara's grandfather with crumbs on his moustache.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: one to three candles; one slice for Nana, Zara or the baby.
- L2: counts to six; the cloth; "two pieces for Zara", "not the baby".
- L3: "how many?"; cups that differ by colour; "where's my cup?" after the baby swaps two.
- L4: big and small candles; two look-alike grandfathers; "his cup".

**Words it teaches:** candles, one to six, cake, for Nana, cup, my, your, big, small.

### Snap the guests (comes later)

**The pictures:** `party-gifts-4` (two older men, one in the viewfinder)

**What you're doing:** the guests mingle in the garden, and Nani asks you to photograph one of them.

**What you tap:**
1. Move the viewfinder to frame the person.
2. Tap to shoot.

**What you hear and what decides it:** the family word ("Zara's grandfather"), later two words ("grandmother with the baby", "the aunt in red"). There are always two of a kind, two older men or two women, so only the word picks.

**When it's done:** the instant camera prints, and a right shot ticks straight away. A guest who leans into the frame makes a funny print with no tick, and Nani asks again.

**The fun bit:** the guest waving in the back of the shot; the grandfather blinking ("again!"); the baby grabbing at the lens.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: one person, with one clearly different guest nearby.
- L2: two people in one frame; "not Zara".
- L3: whose: "Zara's grandfather".
- L4: "the aunt in red", "the small boy", "two women".

**Words it teaches:** grandfather, grandmother, aunt, baby, with, red, small, photo.

### The party presents

**The pictures:** `party-gifts-3` (the unwrap and the give)

**What you're doing:** Zara's family has a thank-you present for everyone, in plain brown boxes, and you help Nani hand them out.

**What you tap:**
1. A box: it bursts open and the toy jumps out.
2. The toy, then the person Nani names.

**What you hear and what decides it:** who it's for: "the drum for Zara's grandfather". Any toy can go to anyone, so the toy never tells you.

**When it's done:** the person takes it and plays, and the row ticks. A wrong person tries it in a silly way and hands it back with a grin. At the end Nani holds up your own toy, asks "whose is it?", and you say "mine!" (or tap a picture).

**The fun bit:** each unwrap has its sound (the drum booms, the teddy squeaks), and a grandfather drumming proudly.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: one box, one toy, for Nana, Zara or the baby.
- L2: two boxes, five toys, three asked; "not the aunt".
- L3: Zara's own presents: "it's yours", "it's Zara's", "it's the baby's".
- L4: look-alike grown-ups; "two balloons for grandmother".

**Words it teaches:** for, drum, ball, teddy, mine, yours, Zara's, the baby's.

### Who ate the cake? (second visit)

**The pictures:** `party-gifts-5` (the line-up after two clues)

**What you're doing:** while everyone claps, a slice vanishes from the plate. Nani asks who did it and gives clues.

**What you tap:**
1. Listen to a clue.
2. Tap everyone it fits: they stand, and the rest sit down with a huff.
3. Next clue, until one is left; tap them.

**What you hear and what decides it:** the clues: "someone small", "in red", "the one with the big cup". Nobody has crumbs until the end, so the clues are heard, not seen.

**When it's done:** the one who did it grins, and cream appears on their chin. The host cuts another slice and they share it. Nobody is called a thief or shamed.

**The fun bit:** the huffs, and the reveal: often a grown-up did it.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: "a girl": one of three.
- L2: "someone small", then "in red".
- L3: "someone big", "not in red", "not the aunt".
- L4: six people, three clues, one from the cups on the cloth.

**Words it teaches:** who, small, big, red, not, girl, boy, cup.

## 5. School day (Nani, then the teacher)

### The wake-up clock

**The pictures:** `wake-up-clock-1` (the first wake), `wake-up-clock-2` (times only heard), `wake-up-clock-3` (seven at night), `wake-up-clock-4` (everyone up, chai served)

**What you're doing:** tomorrow is school. At bedtime Nani tells you when Ali, Nana and Ma each get up, and you're the alarm clock.

**What you tap:**
1. Listen at bedtime: "Ali wakes up at six."
2. The wall clock runs fast through the night and pauses at each hour.
3. At each pause, tap the sleeper whose time it is, or nobody.

**What you hear and what decides it:** the hour. From L2 the bedside alarms are frosted and the hour isn't said at the pause, so the heard number against the wall clock decides.

**When it's done:** a right tap: the alarm rattles and they bolt upright. A wrong one: a sleepy head shake and a roll-over. Late is fine: after their hour, a tap wakes them happily. At sunrise the curtains open and Nani brings chai and milk.

**The fun bit:** the wakes: Ali's pillow flies, Nana puts his glasses on upside down, and Simba, on a different bed every night, gets launched.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: three hours between five and nine; the digits show and Nani says each hour.
- L2: the times are only heard; any hour to twelve.
- L3: morning or night: "seven in the morning".
- L4: before and after: "Ma wakes up after Ali".

**Words it teaches:** wake up, one to twelve, o'clock, morning, night, before, after, goodnight, get up.

### Getting ready

**The pictures:** `do-it-engine-4` ("comb your hair"), `do-it-engine-5` (the sock-puppet miss)

**What you're doing:** Ali and two little cousins slept over at Nani's, and it's a school morning; you help get them ready.

**What you tap:**
1. Listen to Nani's call.
2. Tap the thing: the toothbrushes, the comb, a bowl on Nani's tray.
3. The child she named does it.

**What you hear and what decides it:** "Ali, comb your hair." Nani calls the jobs in any order and to any child, so only the words say which.

**When it's done:** the child does the job and the row ticks. At the end a mirror slides in; they laugh at themselves, combed but still in pyjamas, and a twirl puts them in uniform. The bus horn beeps.

**The fun bit:** the bed-hair springing flat with a "boing"; the foam moustache. Wrong picks are jokes: a sock on a hand as a talking puppet, shoes on the wrong feet. Food is never played with.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: brush your teeth, comb your hair, eat your breakfast.
- L2: wash your face or your hands; "not your face!".
- L3: socks or shoes; other ways of saying it.
- L4: "first … then …"; get dressed.

**Words it teaches:** brush, teeth, comb, hair, eat, breakfast, wash, face, hands, socks, shoes.

### Pack the school bag

### The three stalls

**The pictures:** `beach-boat-3` (choosing a stall)

**What you're doing:** time for picnic food: a corn cart, a cassava chips fryer and a coconut cart, and Masi says what she wants.

**What you tap:**
1. The stall that sells it; it zooms in.
2. Make it: corn (pick the bundle, turn it, rub on the topping, tap the paper sleeve), chips (fry, lift the basket, pick the cone, shake the salt) or coconut (pick it, push in the straws).
3. Pay: the stack of coins, then the stallholder's hand.

**What you hear and what decides it:** the food picks the stall; then how many, the size, "with lemon" or "without chilli".

**When it's done:** Nani slurps the coconut, "aah"; the stall greys out and the food drops into the basket. At the third stall Masi hands you her purse and you order it yourself.

**The fun bit:** the corn kernels popping black; the thwack of the coconut's top coming off, and Nani's long slurp.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: one item each; the cone size; prices one to three.
- L2: counts; "without chilli"; a queue orders too; prices to five.
- L3: "one for Nani with chilli, one for Masi without".
- L4: a middle-sized cone and coconut beside the asked one.

**Words it teaches:** corn, chips, coconut, with, without, lemon, chilli, salt, big, small, I want.

### The picnic

**The pictures:** `beach-boat-4` (the crab stealing the last chip)

**What you're doing:** the cloth is spread on the sand, and you set out the food you bought.

**What you tap:** a thing from the basket, then the place it goes. Every place has the same faint outline.

**What you hear and what decides it:** whose it is ("this is for Ali"), later how many and where ("in the middle", "next to her plate").

**When it's done:** each thing hops into place and its row ticks; a wrong place slides back. Then everyone eats, Ali's lemon drink goes in his box to take home, and Nani says "aah".

**The fun bit:** the crab climbs out of the basket, snips the last chip and scuttles off sideways, and Masi laughs.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: one thing at a time, for Nani, Masi or Ali's box.
- L2: counts: "two cups for Masi".
- L3: where: in the middle, next to, on.
- L4: pour the drink: a little, half or full.

**Words it teaches:** for Ali, Nani's, cup, plate, two, in the middle, next to, on.

### The ride on the water (boat day)

**The pictures:** none in this run.

**What you're doing:** Masi's friend takes everyone out on his boat, and Nani names things as they pass.

**What you tap:** the thing Nani names as it floats past.

**What you hear and what decides it:** the thing's name (a bird, a boat, a buoy); later its size or how many.

**When it's done:** each find ticks. Past the first level, Nani calls "faster!" or "slow down!" to the boatman, then it's your turn to say it, and the boat rushes or eases.

**The fun bit:** the first time the boat goes fast, Nani's big sun hat blows off into the sea, which is why you go fishing next.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: one named thing; one steady speed.
- L2: "two birds", or a size; the speed calls start.
- L3: two things in one line.
- L4: a big boat and a small boat.

**Words it teaches:** boat, bird, fish, ship, big, small, two, faster, slow down.

### Litter fishing (boat day)

**The pictures:** `beach-boat-5` (the shoe with a crab in it)

**What you're doing:** the boat stops in a calm bay. The sea has rubbish in it, and Nani's hat is down there too; you fish it all out.

**What you tap:**
1. The thing Masi names: the hook drops onto it.
2. The reel, three or four times, to wind it up.

**What you hear and what decides it:** "The shoe!" Which thing; later its size or colour, or how many.

**When it's done:** it rises in bubbles, swings into the basket and the row ticks. A wrong tap: the hook boinks off and comes back empty. The last catch is always Nani's hat, which she puts on, dripping. Then the boatman nets the rest and the fish come back.

**The fun bit:** the shoe spits out a tiny crab; the kettle pours a fish back into the sea.

**How it gets harder:**
- L1: one of each thing.
- L2: two of each: "the red bottle", "the big shoe".
- L3: "two cans"; "the bottle, not the red one".
- L4: Nani's hat beside a girl's small one.

**Words it teaches:** shoe, bottle, can, bag, sock, ball, kettle, hat, red, big, two.
