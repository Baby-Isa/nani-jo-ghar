Independent critic, flaws first. ## Findings, most serious first

### 1. MUST: four of the six contrast pairs leak by pattern, or force Nani to ask for things nobody wants
- **Place:** §3C the pairs table (hot/cold, full/empty, old/new, ripe/not ripe); the leak test "Patterns" ("which state is asked is drawn fresh each ask with no alternation"); §4 step 10; §9 row 10 ("things named as they are").
- **Problem:** the X one is leak-proof only if both states of a pair get asked for. Nobody buys a cold samosa, an empty ghee tin or an old rolling pin, and the design's own tone rule is "one natural sentence per person". So either Nani always asks for the hot, full and new one, and a child with no Kutchi learns "pick the steaming one, the heavy one, the shiny one" (a picture-and-pattern win: the state is visible or felt before the word is needed), or Nani asks *Muke thundo samosa khape* and the game teaches an unnatural line. The design says both ("drawn fresh ... no alternation" and "named as they are") without noticing they conflict. Hot/cold has a second fault: steam is drawn on the plate before any tap, so it isn't a "test by touch" pair at all, and §3C step 4 ("tap a thing, it lifts") sits oddly beside "the child never touches anything hot".
- **Evidence:** research-mechanics finding 6 (adjectives are learned as "this one, not that one"; contrast must go both ways); research-other-games lesson 2 ("show an open need the picture can't settle"); the master map's chai row (the "gone cold" leak needed two glasses without steam); lessons §1 (OTH lessons 2, 6) and §10 (name things as they are).
- **Change:** keep only pairs where a real shopper wants either state, and give each state its reason in Nani's line: big/small (✓); soft/hard mangoes (soft to eat, hard for the pickle: the family's own cooking); green/yellow bananas (green for cooking, as East African kitchens do, yellow to eat); long/short and thick/thin at the cloth; plain/dotted. Move hot/cold to water, where both are wanted (*garam paani* for Nana's chai, *thundo paani* for the walk home) and the glasses look the same until lifted (steam or drops on the glass), or hand hot/cold back to Cook's chai where it already lives. Drop full/empty and old/new from the X one; "the empty tin" belongs in Cook's pantry as a put-it-back ask, where "empty" is natural. Rewrite the "Patterns" line to say both states are asked in every pair, and add a bot strategy "always the good one".

### 2. MUST: the first visit's deciding words are not recorded by the design's own rule, and the count hides it
- **Place:** §1 "Counted: about 32 deciding words ... 19 are recorded (✓) or heard"; the pairs table ("*wadho* heard, *nindho* ✓: visit 1"); F3 and F4; §3C leak test "a pair runs outside the lab only once both its words are recorded (visit 1's two pairs already are)"; §9 row 1.
- **Problem:** the key defines "heard" as "no clip of its own yet", then counts heard words as recorded. *Wadho* (big) is heard inside 30 lines and has no clip, so the engine cannot build *Muke wadho tapelo khape*, and visit 1's first pair fails the design's own gate. The same goes for *ambo* (serve), *chai*, *bataato*, *oho* and every number: two and five to ten are handout drafts, 11-100 don't exist. *Khanigin* (F4, said by both sellers) and *ghan* are engine drafts never confirmed by Mum. So on visit 1, counted honestly, serve runs on lemon, onion, tomato, peas, chilli and "one"; the X one runs on *nindho* alone; pay runs on nothing. Non-negotiable 2 says a line the engine can't say is a gap to report, not a ✓.
- **Evidence:** `lexicon.json`: `a.big` confirmed but no clip cited; `num.10` "handout, never confirmed by mum"; `v.take-it-yourself` and `phrase.here-you-are` draft; the audit flaw 4 (no number has a clip); the master map rule 3.
- **Change:** (a) recount §1 with ✓ only and say plainly which parts can run on visit 1 today; (b) use the whole recorded lines as the L1 asks instead of the engine frame: *wadho ginech* ✓ and *nindho ginech* ✓ ("buy the big one / the small one") are complete, natural and already in Mum's voice, so big/small is playable now; (c) put at the top of Mum's next sheet, in this order: *wadho* alone, one to ten as clips, *paisa* and the price line, *khanigin*, *ghan*, the join for a second row; (d) say in §9 row 1 that pay is lab-only in full, not "with one exception".

### 3. MUST: the first-ever round is five mechanics at three stalls, with a speaking turn after four watches
- **Place:** §4 (15 screens: greeting, help, crates, two customers, the gift, the walk, two picks, your turn, pay, the cloth, pay, the goat, the end); §3D ladder L1 ("rung 1"); decision 23 ("trimmed"); §1 "New words per level" (L1: 4-5).
- **Problem:** the day already has the door, the bag, the ride and the town map before this. Then the first shops round asks a 3-year-old to learn serve (tap fruit, tap bag), the gift, the lift-and-give, speaking, pay (tap coins, tap hand) and the cloth, each with its own showing, and to meet lemon, onion, one, two, big, small, hot, cold, egg, three, red, *khanigin*, *shabash*, *barabar*, *kitla* and the salaam in one go: about 14 new words, not 4-5. The lessons say the first session is tiny and "lose them by confusion, not by boredom". Speaking after watching two customers and two of Nani's asks is not "many times"; Find it's own quality pass put Ask for one at L2+, and `speaking.md` makes a frame speakable only after the child has acted on it as the listener "several times". The day doc itself says a first shops day takes two stalls and pay, "and later visits add the rest".
- **Evidence:** lessons §2 (K1001 §8D), §10 (3-5 new words a round); `speaking.md` §11 and the tracker rule; `find-it.md` Q3 3b "Keep (L2+)"; `days/shops.md` "The list is long by design".
- **Change:** the first-ever round is the fruit stall and one pay: salaam, help, two customers, the gift, then Nani buys one thing at the same stall and pays 1-3 coins (the pay engine is seen once, small, where the child has just watched customers pay the seller). Round 2 opens the general store with big/small. Your turn moves to L2, as Find it's 3b. The cloth stall opens on round 3. Rewrite §4 and decision 23 to match, and make the "new words per level" table true of the first round.

### 4. MUST: over-paying and over-serving reveal the number, so a dump-all fish completes every count step
- **Place:** §3 shared rule 2 ("extras are handed back with the number said again ... the row is redone on the spot"); §3A step 5; §3E step 6 ("Too many: he slides the extras back, saying the price again. The child adds or takes away on the spot"); the bot lines ("count 1 in 2", "1 in 5").
- **Problem:** rule 2 and step 6 contradict each other, and step 6 is the one written out: the seller slides back the extras, leaving exactly the price on the counter, and the child gives. In serve the same: tip six lemons on the pan, the seller hands four back, the bag holds two. The row doesn't tick (rule 1 holds), but the step completes with no Kutchi at all, the child learns "put everything out and he fixes it", and the bot numbers for the count are wrong: with dump-all the count is 1 in 1, so serve L1 is 1 in 2 per order, not 1 in 4. "Too few" has the same shape in slow motion: add one, give, add one, give, until *barabar*.
- **Evidence:** lessons §1 (ticks mustn't let a child fish; the bot plays a learned save); the farm design's rule as written ("that row redoes on the spot"); research-mechanics finding 1 (the word must be the move, not a toll).
- **Change:** at a wrong give, everything comes back (the pan tips back into the crate, the coins slide back into the purse) with the number said again, and the row starts over; the "and ...?" patient hand stays for too few, but after the second wrong give the helper card opens (costing the bulb) rather than a third try. Add "dump all" and "add one, give" to the bot's strategies, and redo the numbers. Reconcile rule 2 and §3E step 6 so there is one wording, coded once in the shared count (decision 9's own argument).

### 10. SHOULD: the first-ever showing lights the bag at the right count, then never again

## Verdict

The bones are right and better than the day doc they replace: serve from behind the counter before ordering, one describing-word game where the adjective is the whole move, pay as "give me N" with no digits, the give that closes a count, every miss a joke. The research is used, not quoted, and the Sceptic has been thought about at every stall. But it is not ready for Zafar as a yes-to-all list. Four of its six pairs leak by pattern or make Nani ask for cold samosas; the first visit's deciding words are counted as recorded when the engine can't say them; the first round is five mechanics and a speaking turn where the lessons ask for one tiny thing; a dump-all fish completes every count; the L4 counters don't fit a phone; it is the largest art and recording ask on file while calling itself medium; and Zafar's own spine for the day (the lanes and the chase) is set aside as an assumed yes. Fix the seven MUSTs (the pairs, the word count with *wadho ginech* ✓ as the L1 ask, a one-stall first round, everything-back on a wrong give, a two-scene visit 1, the lanes as his question, the phone pixel budget with two-row coins) and this becomes the design that finally gives the numbers above ten and the describing words a home.
