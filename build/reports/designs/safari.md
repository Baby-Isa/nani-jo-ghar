## 1. The language goal

**Word groups:** wild animals (main, both); verbs seen in motion (main, A: Zafar's four, eat, run, swim, climb); animal body parts and describing words (main, B: neck, ears, tail; stripy, spotty, big); sizes (big, small, baby: both at L4).

**What each game teaches that the other doesn't:**
- **A** teaches the animal names, and verbs **seen** in motion ("the elephant that's swimming", "who's climbing?"). It is listening only.
- **B** teaches what an animal **looks like**: its parts and the words that describe it. Only a part shows, so the heard part or describing word decides. No other game on the farm or the safari teaches either group, and describing words are the biggest short group on the master map. From L3 the child names the animal from the part, which is the day's speaking.

"Heard" means Mum says it only inside longer recorded lines. I never invent Kutchi, and I don't assume a known word is the one Kutchi uses for a new meaning.

## 3. The games

### A. Snap from the jeep

**Why (said at the start):** Hannah Mami: "Look! Take photos for Ali." She is in the driver's seat, turned three-quarters to the child.

**Steps (the farm's Snap gesture, unchanged):** the jeep drives (the window ride, side view). A group of animals comes into view in one of the near loop's scenes, and Hannah Mami names one. Its card pops up big, then flies into its row. The child **taps the animal**: the camera frame (soft white corners) glides to it and follows it, and the jeep eases to its slowest. Once the frame has locked on (about 0.4 s), its corners go gold. The child **taps inside the gold frame**: click, flash, the animal reacts, and the **instant camera prints**: the print slides into the card's row, and **a right shot ticks at once** (Zafar, 1 Nov: a tick with each correct shot, straight away). No darkroom. Tapping a different animal before the shot moves the frame (that's the undo). **There is no shutter button: the cog stays the only button on the play screen** (lessons §6; the farm's Snap, stage 7).

**The verb scenes on the near loop.** The ride's near loop (the layer at speed 1, where the animals stand) is built as three scenes in a row, so every verb has its prop on the same layer as the animal doing it:
- **The waterhole:** a grassy bank with a bush (eat), a deep middle (swim), and the open bank (run).
- **The tree:** a climbable acacia standing on the near line (climb: **the lion in the tree**, Zafar 1 Nov; it climbs up and lies along a low branch, legs dangling), its low leaves (eat, for a giraffe), and open grass beside it (run).
- **The open grass:** grazing (eat) and room to run.

Each group is placed in one scene, and only asks a verb whose prop is in that scene. The middle loop's acacia stays scenery.

**What "run" means on a moving road:** a short dash that stays inside the group's width. The runner dashes ahead about one body length, stops, turns, and trots back, and does it again. It never leaves its group's span, so the group still passes together and nothing leaves before it's been asked (the ride's fairness rule).

**Level ladder** (photos per round 3, 3, 5, 7: the rulebook's formula with a top of 7, which gives 1, 3, 5, 7, and L1 overridden to 3, the game's one override, because a ride needs a few animals. The top of 7 is set by the bot: §3 A, "The bot"):

| **L2** | **Three of a kind doing different things** (Zafar's "two of a kind", made three so a learned save can't win) | "Take a photo of the elephant that's swimming." | Three elephants or three zebras at the waterhole: one eating, one swimming, one running (zebras really swim across rivers). The verbs are eat, swim and run. Hannah Mami's speed calls come back between asks |
| **L3** | **Other ways of saying it:** the verb alone | "Who's climbing?" | Three kinds in one scene, **one member per verb**: at the tree, the lion climbing, a giraffe eating leaves, a zebra running. Adds climb. Only the verb picks |
| **L4** | **The look-alike:** baby and grown-up, same kind | "Take a photo of the baby giraffe that's eating." | A big giraffe eating, a baby giraffe eating, a baby giraffe running (or the same with elephants at the waterhole): three words decide, and any two of them leave two candidates |

**The one-answer rule (coded, not just written; lessons §9):** every ask has exactly one right animal in its group. At L2 the three members do three different verbs. At L3 no two members of a group share a verb. At L4 exactly one member fits size, kind and verb together. The data check refuses a group that breaks it.

**Pacing (lessons §4):**
- Nothing leaves before it's been asked. An ask comes as its group enters from the right, never while it's leaving.
- The jeep runs at its slowest while an ask is live: about 50% slower than today's ride at L1, and about 15% quicker per level, set in data.
- **The ride's speed calls stay** (Zafar, 20 Oct: "that's the fun of it"), **only at later levels** (Zafar, 1 Nov: the start stays simple). From L2, between asks, Hannah Mami calls "faster!" or "slow down!" as Nani does on the ride, and everything really rushes past or slows. When a group that will be asked comes in, she calls "slow down!", so the ask-driven slowdown is her own call, not a hidden rule. At L1 there are no speed calls: one slow speed.
- A group that reaches the left edge unshot comes round again on the next loop.
- No timer.
- **The monkeys (Zafar, 1 Nov: monkeys too).** Once a round, between asks, a small troop of three monkeys bounds out of the acacia, hops onto the jeep's bonnet, and one peers in through the windscreen and pulls a face before they all scamper off. Hannah Mami laughs and names them ("monkeys!"). It's the ride's funny beat: unscored, never in an ask, and the frame never goes to them on visit 1. From visit 2 the monkey is an askable kind (eat, climb, run), in a second tree, so "who's climbing?" never has two answers in one scene.

**Leak test:**

| Way to cheat | Why it fails, and the fix |
|---|---|
| **Reading** | No text in the play area. The card shows the animal's picture only while the noun is supported, and **never shows the verb as a picture** past support. A verb card is a speaker only |
| **Matching** | From L2 all three elephants match the elephant picture; only the verb picks. At L3 there's no noun to match. At L4 there are two babies and two eaters |
| **Eliminating** | A first wrong shot leaves fewer candidates, so the elimination is free. But **only the first shot ticks gold and counts for level-up and the bot's win** (lessons §1: tick only the right item, first go). The re-asked row closes plain |
| **Patterns** | Which member does which verb, the order within a group, the scene, the order of groups and whether a group is asked at all are all random per round. Unasked groups pass too (the ride's rule). The same verb can be asked twice in a row |
| **Waiting** | Nothing shoots itself and nothing glows on the answer. The frame goes gold on any animal, so gold isn't the answer. The hint (7, 9, 11 or 13 s after the last action) repeats the ask. The helper card, which costs a bulb, shows the full answer |
| **Running off** | The runner's dash stays inside its group's width, so a runner is never alone on screen ahead of its group, and never out of view when its ask comes |
| **Spraying shots** | Every shot makes a print. A wrong one is a funny print with no tick; the re-asked row closes plain, and a round with a plain row never moves up |

**The bot (`leak_snap` plus `leak_window_ride`).** Winning means a round that counts towards moving up: every row ticked on the first go (Zafar, 1 Nov: level up only on right-first-time rounds). Every bot below must win under 10% of rounds from L2.

|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | 3 | random tap: 1 in 2 | 12.5%, within the level-1 allowance, as on the ride today |
| L2 | 3 | knows the nouns, not the verbs: 1 in 3 | 3.7% |
| L3 | 5 | knows the nouns (no use here): 1 in 3 | 0.4% (all 5) |
| L4 | 7 | knows the nouns and the verbs, not the sizes: 1 in 2 | 0.8% (all 7) |

(With every row needed, L4 could drop to 6 rows (1.6%); it stays at 7 for the round's length.)

**Mistakes (no verdicts):**
- The print shakes gently in the tray, and Hannah Mami says what's in it ("That's the elephant running!") as a recast. Then she asks again.
- The wrong animal still reacts, with a different funny reaction from the right one's (the zebra flicks its tail at the camera).

**The delight moment:** each animal reacts to the shot in its own way.
- The giraffe bends down and stares into the lens until its eye fills the frame.
- The elephant squirts water, and drops run down the screen.
- The zebra flicks its tail and trots a step.
- The lion, stretched along its branch, yawns hugely and rolls onto its back on the branch.

Behind them, a print slides out of the instant camera and fades up in two seconds.

**Speaking:** none in A. It's a listening ride; B carries the day's speaking.

### B. Who's hiding? (the second game, proposed)

**Why this game:**
- **The teaching gap.** A teaches the names and the verbs. B teaches what the animals look like: their parts (ears, neck, tail) and the words that describe them (stripy, spotty, big). The farm, the pet hotel and the rest of the safari teach neither, and describing words are the master map's biggest short group.
- **Not a repeat.** The first draft's B ("Playtime": tap the baby, tap the place, the place is the verb) was the farm's photo shoot again, on the same verbs, one trip later (critique 2). B uses a different deciding group and a different move.
- **Where it comes from.** The day doc packs binoculars, and the first draft's parked "Who's hiding?" scored best of the parked ideas.
- **What it borrows:** hide-and-seek's openable piece (a place that opens and shows what's in it) and its "every flap reacts" rule. Honest novelty: the hider partly shows, so the heard part or describing word decides, not the place.

**Why (said at the start):** Hannah Mami lifts the binoculars from the bag: "Shh! Who's hiding?" She stands at the right edge with the binoculars; the long grass fills the middle.

**Steps:**
- Clumps of tall grass stand in one band across the scene. Behind each one an animal hides, with one part showing: a neck above the grass, ears and a grey back, a striped tail.
- Hannah Mami looks through the binoculars and says who or what she sees: "The zebra is hiding. Where is it?" (*kida ai* ✓). The card pops up (a picture while supported, then a speaker) and flies into its row.
- The child **taps a clump**. The grass parts, and the animal pops up with a grin and its own little sound. On a right find Hannah Mami names it ("The zebra! *Shabash*"), and the row ticks.
- **After every find, everyone re-hides:** a rustle runs across the grass, the animals duck down and come up behind new clumps with a new part showing. No clump is ever used up.
- One tap per find. Nothing to undo: a wrong clump costs only the gold tick (below).

2. **The ask.** Hannah Mami: "Look, an elephant! Take a photo of the elephant." The elephant card pops up big beside the side panel, then flies into row 1.
3. **The light.** Everything is still (lessons §4: a light waits until everything is still). A gold box hugs the elephant; everything else is dimmed and can't be tapped. There's no finger.
4. **First tap.** The frame glides to the elephant, and its corners go gold. The box shrinks to the gold frame.
5. **Second tap, inside the frame.** Flash and click. The elephant turns and squirts the jeep, and drops run down the view. The print slides into row 1 and fades up. Hannah Mami: "That's the elephant eating! *Shabash*" ✓. The elephant has been heard three times.
6. **The ride starts** at the slowest speed, and stays there all round (L1 has no speed calls). The savannah layers drift. A giraffe and a zebra walk in from the right on the near verge, in the open-grass scene.
7. **Ask 2, no light.** The card glows once for 1.2 s ("look here"), and the child does it alone. The giraffe stares into the lens and fills the frame.
8. **Ask 3:** the zebra, with an elephant beside it. It flicks its tail and trots a step. The third print lands.
9. **The end.** The jeep stops by the long grass. The three prints fan out on the dashboard, and Hannah Mami says "for Ali?" The child taps one, and it tucks into the visor. Then the shared two-page end screen.

**B, first time** (straight after, the same stop):
1. **The scene.** The long grass, three clumps in a band: a giraffe's neck above one, an elephant's ears and grey back above another, a striped tail beside the third. Hannah Mami at the right edge lifts the binoculars: "Shh! Who's hiding?" Each clump rustles once in turn: the start shows what's live.
2. **The ask.** She looks through the binoculars: "The elephant is hiding. Where is it?" (*kida ai* ✓). The card pops up (the elephant's picture while supported) and flies into its row.
3. **The light.** A gold box around the elephant's clump; everything else is dimmed. The child taps it. The grass parts, the elephant pops up and sprays the grass. "*Ker ai*? The elephant! *Shabash*." Tick.
4. **The rustle.** Everyone ducks and re-hides: now the elephant is behind the left clump, the zebra in the middle.
5. **Ask 2, no light:** "The giraffe is hiding. Where is it?" Just the 1.2 s point at the card. The giraffe's head sways up and up. Tick.
6. **Ask 3:** the zebra. It pops up chewing. Tick.
7. **The end.** "Everybody out!" All three pop up together. The frame glides over the group and goes gold, lit in a box; the child taps inside it, and the group photo flies to the book's corner. Then the shared end screen.

Both rounds together take about four minutes.

## 8. What it borrows

- **Pokémon Snap** (Nintendo, 1999; the New one, 2021): the on-rails photo ride, and the joy of catching an animal *doing* something. We take that, not the scoring.
- **Ranger Rick Jr. photo safari, Wild Earth: African Safari:** photo missions on safari. Our missions are heard, never read.
- **Tap-to-animate toddler apps** (Safari Train for Toddlers, Toddler Tapping Zoo, Fiete Kinderzoo): tapping an animal makes it move and sound. We add the choice: the heard word decides which.
- **Lift-the-flap peekaboo** (Peekaboo Barn, Spot's peek-a-boo books, via the hide-and-seek design): every flap reacts, right or not. B adds the part that shows.
- **Sago Mini:** no timers, and nothing leaves before it's asked.
- **Toca Boca:** everything reacts, and a miss gets a joke, not a frown.
- **Our own games:** the ride's road as data, the farm's Snap kit and animal actor, hide-and-seek's openable, the shared "say it" moment.
