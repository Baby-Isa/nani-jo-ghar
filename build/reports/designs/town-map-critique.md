# Critique: the town map design (`town-map.md`), 1 Nov 2026

Independent critic, flaws first. Checked against the lessons checklist, the design method, the two 31 Oct research reports, the audit, the master map and Zafar's answers, `game-settings-map`, CLAUDE.md, the rulebook, `guide-and-card.md`, `speaking.md`, `cast.md`, `days/README.md`, `days/shops.md`, the sibling designs (`shops.md`, `farm-animals.md`, `party-guests.md`, `pick-it-up-put-it-back.md`, `do-it-engine.md`), the word inventory CSV, `data/faces.json`, and both mock-up images. Nothing edited but this file.

## Findings, most serious first

### 1. MUST: at L1-L2 the deciding word is probably English, and the design knows it
- **Place:** §1 "A real risk to check with Mum", open question 1; §3 the leak test "Reading"; the ladder L1-L2.
- **Problem:** the only words that decide a move at L1 and L2 are the place names, and the design itself says "library", "post office", "school" and "station" are likely English loans in the family's Kutchi. A child with English and no Kutchi then hears "... library ..." inside a Kutchi sentence and taps the library: non-negotiable 4 fails on the two levels a 3-year-old spends most time in. The design's answer ("L1-L2 still teach the frame and the who; the test moves to L3-L4") concedes the leak instead of designing around it, and the leak bot as written ("plays recorded words only") can't catch it, because a loan word *is* a recorded word. Meanwhile the two places with real Kutchi in the repo today (*bajaar* in *bajaar mai vaan*, engine; *ghar*, engine) are kept out of L1-L2: the market is "a decoy from L3" and the house is "visit 3".
- **Evidence:** the inventory: `market` and `clinic` are "meaning-only (no Kutchi)", `Go to the market` has *bajaar mai vaan*, `house` has *ghar*; the master map rule 3 ("a game counts as teaching only with its deciding words recorded"); lessons §1 (AUD flaw 2).
- **Change:** (a) choose the first-visit 8 and the L1 three *after* Mum's answer, not before, and write that as the gate in §10; (b) put the market (*bajaar*) in L1 in place of the library, so at least one real Kutchi word decides from the first round and "let's go to the market" (F8) lands on a place the child has already sent someone to; (c) add a bot variant that treats every loan word as known and must still win under 10% at L2; (d) say plainly in §9 row 1 that until Mum confirms, L1-L2 are not counted as teaching.

### 2. MUST: from L2 the "who" is won by elimination, and the Sceptic maths is wrong
- **Place:** §3 "What happens in one errand" step 4 ("from L2 the next call doesn't wait for the walk, so two or three people can be out at once"); the face row ("a person out on an errand shows as a soft, faded circle"); the leak test "Eliminating" ("a person out on an errand can't be called ... with up to 2 out, 3 of 5 faces are still possible"); "The Sceptic at L2: 1 chance in 4 for the face".
- **Problem:** L2 has 4 faces, not 5. If calls come while people are out and the people out are faded and uncallable, the who is 1 in 4 for the first call, 1 in 3 for the second and 1 in 2 for the third. The design's "two words per move from L2" is then one and a half words, the bright faces are a visible shortlist, and the stated 1-in-20 odds are false. The research named this exact leak for this exact game ("as people leave, fewer badges and places remain"); the design fixed it for the end of the round (people come back) but reopened it mid-round.
- **Evidence:** research-other-games §2 "Where it leaks: elimination"; lessons §1 (two words per move); the design's own ladder (L2: 4 faces, 3 errands).
- **Change:** pick one and write it in the errand drawer: (a) the next call waits until at most one person is out (so the who is never worse than 1 in 3 of 4), or (b) anyone can be called, and a person who is out picks the new errand up on the way back (their walker turns at the stop; the call is "Nana, now the bakery"), so no face is ever off the table; and (c) at L2 keep faded faces tappable with a soft "busy" wave rather than greyed out, so the row never reads as a shortlist. Redo the Sceptic numbers with the rule chosen.

### 3. MUST: a miss costs nothing and is rewarded, so fishing wins; the tick rule contradicts itself
- **Place:** §3 step 3 "Wrong place" (the face stays lifted, the child tries again); "Each place's funny refusal" (12 of them); §4 step 6 (a miss, then the bakery, then "row 2 ticks"); §9 row 1 ("Ticks land only on a right first pick"); "Level up: after 3 rounds with at most one hint each".
- **Problem:** with 3 to 8 open places, tapping them in turn always wins, each wrong tap plays a different funny animation (the ducks, the hopping postbox, the balloon squeak), and nothing in §3 says what a miss costs: the row still ticks (§4), misses don't count against level-up (only hints do), and there is no first-go rule. Twelve rewarding refusals are an invitation to tap wrong on purpose. The lessons say "tick only the right item, first go" and the research's hide-and-seek fix is "only a first-try find earns the badge". §9 claims this is done; §4 shows it isn't.
- **Evidence:** lessons §1 (C1017 P62-63; CHT-14); research-other-games §3 "Opening everything is elimination"; the rulebook's word ladder ("a miss brings it back").
- **Change:** write the miss rule in §3 and code it: a first-go errand ticks solid; after a miss the row closes with a hollow tick (progress, no verdict); a miss resets that place word's ladder; level-up counts a round with any miss as a round with a hint; and each place's funny refusal plays once per round, after which a miss is the shared shake and the word, so the refusals stay a surprise and not a toy. Fix §4 step 6 and §9 row 1 to match.

### 4. MUST: the cast, the station and the L4 needs don't add up across phones and big screens
- **Place:** the ladder (L2 "4 faces (adds Masi)"; L3 "5 (Masi comes on the train in the first round; she stays)"); decision 14 ("Masi arrives by train from L3"); the places table (the station: "big; phones from visit 2"); L4 calls ("Ma is thirsty" → the café: "big; phones from visit 2"); decision 19 ("the same difficulty on every device"); prompt town-map-2 ("a young aunt in a lilac dupatta, and a second young woman in a mustard dupatta").
- **Problem:** four things clash. (a) Masi is added at L2 and arrives at L3: she can't do both. (b) Five faces at L3 needs a fifth person, and the people table has four in the row (Nana, Big Ma, Ma, Masi); prompt 2 quietly invents one in a mustard dupatta. (c) On phones the station isn't open on visit 1, so "Masi comes on the train" can't happen on phones at all. (d) "Ma is thirsty" needs the café, which phones don't have on visit 1, so L4 on phones has two needs plus the fire station, not three, and the look-alike pair differs by device, against decision 19's own aim.
- **Evidence:** the design's own tables; `days/README.md` (Masi is the beach's host); `cast.md` (Masi, Ma generic; no fifth woman).
- **Change:** one cast for visit 1 on every device: Nana, Ma, Big Ma from L1, Masi from L2, stepping off the same bus (the train is visit 2's delight, when the station opens everywhere). Four faces through L4. Make the L4 need set device-independent: poorly (clinic), play (park), cook (market), Masi's parcel (post office), and keep thirsty for visit 2 with the café. Remove the fifth woman from prompt 2 or name her (open question 3 is where Hannah Mami is decided, not a prompt).

### 5. MUST: two Nanis on one screen, against the shared guide-and-card rules
- **Place:** §3 "The pieces on screen" ("The bus stop sits at the map's edge, with Nani standing at it" and "Nani in the guide box, saying the current step"); §4 steps 2, 5, 7, 8; §7 ("Nani's bubble goes into free space over the sky strip"); all four prompts (a grandmother at the stop).
- **Problem:** `guide-and-card.md` R3 says a guide who is **on screen** speaks from a bubble beside her face, and only an off-screen guide uses the guide box; R4 says one face per person ("you see the same face twice ... it feels wrong", Zafar). Here Nani stands on the map *and* sits in the guide box, and her calls (F2-F4, F7-F8) are given to the box. The layout then also needs a bubble for the map Nani "over the sky strip", and at 740x320 the sky strip is about 25 px tall, so the bubble lands on a tile or a face. Non-negotiable 6 says the shared screens are never restyled per mode.
- **Evidence:** `guide-and-card.md` R3, R4; lessons §3 ("bubbles belong to faces", "one line per moment"), §6 ("nothing goes over a face").
- **Change:** pick one Nani. Recommended: Nani is the off-screen guide (the box, as in the pantry), and the bus stop holds the bus and the shopping bag only; the parade's "well done" and the mango come from the box, and in story mode she walks into the market as a walker only at the very end. If Zafar wants her on the map, the box must go and her lines become bubbles at the stop, with the bubble's free space written for every size.

### 6. MUST: the walker is the farm's animal actor again (reuse missed)
- **Place:** §0 engine ("one new piece, the walker"); §6 "New" row 1 ("shared ... the farm's animals home and hide and seek's cats could use it later"); decision 23.
- **Problem:** `farm-animals.md` already names "the animal actor: an animal that walks to a place and does an action ... a walk to a named place, and an action loop, all as data; the safari and the adoption centre reuse it", and `do-it-engine.md` defines actors, actions and a call queue. A person who walks a road path, enters a door, waits and comes back carrying a sprite is the same actor with a path and a carry slot. Designing it twice on the same night is the audit's §5 fault (one job built on several engines) repeated at the design stage.
- **Evidence:** `farm-animals.md` §0 and "New to build" 1; `do-it-engine.md` §0; the master map Part 4.
- **Change:** declare the walker as the animal actor with two additions (a multi-point road path and a carried-thing slot), owned by whichever design builds first, and count it in the farm's "new" list, not here. Drop decision 23 or rewrite it as "the actor gains a path and a carry slot".

### 7. MUST: the art list is three runs, not one, for a game the map sizes as "small"
- **Place:** §0 ("The art is the work: two base maps, 12 tiles in two states, five walking people, about 14 carried things"); §7 the art list (plus 2 houses, a garden, the bus, 3 walk frames × front and back × entering per person); §5 visits 2-4 (first-then, the speaking handover, identical houses, identical fruit stalls).
- **Problem:** that is about 70 pieces before faces. Non-negotiable 8 is one supervised ChatGPT run from one paste block that Zafar approves piece by piece; the research and the master map size this game as "small: a reskin plus a walk; the art is the new part". Visit 1 on phones uses 8 tiles, 4 walkers (Nani doesn't walk; see 5) and 8 carried things; the café, toy shop, station, cloth shop, houses, Masi's train arrival, the parcel and the kite are visit 2+ and don't need drawing now. Forms C, D and E are designed to the same depth as A and B though Zafar's scope says only "later: directions".
- **Evidence:** research-other-games §2 "Size"; the master map Part 5 row 7 ("the mock-up is done ... lab"); `art-run` (one block, backgrounds locked first); the method §3 (keep 6-8 per stage; park the rest).
- **Change:** split §7 into run 1 (the phone base map and the big base map, 8 tiles × 2 states, 4 walkers, 8 carried things, the bus) and later runs; count the pieces in the doc. Keep C, D and E as one-paragraph "later visit" notes in §5 with their leak lines, and take their full five-question tables out of the brief, so the builder's scope is A and B.

### 8. MUST: the face column doesn't fit with the cog at 915x275, and barely at 740x320
- **Place:** §7 layout ("915x275: the face column fits 5 circles at 48 px with 6 px gaps"; "740x320: 3 to 5 face circles of at least 52 px"; "the cog is top right of the play area").
- **Problem:** 5 × 48 + 4 × 6 = 264 px of 275; 5 × 52 + 4 × 6 = 284 px of 320. The cog sits top right of the play area, which is the top of the face column, so the cog and the top face share the same corner, with nothing left for the column's margins. The rulebook puts the cog's place per size in the central file, so the column must leave it room, not the other way round. The walker, drawn in a 125 px tile, is about 30 px tall, under the 44 px tap floor that the design cites for the hurry tap.
- **Evidence:** the design's own numbers; the rulebook ("The cog and menu"); lessons §6.
- **Change:** on phones, four faces at most on visit 1 (finding 4 helps), the column starting below the cog's reserved square; or put the faces along the map's bottom pavement strip as a row of 4 with the cog free at the top right. The hurry tap's hit area is the walker's whole tile, or tapping the road hurries whoever is on it. Then check it at all five sizes on the lab page, not on paper.

### 9. SHOULD: overlapping voices from L2
- **Place:** §3 step 4 ("from L2 the next call doesn't wait for the walk"); F5 ("I went to the library!" on the way back); §9 row 3 ("no overlapping voices").
- **Problem:** Nani's next call will land while a returning person is saying F5, and with three people out, three F5 lines can overlap each other. The design asserts the rule and breaks it in the same section.
- **Change:** one speech queue: a call waits for the current line; F5 is skipped (a wave only) when another line is due; never two voices at once. Say it in §3 and give the builder the queue from the do-it engine.

### 10. SHOULD: "no two errands in a row to the same place" lives only in §9 and narrows L1
- **Place:** §9 row 12 ("no two errands in a row go to the same place"); §3 ("a place can be asked twice"; "calls are drawn with replacement"); the leak test "Patterns".
- **Problem:** the rule isn't in the drawer's spec (§6 "The errand drawer"), so it's a builder question; and at L1 it makes the second errand 1 in 2 instead of 1 in 3, because the child knows it's not the park again. The same section claims "the answer's distance from the bus stop is spread evenly in the data", which can't be true when the tiles are shuffled per visit (decision 16): distance is only known at run time.
- **Change:** put the no-repeat rule in the drawer and make it "not the same place twice running from L2" (L1 has only three places); move the distance spread from "data" to the drawer's run-time draw and the bot's check.

### 11. SHOULD: the card's pictures make L1-L2 a picture match until the ladder hides them
- **Place:** §3 "The order card" ("the face and the place picture while those words are supported"); the leak test "Matching".
- **Problem:** at L1-L2 the card pops after the call and before the pick, with the face and the place picture on it; the child matches the picture to the building and never needs the word, for as long as "supported" lasts (three right picks in a row per word, over rounds of 2-3 errands). The rulebook allows support, but the party design made the stronger choice for the same situation: the picture shows only after a try, or on the hint clock, once a word is past support. Here the place picture is the whole answer.
- **Change:** at L1 keep the picture (the first round is a tutorial); from L2 the card shows the face and the Kutchi, and the place picture appears on the hint clock (7 s) or after a miss. Write it as the card rule for this game and make sure the leak bot's "reader" variant reads the picture too.

### 12. SHOULD: the first round leaves two moves undefined
- **Place:** §4 step 5 ("all three faces are live, and nothing glows"); §3 step 2-3.
- **Problem:** a child who taps a place before a face (likely at 3) gets nothing specified. "Tapping another face swaps the choice" is the undo, but what the lifted face does when the child then taps a closed place is "a soft knock" and the face stays lifted: fine. The place-first case is a builder question. The walkthrough also scripts a mistake as if it will happen ("the child taps the library (a mistake)") and counts it among "every action touched once"; a child who doesn't miss never sees the refusal shown.
- **Change:** place-first: the place does a small "who?" shrug and the faces bounce once; no sound. Say the refusal is shown on the first real miss, whenever it happens, not in the guided round.

### 13. SHOULD: the mango comes from a market nobody has visited

- **Problem:** at L1 and L2 the market is closed, and in the day the shops come *after* the map. Nani serves fruit from a shop that hasn't opened yet.

Big Ma is a real-likeness character whose photos haven't arrived, and the sheet-first rule means no Big Ma art until they do; she is an L1 face here. *dawa* (medicine) is in the engine, heard inside one line, so "to record, no Kutchi" is wrong. ### 16. SHOULD: two L4 needs and one carried thing don't quite fit
- **Place:** L4 calls ("we need to cook" → the market); the L4 needs table ("a story for Ali's bedtime" → the library); §2 stage 7 ("Nani reads Ali's new book to him").
- **Problem:** "we need to cook" with an open bakery beside the market is arguable for a 4-year-old (bread is food); the rule the design needs is "every L4 need has exactly one plausible open place", checked by Mum, not by us. Ali is the tall, lanky cousin (`cast.md`); a bedtime story is Isa the baby's. Small, but the family will notice.
- **Change:** write the one-plausible-place rule into the drawer with the bot checking it per open set; make the bedtime story for Isa, or make the library's L4 need "Nana wants to read" (*read* has no home anywhere else in the map, Part 3).

### 17. SHOULD: the prompts ask for sizes ChatGPT doesn't make, and prompt 2 is the "too busy" test
- **Place:** prompt 1 ("about 2.4:1"), prompt 3 ("about 3.3:1"), prompt 2 (12 places, 3 walkers, a bus stop and 5 faces in one 16:9), prompt 4 ("must look exactly alike").
- **Problem:** the image model renders 1:1, 3:2, 2:3 or 16:9-ish; a 3.3:1 ask comes back 16:9 with the scene squashed or with bars, and the layout judgement is then worthless. Prompt 2 reproduces the very density the 31 Oct mock-up was testing ("does 12 tip into busy?"), with three carried things that must read at phone size; it will muddle. Prompt 4's four identical houses will come back four slightly different houses, because the model can't repeat a tile; in the game the identity comes from one tile drawn once and reused in code. None of the prompts produce text or name real people (good), and prompt 3 attaches Nani's sheet while saying "placeholders, not real people", which should say that the grandmother at the stop is Nani from the sheet and only the row faces are placeholders.
- **Change:** ask prompts 1 and 3 for 16:9 with the strip composition drawn inside guide bars, and judge the crop; cut prompt 2 to 8 open places and two walkers, or run it as the busyness test and say so; in prompt 4 ask for one house tile on the plot nearest the park plus three plain "same house" copies, and note for the judge that identity is a code property; fix prompt 3's likeness line.

### 18. SHOULD: the lit box on a face circle needs its exception written
- **Place:** §4 step 3 ("The lit box on Nana's face").
- **Problem:** lessons §2 says the box never crosses a face. Here the face circle is the target, so the box must go on it. The safari critique hit the same wall.
- **Change:** a breathing gold ring round the circle (the circle's own glow), not a box over it; write it as the shared face circle's lit state, so every game that lights a face uses the same thing.

## Verdict

The bones are right and they are Zafar's: one gesture, a tilted map, people who walk off and come back carrying something, "I want a book" as the step up, and needs not drawn on any sign above that. The delight is real (the duck, the helmet, the parade) and the day fits. But the design claims leak-proofing it hasn't done: its L1-L2 deciding words are probably English, the who is a visible shortlist from L2, and a miss costs nothing while being rewarded twelve different ways, so a child with no Kutchi can fish through the open places and level up. Its cast and ladder don't agree with each other or across devices, it puts Nani on screen twice against the shared rules, it re-invents the farm's actor, and it asks for three art runs for a game the map sized as small. Fix the eight MUSTs (most are a paragraph each in §3, §7 and §10, plus a cut to the art list) before this goes to Zafar as a numbered list; the SHOULDs can ride with the brief.
