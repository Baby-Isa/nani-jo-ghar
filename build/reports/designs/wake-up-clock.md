## 0. One line

Three people sleep in three beds. At bedtime Nani tells the child each one's wake time. A big digital wall clock runs fast through the night and pauses at each hour, and the child wakes each person at their time. **Day:** the school day, which it opens (Zafar to confirm the order, decision 18). **Status:** new → designed (this doc), **waiting on Mum** (the gate below). **Engine:** one new small engine (a running clock, plus tap a person). Everything else comes from the shared kit (order card, guide box, bulb and eye, word card, the light, rules, speech). **Rough size:** small to medium. It needs a lab test first (master map Part 5, row 15). One background in two lighting states, about 18 character poses and a set of clock digits.

### The gate: Mum first (before any art run or lab page)

The whole game rests on one unasked question: **does the family say the time in Kutchi at all?** 0 of the 12 deciding words have their own clip, 6 of them (five to ten) are class-handout drafts Mum has never confirmed, eleven and twelve aren't in the repo, and the hour form ("___ o'clock") is unknown. So the order is:

1. **One voice note to Mum** (Zafar sends it; Claude writes it): "How do you say 'wake me at seven'? And 'it's seven o'clock'? And six, eight, nine, ten?" Plus: "Do you say the time in Kutchi or in English at home?"

3. **If the family says the time in English:** the number no longer decides, and this game can only teach the frame and morning or night. It goes back to Zafar as a numbered choice (park it, or rebuild it around the part of the day); nothing is built.
4. **The mock-ups (§11)** can go ahead before her answer only if Zafar wants to see the scene regardless. They cost his time, not Mum's.

## 3. The game

One stage with one core mini-game, **wake them at their time**, plus its level twists. A clock game doesn't need a pool of variants: the research says clock apps are drills with a short shelf life, so it stays small and funny, and the variety comes from who sleeps where, what time each wants and the gags.

**The why:** tomorrow is school. Nani knows what time everyone must get up, and she makes the child her alarm clock for the night. The child is the alarm: they don't set times, they ring.

**The people:** Nana (a grandfather in glasses), Ma (the child's mother) and Ali (the cousin, about 7). Simba sleeps curled on the foot of a random bed. Nani speaks from the guide box.

**The scene:** Nani's big bedroom at night. Three beds stand side by side, headboards on the back wall. Each has a bedside table with a small digital alarm. A big digital clock hangs on the wall above the middle bed, with a small moon or sun beside its digits. A window to one side shows the moon, then the dawn, then the sun.

### The steps (one round)

1. **Bedtime (the asks).** Each sleeper in turn sits up, front-facing, and nods while Nani says "Ali wakes up at six" (the bubble sits beside the sleeper, over the blanket). Their card pops big beside the side panel, then flies into its row. The card's picture is **their bedside alarm** (digits at L1, frosted from L2), with a speaker and the Kutchi. Their real bedside alarm lights up with the time. They lie down and snore softly. During the asks a tap in the play area skips the line and lands the card (the kit's rule); no bed is live yet.
2. **Goodnight.** Nani: "goodnight" (ambience). The lamp clicks off and the room goes blue with moonlight.
3. **The clock runs.** The minutes whizz (4:58, 4:59 ...) for about 1.5 s between hours.
4. **It pauses at each hour** (his yes, 1 Nov) for 6 s at L1, about 15% less a level, set in `data/rules.json`. **Every pause is the same length whether anyone is due or not**, so the pause is never a tell. At L1 Nani says "it's five".
5. **The child decides:** tap the sleeper whose time it is, or nobody. **The wall clock is not a button** (decision 6); there's no hurry tap. Tapping a card's speaker holds the clock while its line plays, so listening again is free. The holds at one pause add up to at most `holdMaxS` (6 s, in data); after that a replay still plays but the clock moves on (a late wake costs nothing).
6. **A right tap:** the alarm rattles, the sleeper bolts upright with wild hair, yawns and stretches. From L2 Nani adds "seven: get up!" (the hour is said only **after** the move). The row ticks and the clock runs on.
7. **A tap on the wrong person, or at the wrong hour:** a small shake, then the sleeper shakes their head with a sleepy "mm-mm", rolls over (face still to us) and pulls the blanket up, and Nani whispers *hever na* ✓. Each person has their own funny roll-over. Nothing else changes and the child can tap again at once (redone on the spot).
8. **Missed hour:** nothing happens and the sleeper sleeps on. After their hour, a tap wakes them just as happily. **Late is never a verdict** (his rule).
9. **The end of the stretch:** if anyone is still asleep when the clock reaches its last hour (sun high), Nani comes in, opens the curtains and they all get up smiling.
10. **The end:** curtains open on today's sky. All three stretch at the window. Nani comes in with chai for Nana and Ma and milk for Ali ("aah"), then says *shabash, beta* ✓ and "the bus comes at eight". The word card shows the round's new hour.

**Taps during a line (written so the builder doesn't guess):** once the clock runs, **a bed tap during any line is a move**: the line stops and the tap is judged at the hour on the wall clock, never lost (lessons §4). A tap elsewhere in the play area during a line skips it, as the kit does. The speech bubble and the side panel never skip (the kit's `skip.never`). A first-time showing (the light) is never skipped; a tap on the lit sleeper is the move it waits for. Build: `data/rules.json` `skip` gains a per-game list of move targets (`skip.moves`, selectors whose tap stops the line and counts as a move); the clock's beds are its first entry.

**The window toy:** in any pause the window can be tapped: the moon bobs, a star twinkles, at dawn a bird flies past. It never moves the clock and never counts. It gives the hands something harmless to do in a nobody hour.

**Counting (first tap only, his rule; never shown):** a sleeper counts as **right** when the first tap at their hour lands on them and they weren't helped (no eye on their card, no helper card naming them). A tap on them at an earlier wrong hour doesn't spoil them; the lab runs the leak bot on this rule and on the stricter one (also no earlier wrong tap) and keeps this kinder one if it passes. Only right wakes move the word ladder. **The tick exception, written for the row:** a late, fished or helped wake still ticks the row, because the sleeper is visibly up (the tick tells the child nothing they can't see) and progress shows; this is the clock's written exception to "tick only the right item, first go" (lessons §1). There is no accuracy badge: the end card shows the ticks and the bulb only.

**Level up (an override, written in the game's row and coded):** the central rule (`advance`: 3 consecutive finished rounds with at most one guide hint; the eye and the bulb never count) would let a reader peek every card and climb to L4 by reading digits. For this game **a round counts towards level-up only when all three wakes are right** (first tap at their hour, no eye, no bulb). It takes 3 counted rounds at a level; a round that doesn't count neither counts nor resets the count (kinder, and a peeked round can never count, so reading never climbs). Never down. A grown-up can pick any level. Build: `data/rules.json` `advance` gains a per-game override (`advance.perGame.clock = {counts: "allRight", consecutive: false}`), read by `js/shared/rules.js`.

**Hints (an override):** the kit's timed guide hint (7, 9, 11, 13 s from the last action) is **off for this game**: with the clock held, waiting 9 s would open the helper card with the next sleeper, which is waiting's win. The bulb is the only help, by the child's choice. Build: `data/rules.json` `levels` gains a per-game switch (`levels.perGame.clock.hintS = [null, null, null, null]`), read by `js/shared/rules.js` and `tokens.js`. The helper card (the bulb's) shows the next sleeper's **face** and says their hour in Kutchi; at L1 it also shows the digits and the English, and **from L2 never digits and never English** (it follows the level's `cardPicture` and `cardEnglish` flags, so its shape doesn't change). A sleeper named by the helper card doesn't move the ladder, and the round doesn't count for level-up.

### The level ladder (each adds one thing; the Kutchi gets harder, the tap never changes)

Empty pauses (nobody due) are capped at about 2-3 a round at every level, so a round has 5-6 pauses and stays under about 90 s.

| Level | What it adds | Times and stretch | Support |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L1: hear it and see it** | the core | 3 hours from 5-9, never all three adjacent; the clock runs from 4:58 to the last asked hour, so the pauses are 5 to 9 at most (2 empty at most) | bedside digits shown (big screens); the card's alarm picture shows the digits (every layout); Nani says each hour at its pause; pause 6 s |
| **L2: hear it only** | **the number decides**: bedside alarms frosted, card alarm frosted, and the hour isn't said at the pause (a soft chime only; said after a wake) | 3 hours inside a 5-6 hour stretch drawn anywhere in 1-12 (so the stretch moves round, and 10-12 come in); 2-3 empty pauses | the eye un-blurs one card and its alarm (costs a bulb; that sleeper doesn't count); pause 5 s |
| **L3: morning or night** | **the part of the day**: the night runs in two parts, an evening part and a morning part of three hours each, joined by one fast-forward that always looks the same (the moon sweeps across, about 2 s). The two parts share at least one hour, and at least one ask is on a shared hour, so it comes round twice. "Ali wakes up at seven in the morning", "Nana at ten at night." The moon or sun on the wall clock settles which seven it is, once the word is known | the parts drawn from a small set in data (for example 7-9 pm with 6-8 am; 9-11 pm with 7-9 am); the asks drawn from the six pauses independently of who asks, so the parts tell nothing about who is due | as L2; pause 4.3 s |
| **L4: before and after** | **a relation**: two of the three asks name the third sleeper, in one round ("Ma wakes up **after** Ali", "Nana **before** Ali" = the hour after and before Ali's), so *before* and *after* must be told apart. The pair counts together: if the first of the two is woken right, both count; if not, neither (the second would be settled by elimination) | as L3 | as L3; pause 3.7 s |

**No look-alike at L4.** The rulebook's L4 look-alike (`levels.lookalike`) is off for this game: the repo's pairs (*sat*/*ath*, *no*/*do*) are unconfirmed drafts, one probably wrong. After Mum's number round, any hours that sound alike in her recordings go in `data/clock.json` as a pair list. That list keeps them out of the same speaking round. Using them as a challenge is a later choice for Zafar, not a level.

**How many:** three sleepers at every level. This overrides the rulebook's "L1 is 1", which the rulebook allows for one level, written in its row: three beds are his design, and a round of one is over before it starts. The first-ever round walks the child through the three one at a time.

**Rulebook mapping:** L1 is the core (yes asks only, everything shown). L2 removes the visual (the rulebook blurs the picture at L3, but here the digits *are* the answer, so they blur a level early). L3 is other ways of saying it (with the part of the day). L4 is added complexity (a relation). No "no" asks at any level (`noAsks` off for this game; a "don't wake me" sleeper is an idea, §5).

### The leak test

| Way to win without Kutchi | At L1 | From L2 | The fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reading** | digits on the alarms, the card and the wall: a digit-reader wins. Allowed, because the words are supported (taught; right picks while supported move the ladder, 29 Oct) | no digits except the wall clock; the card's English is dotted and its alarm frosted; the helper card shows no digits and no English | frosted from L2; the un-blur is the eye, costs a bulb, and that sleeper never moves the ladder; **a round with any eye or bulb never counts towards level-up** (override above), so reading never climbs |
| **Matching** | digit to digit (supported) | sound to sound isn't possible: the hour is never said **before** the move from L2, only after a right wake | the hour is said at the pause only at L1 |
| **Eliminating** | n/a | the answer space is open: 5-6 pauses times 3 sleepers, plus "nobody". Knowing two sleepers' hours says nothing about the third's. Nobody wakes by themselves, so there's no countdown to read. At L4 the relation pair counts together, so the second of the pair can't be won by elimination | nothing to eliminate |
| **Patterns** | n/a | bed order shuffled every round; times random from the level's set; never the same person-to-time pairing two rounds running; grown-ups don't always wake first; the card row is in bed order, never time order; **Simba sleeps on a random bed**, never tied to the wake order; every pause is the same length; L3's fast-forward always looks the same | shuffle and random draw, in data |
| **Waiting** | n/a | waiting wins nothing: **no timed hint** (override); sleepers never stir before their hour; the bedside alarm never flashes or rings on its own; no glow on the due sleeper (the lit box is only in the first-ever round); the sky doesn't track anyone; sleepers left asleep are woken by Nani at the end, which counts nothing | no tell before the hour; hints off; holds capped; late wakes count nothing |

**The Sceptic at L2:** she hears Nani's hour at bedtime and sees 7:00 on the wall. Without knowing the Kutchi for seven she can only tap at random. If she taps every bed at every hour, everyone eventually gets up, but no round counts. **The leak bot** (a learned save, one random bed or "nobody" per pause): at each sleeper's hour it lands on that sleeper first about 1 time in 4, so all three are right in about 1.6% of rounds (1 in 4, three times). Target: under 10% of rounds at L2 to L4. The bot runs through `WhichOne`'s blind-odds calculator (§6), on both counting rules.

**The open need the picture can't settle:** three identical frosted alarms and three sleeping faces. Nothing on screen says who is next.

### The delight moment

- **The wake:** the alarm rattles on the table (a tilt, no motion lines), the sleeper sits bolt upright, blinks, hair wild, a big yawn and stretch. Each has their own:
  - **Ali** springs up and his pillow flops onto the floor.
  - **Nana** gropes for his glasses and puts them on upside down, then right.
  - **Ma** sits up, smooths her hair and dupatta, then does a big double stretch.
  - **Simba**, asleep on whichever bed he chose tonight, pops up as that sleeper wakes and lands on all fours with a cross little shake.
- **The roll-overs** (each different, face still to us): Nana's snore gets louder; Ma waves a sleepy hand and pulls the quilt to her chin; Ali sticks one foot out of the blanket and wiggles his toes.
- **The sky:** moon, stars, a pink dawn, then the sun. The curtains swing open at the end.
- **"Kind of funny":** Simba is the running joke. He picks a different bed every night, and the child learns to wait for the launch.

Calm faces and gentle motion throughout (lessons §7: no crazy animation).

### The speaking moment: call the hour

**Opens:** by the speaking ladder (`docs/game-design/speaking.md`), on visit 2 at L2 or above. Never at L1. This is the only later-visit piece kept in this design.

1. **Watch:** from L2 Nani says "seven: get up!" after every right wake. That's many models before the handover.
2. **Handover:** at a right tap, the alarm gives one rattle and the sleeper half-wakes, front-facing. Nani asks "what time is it?" (to record). The mic bubble appears over the child, with three spoken pills: the round's three asked hours, never two that are on Mum's look-alike list (§3, in data).
3. The child says the hour (or taps a pill) and the sleeper bolts up with the usual gag. A wrong pick doesn't stop the wake: Nani says the right hour herself (the recast) and the sleeper gets up. There's no verdict, and the wake already counted on the tap.

It's a play action, not a gate. The tap made the wake, and the speaking is the flourish on top. It's a closed set of 3 with pills always there, and the mic shows that it's listening and what it heard.

## 4. The child's first-ever round, screen by screen

1. **The bedroom at bedtime.** A warm lamp is on, the window is dark blue with the moon, and three beds have their sleepers sitting up. The side panel on the left is empty. Nothing is lit for a moment of quiet.
2. **Show what isn't on the card.** One lit box with a gold glow hugs the wall clock: it whizzes 4:58 to 5:00 and stops (this is what moves). Everything else stays dimmed.
3. **The asks.** Ali (middle bed) sits up front-facing and nods; Nani: "Ali wakes up at six" (bubble beside him, read-along underline). His card pops big beside the side panel (the alarm picture showing 6:00, a speaker, the Kutchi), then flies into its row. His bedside alarm shows 6:00, and he lies down. Nana (right bed): "Nana wakes up at seven"; card; alarm. Ma (left bed): "Ma wakes up at nine"; card; alarm.
4. **Goodnight.** Nani in the guide box: "goodnight". The lamp clicks off and the room turns moonlit blue. The guide box says "wake them at their time" (Kutchi; to record).
5. **The first wake, held.** The clock whizzes 5:58 to **6:00**: the first pause is Ali's, so the first thing the child does works. The clock **holds** (the first-time hold, as for a timed control) and the lit box hugs Ali's bed, his alarm and the wall digits together. Nani: "it's six". The child taps Ali. The alarm rattles, Ali bolts upright, his pillow flies, he yawns. His row ticks.
6. **On its own now.** The light is gone (shown once). At 7:00 the pause is 6 s. The child taps Nana: glasses upside down, then Simba's launch (Simba chose Nana's bed tonight). Tick. If they tap Ma by mistake, she shakes her head and pulls up the quilt, Nani whispers *hever na*, and they can tap Nana straight away.
7. **A quiet hour.** 8:00: nobody. The sky turns pink and the sun peeks up. A tap on a bed gets a roll-over and *hever na*, which is how the child learns "not now", now that a wake has been felt. A tap on the window makes a bird fly past.
8. **The last wake.** 9:00: the child taps Ma, who does her double stretch. Tick. All three rows are ticked.
9. **The end.** The curtains swing open on today's sky (the day's weather). All three stretch at the window. Nani comes in with two cups of chai and a glass of milk on a tray, they drink ("aah"), and Nani says *shabash, beta* ✓ and then "the bus comes at eight". The word card is the round's new hour (six), with its speaker. It hands on to the door. In free play, the shared two-page end screen comes here.

**Length:** about 60 seconds. Nothing is ever lost, and the only wait is the clock's short pause at each hour.

## 8. What it borrows from other games

- **"OK to wake" children's clocks** (Claessens Kid'Sleep, OK to Wake!, Sun to Moon Sleep Clock): a sleeping face that wakes, a night and day display and a digital time. Children already know these from their own bedrooms, so the bedside alarm needs no explaining. Borrowed: the moon or sun **on the clock**, beside the digital digits.
- **Toca Town:** waking the characters is one of the touches reviewers mention. Borrowed: the wake as a comic reaction, different for each person.
- **Animal Crossing's hourly change:** the world marks each hour with a chime. Borrowed: the soft chime at each hour's pause.
- **Kids Tell Time:** gentle guidance when wrong. Borrowed: the recast (Nani says the right hour in call-the-hour) instead of a verdict.
- **Sago Mini (stated design):** no ticking timer. Borrowed: the pause at every hour and the speaker holding the clock.
- **Monki Hide and Seek:** the spots change after every find. Borrowed: shuffle the beds, the times and Simba's bed every round.
