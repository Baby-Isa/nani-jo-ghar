## Reasoning, flaws first

### 1. Top-down versus side-on

Flaws in the mocks: every daar mock mixes cameras in the band (`daar-20.png`: top-down katoris beside side-on jars on one ledge; `daar-07.png`, `daar-08.png`: side-on jars on a flat band under a top-down scene, which is chai's arrangement and fine). The "30-degree compromise" would be a third camera, a third batch of art and a band whose bowls and jars never agree; the bible's rule (§3, one camera per scene; §5 of the s18 review: the camera belongs to the surface) already gives the answer, so I would not open it.

The test at 42 px (my sheet of `assets/cook/items-s17-stove/f-*-jar.webp` against `t-*-katori.webp`): a jar reads as a coloured column with a lid, a katori as a coloured disc. Turmeric, chilli and salt read either way; cumin against mustard against coriander seeds read in neither, only their pills tell them apart. So the camera is not what makes spices readable; the pill is. Choose the camera by the game's other things. Spices in a jar on chai's and eggs' shelf; in a katori on daar's, corn's and sekelo's tray. Sauces the same: `f-tamarind-chutney.webp` on a shelf game, `t-tamarind-pulp-katori.webp` on a tray game; both exist. Bottles, cartons, the egg box and the yoghurt tub only read standing up (`f-water-bottle.webp`, `f-milk-carton.webp`, `f-eggs-in-a-box.webp`), which is why chai, eggs and baking are shelf games. Cubes, cobs, dough balls and pastry sheets only read lying down, which is why the rest are tray games. Where a tray game needs a bottle (none does today), the bottle goes in a bowl as its sauce.

Clinic ([15]): its items are on a belt and a tool column with their own design; moving them to a band for consistency's sake is a redesign with no play reason yet. Recommend only: when the clinic's screens are next redone, the same rule decides.

### 2. The tray and the bowls

`corn-05.png` is a flat plank with a faint lip and quiet grain; `corn-06.png` has a real raised rim and a louder, darker grain. At 740x320 the band is 96 px tall; a rim is two thin lines, cheap, and it says "tray on the worktop", which a flat plank does not. The loud grain of 06 competes with the bowls, which the bible says must be the most saturated thing. So his instinct is right: 06's shape, 05's grain, the rim thin. One tray picture, cut with its contact shadow, stretched in width by code (the grain is fine enough to repeat).

Bowls: glass (`chaat-23.png`'s band, today's `assets/cook/items-s17-stove/t-small-glass-bowl.webp`) loses its rim at 42 px; steel katoris on walnut flash, as today's corn tray shows (my real shot at 740x320); the cream ceramic bowl of `samosa-17.png`, `samosa-18.png` and `samosa-31.png` reads at 42 px and sits on walnut. One bowl for every tray game so the eye learns one thing. He is right that walnut bowls on a walnut tray vanish (`chaat-23.png` right side).

### 3. Daar

How it is cooked at home (to check with Mum, the authority; the recipe is hers, not mine): wash and soak the daar a while, boil it soft with hardar and loon (a pressure cooker in most homes), whisk it smooth, then the vaghar: hot oil or ghee, rai, jeeru, lasan, mirchi, curry leaves, sometimes onion and tomato, poured over, then a stir. As mechanics: soaking is waiting (leak test, non-negotiable 7); boiling is chai's heat ring again, which teaches nothing new here; whisking is the stir we have. The tadka is where daar's own words live (jeeru, rai, lasan, mirchi, the kitchen's first meeting with them, per `kitchen.md`), so it stays; the order Nani says is the mechanic.

Pouring from above ([9]): a stream with no visible source is the one thing a top-down camera cannot show, and `daar-21.png` proves it (cumin falls from nowhere). Chai's way, the bowl beside the pot tilting with a stream to the rim, is built (`scene.pourMs` in `data/stations/daar.json`, the shared pour SH-117), consistent, and animates well. Keep it.

The pot: today's pot is already 580 of the scene's 630 design px (`potR` 290), so `daar-08.png` is close to what we have once the hob is the kit's; take 08's proportions and drop the dial when speeds go, which frees the right side for the daar bowl and the katori. `daar-07.png` has a hand (no hands, H13) and a fringe of lemon and coriander that daar never uses.

Stir ([6], [17]): today the badge counts laps but it is small and the target never shows (right: showing the target would let a child match). Clearer without stopping: a row of pips under the pot, one lit per lap done, and Nani's voice counting each lap as it closes; "bas" at the number; stirring on past it only costs the score, as now. Flaw to log: the bare numbers (hakro, ba, trae) are heard only inside longer lines, so counting aloud is a recording gap for Mum until then (grey italic rule). First time: the ladle itself makes one slow ghost lap with the finger, then the child takes over. Speed: his read is right; "aste thi / jaldi" needs a lesson the game cannot give at L1. Take it out of L1-L3 and bring it back at L4 with a word moment (CHAI-67's system: two pots side by side, one stirred slowly, one quickly, the words heard), the way chai teaches half and full. The tortoise and hare dial goes.

### 4. Chaat

Top-down with an opaque bowl is right: `chaat-13.png` and `chaat-23.png` read as food; today's glass bowl (my `assemble` shot) is a grey trough at 740x320 and its side-on layers were flat bands (cook.md §14's own complaint). Items on the tray, not around the bowl: at L4 chaat has up to ten things (CHT-17), and ten bowls round a bowl shrink it and move every round.

How a studio layers a dish: Toca Boca-style kitchens never render combinations. Each topping is one transparent sprite on the same registered canvas as the bowl, drawn in the order it was added; the base layer fills the bowl, later layers are smaller or have gaps (a chutney as drizzle lines, sev as loose strands, dhania as leaves), so what is beneath still shows at the edges. Ten toppings are ten pictures plus the empty bowl; chaat's ten side-on strips (`assets/cook/items-s08b/chaat-strips/`) are exactly this idea, just in the wrong camera, and `assets/cook/items/chaat-bowl-full-t.png` shows the finished look to aim at. His base-combination renders (4 to 8 of potato and chana) are not needed: two scatter pictures that overlap do it, and the order is whichever was added second on top.

His `chaat-side-on-zafar.png`: the ledge, pills and jars are handsome, but the bowl shows two flat layers; six layers in a bowl 130 px tall at 740x320 are 15 px each, unreadable; the tick button is out (principle 2); the recessed glowing shelf is not chai's stone ledge (one shelf everywhere, non-negotiable 6). Keep it as baking's camera reference (question 8).

### 5. Samosa

One sheet at a time on `samosa-18.png`'s rounded-rectangle board: yes; today's six strips (`samosa-740x320.png`) are each 40 px tall on his phone and the fold never read. The sheet stack top left, a tap brings one that grows as it flies (a tween, cheap); the steel plate right with the asker's face badge (chai's tray logic) collects them; when the asked number is on it the fry opens by itself. Fillings: the same stacking rule as chaat, one transparent blob per filling on the sheet (`assets/cook/items/v3/samosa/fill-*.webp` are close), so every combination is free; a sprite sheet of 120 combinations is the wrong tool and would be redrawn every time a filling changes.

The fold: a real samosa is a cone fold from a strip, three moves, which is what the coded fold does today and what failed with him. His two-corner fold on a square sheet is two diagonal swipes: the first corner over the filling to a third from the far corner, the second corner over that. It reads as a triangle in two moves, every fold the same direction as the arrow, and the coded pastry fold (`Cook.Pastry`) already bends a texture along a line, so it is buildable. Two swipes, not one, so the fold is still a thing the child does. Fry: `samosa-31.png` (or `samosa-02.png` without its serving tray): karahi on the hob, the paper plate, no second tray since the band already has one; each samosa gets chai's heat ring and a tap to lift (the jharo lifts it, no hand). Realism of the oil is pre-rendered pictures cross-faded plus code bubbles (bible §8).

### 6. Corn

Use sekelo's grill layout for corn: his `sekelo-threading-zafar-pick.png` proportions for the board, `sekelo-47.png`'s grill with deeper trays. For corn the left column is the raw crate above (`corn-05.png`'s is good) and the person's plate below, the grill tall at centre-right. Fit, trays aligned with the grill's top and bottom: the scene is 224 CSS px tall at 740x320, so a grill of about 200 px gives two trays of about 95 px each and cobs in them about 70 px long: readable, tight; comfortable at 930x430 and above. `corn-26.png`'s tongs and brush are clutter.

Spices: per cob, as it lands on its person's plate, the plate lit, the dressing tapped; this is chai's "pour into the glass" step and keeps the taps to three per cob (on, turn, off) plus one per dressing. One cob at a time at L1-L2 so nothing needs turning while a child dresses; several from L3, with the green windows at chai's floor (CK-65 already sets greenMs from chai). Batch-dressing all cobs the same way would break the per-person orders corn already has (`Muke hakro makai khape` with amli ji chutney and limu).

### 8. Baking

The sheet's purpose (`cook-baking-eggs.md` §0, B1): baking is "the only game about how much": a little, a whole cup, heaped, half, two and a half, the pour stopping at the word. A level in a jug is a side-on fact; from above a jug is a disc with no level. So baking is the side-on game, camera E, as designed, and the mocks (`baking-09.png`, `baking-14.png`, `baking-29.png`) are wrong for it: a top-down bowl and tray with no jug at all. Lines: his sheet of 1 Nov said no lines, the cup ticks at the word; chai draws dashed half and full lines on every cup, never answering the order (chai-tray.json). Chai's two lines on the jug keep the family consistent and give the word something to land on; a little and a lot stay bands with no line. His `chaat-side-on-zafar.png` is the camera and ledge for baking's counter, with chai's stone ledge in place of the recess, the oven at the right as the sheet has it. Pragmatism, as he says: nobody minds one side-on game, and pantry already is.
