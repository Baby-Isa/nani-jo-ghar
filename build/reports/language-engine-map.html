How the language engine builds a sentence

Nani jo Ghar, 12 November 2026. The parts of the engine, one sentence passing through them, and how complete each part of Kutchi grammar is.

The picture: layers, not a hub

A sentence is built in six steps, always in the same order, and each step needs the one before it. The engine cannot choose munji over munjo until it knows chai is a she-word, and it cannot voice a word it has not built.

The hub picture is right about one thing. The engine’s own code holds no Kutchi. Everything it knows sits in stores of data beside it, and each step reads from its own store. Context (polite, or to a child) is not a store. It is a note that travels with the request and changes which form a step picks.

munji
“My”, matched to chai, a she-word. For a he-word it would be munjo.
chai
From the word store. A she-word keeps its form before “in”.
me
“In”. It goes after its noun.
ba
Two. Only “one” changes for he-words and she-words.
khun
Sugar, counted, so the plural form. For khun that is the same word.
wijij
“Put in”, in its polite form. Said the everyday way, the sentence ends khape.

How a score is made. Each area has a short list of things the engine needs to know. Each is worth 1 if Mum said it and the engine holds it (or Kutchi has no such thing), a half if it was heard once, is unclear, waits on a ruling or is not built yet, and 0 if nobody has asked. The score is the share of points, to the nearest 5. A spelling still to confirm does not lower a score, and recordings are counted under vocabulary.

The lists, each with its source in Mum’s answers, are in docs/language/grammar-kb.md (score sheet). The plain-words guide is docs/language/engine-map.md. Counts come from data/lang/ as built on 12 November. This page is a snapshot: the scores are re-made after every recording session.
