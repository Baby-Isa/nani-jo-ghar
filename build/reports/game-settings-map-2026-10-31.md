## 4. The three home games: what each should lean on

They overlap in setting (Nani's house) and a little in positions. That's fine if each owns a different kind of word.

| | Hide and seek | Pick it up and put it back | Clean-up day |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Owns** | the house's **places and containers**: the cupboard, under the bed, behind the curtain, the wardrobe, the box | **positions relative to things:** on, under, next to, this side, that side | **verbs done to the house:** turn off, switch off, open, close, sweep, wipe, put away |
| **The child hears** | "look in the cupboard" (it glows at first) | "my bag is on the table in the kitchen"; then "put it under the chair" | "turn off the tap", "close the curtains" |
| **The fun** | the reveal: the cats are, or aren't, there | the doll's house; carrying things between rooms | the dirt disappearing, the light going off |
| **Comes** | first (learn the places) | second (the places plus positions) | third (verbs on places and things) |
| **What it teaches** | room and container names, "where?", open and close | positions, household nouns, owners | home verbs, describing (dirty and clean, on and off) |

**Zafar's day at home** (hide and seek, then pick up and put back, then the clean-up) uses exactly this order. I recommend it as one day, with each game also playable alone.

**What already exists:** Tidy up (parked) has "put away", the dastarkhwan, the sweet box and the shoe mountain. Find it has "where". These two new games are their natural successors. Per non-negotiable 1, Zafar decides whether Tidy up's mini-games fold into them or stay.

## 5. My additions and pushback

1. **Leak points in the new ideas** (non-negotiable 4: no winning by matching or eliminating):
   - **Pick it up:** if there's one bag, the child sees it and never needs the words (Zafar noted this). Fix: two or three of the same thing in different places ("my bag on the table", Ali's bag under the bed), so the place words decide.
   - **Put it back:** outline **all** the spots (on, under, this side, that side), never just the right one, or it's won by elimination. Zafar's version does this.
   - **"This side / that side" before left and right** (Mum's earlier advice, idea 11).
   - **The clock:** matching the bedside digits to the wall clock is number matching, not Kutchi. At level 1 that's fine (supported). From level 2, each person **says** their time and the bedside clock is blurred: the heard number decides, with the un-blur as the help.
   - **The town map's "I want a book"** level is leak-proof and reuses every noun; a strong idea.
2. **"Where does it hurt?" over a skeleton game.** A skeleton teaches bone words a child doesn't need, and Kutchi may not have them. A body chart where the patient says where it hurts covers 20+ body words with one mechanic, inside the clinic we have.
3. **The clinic extensions are cheap reskins:**
   - the nose as the ear game's twin
   - the finger and the head on the scrape game's bandage
   - the tummy on the tongue game's drinks

   Hair already has a game.
4. **"Careful" already has a home:** the clinic's foot game is the thorn game (pull the splinter out without touching the edge; touching makes the patient wince). Nani's "careful" on a wince is natural there.
5. **Feelings, an idea for Zafar's open question:** at the party each guest has a need you can see:
   - sad (the balloon flew off): give a balloon
   - tired: a chair
   - hot: a fan
   - cold: a shawl
   - thirsty: water
   - hungry: food

   The guest says how they feel; the child picks what helps, then the guest smiles. It's real life (looking after guests is very Nani), it's the party's job, and the feeling word decides.
6. **Verbs, the biggest remaining gap.** Moving-yourself verbs (run, jump, sit, stand, walk, dance, stop) lost their home with the park and sports day.
   - **My fix:** musical statues at the party (dance, stop, sit, jump, turn), as "Nani says, do it".
   - The mind verbs go to hide and seek (look, find) and the book (remember).
   - The saying verbs go to the party's introductions and Conversations.
   - Care verbs go to the adoption centre; home verbs to the clean-up.
7. **Music and art:** no game is worth building for the words alone. The art corner at school (draw, colour, a big circle) covers drawing; musical statues covers dancing. I'd leave music out.
8. **Days of the week** have no home. A school day could say "today is Monday", but it's weak for a child of 3-5. I'd leave the days for the evening book later (A2).
9. **Shapes:** only if Kutchi has the words; ask Mum before designing for them.
10. **Describing words have no game of their own.** That's fine, because they ride everywhere. "The X one" (the big goat, the dirty cup) should be a level in several games, not one game.
11. **Celebrations out: what about the existing Eid lines?** Cook's day-6 finale says "Eid Mubarak" (LNG-15) and First launch's hook still mentions Eid. Should they become the birthday too? (Non-negotiable 1: not changed without his word.)
