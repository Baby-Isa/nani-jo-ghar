# The evening book: review games (research, 19 Oct)

## Conclusion

Make the book a dialogic reading:

- **Nani leads:** she asks, the child taps or drags, and she says the whole sentence back with the word in it. That feedback is what makes retrieval work for preschoolers [7].
- **Every question is cued by a picture from the child's own day.** Preschoolers learn from cued recall, not free recall [7], and personal, same-as-play pictures help words stick [11, 12].
- **Receptive first,** then choosing the word's sound, then saying it at L4 [3, 4].
- **4-6 prompts in total.** The evening already gives spacing [6, 9], and sleep after a story strengthens new words [10].
- **Only the task can be tapped:** hotspots distract [13].

**Four leaks in the current plan, and the fixes:**

- **G1 picture pills** let shapes be matched when the page shows the answer. Use them only where it doesn't.
- **Written Kutchi on pills** can be matched to the sound by anyone who reads English. Use pictures or speaker icons.
- **Elimination** wins if she asks for every item, or if a distractor is one the child has never met [17]. Ask for fewer items than are shown, and take distractors from today's words.
- **An idle glow on the answer** lets waiting win. Idle help only says the line again; the bulb removes one wrong choice.

**First build: games 1-4.**

## Evidence notes

- **Dialogic reading:**
  - PEER is prompt, evaluate, expand, repeat; CROWD is completion, recall, open-ended, wh- and distancing prompts [1, 2].
  - Expressive vocabulary gains d = .59, falling by age 5-6 [3].
  - Dialogic style and more hearings raise word learning [4].
  - Second-language learners gained when the reading added their stronger language [5].
- **Retrieval:**
  - Spaced retrieval doubled preschoolers' recall [6].
  - It needs cued recall and feedback [7] and holds for new words [8].
  - Spaced beats massed at age 3 [9].
  - Sleep after a story strengthens new words [10].
- **Books:** a repeated story beats varied ones [11]; personal pages help [12]; voice helps and hotspots hurt [13]; apps should be active, engaged, meaningful and social [14].
- **Apps (reviews, not trials) [21]:**
  - Duolingo ABC reviews earlier material on a fixed path.
  - Gus on the Go has the child tap the animal named, with native voices.
  - Endless Alphabet animates meanings but has no check.
  - Khan Academy Kids and Lingokids adapt and mix in memory games; Little Pim is video only.
  - Worth taking: native voices, "tap the one named", short rounds.
- **Grandparents:** they pass language on through shared activities and stories [19], and grandmothers deliberately keep the heritage language alive [20].
- **Gaps:** no study found on sequencing or "what's missing" for vocabulary at 3-5, and none on a heritage language in an app.
