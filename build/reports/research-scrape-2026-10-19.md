# The scrape: research and three options (CLN-172, clinic decision 1)

Read-only research, 19 Oct. Sources: the code (`js/clinic/heal/games/cut.js`, `data/clinic/heal/cut.json`), `docs/game-design/modes/clinic.md` § B, the 18 Oct play-test (P20, P42), `data/lang/lexicon.json`, `data/family-audio.json`, and the web (list at the end).

## Conclusion and recommendation

**Replace the scrape with Option C, "the sore arm": line up the bone on a friendly X-ray, wrap a cast in the colour said, then decorate it with stickers the doctor calls out by count, colour and name.** It is the one moment every hospital game in the genre builds towards (Toca, Dr Panda, Pepi, BabyBus all have the cast; Dr Panda ends every cure with stickers), it uses the swipe and match mechanics Zafar asked for, it reuses the largest pool of Kutchi Mum has already recorded (Cook's nouns, *laal*/*lilo*, the numbers, *wadho*/*nindho*), and the stickers give the end-of-game "show it off" that the scrape lacks. Build M; art S (the plain forearm exists, the cast can be a tileable band like the knee's bandage, the stickers are Cook's icons at first). Needs Zafar's explicit yes to replace (rule 2). Fallback if he says keep the injury: Option A, whose wash step is owed to the foot anyway (P42).

Why the scrape is dull, in genre terms: every step is "tap the dish, tap the spot, repeat". Nothing changes under the finger, the patient barely reacts, and nothing is kept at the end. The games children return to do three things the scrape does not: something visibly moves or transforms under the finger (bones snap together, bubbles drag out, dirt wipes away), the patient reacts and thanks them, and a souvenir stays (stickers, a decorated cast).

Zafar's surgery ideas (open heart, valves, knee surgery): no. Blood and organs break "nothing scary or gory", Kutchi has no words for them, and the genre's child-safe version of "broken bones you put back" is exactly the cartoon X-ray line-up in Option C.

## What the genre does (web research)

- **Toca Doctor** (3+): 21 one-touch puzzles, each a different finger move: pull splinters, band-aid a sore, straighten a leg bone and plaster it, drag bubbles out of a gut, pop bugs, place ear parts by shape. No timers. Reviewers: fun, cute, "somewhat repetitive", and Common Sense Education says little is learned without an adult. Lesson: one new finger move per injury, short.
- **Dr Panda Hospital** (2-6): swipe, tap, sort, move. Reset bones by swiping on an X-ray; a magnet down the throat; ointment; injections. Every cure earns a sticker; the animals are "so appreciative". Lesson: the patient's gratitude and a kept reward carry the fun.
- **Toca Life: Hospital, My Town Hospital, Pepi Hospital, Miga**: open dollhouses. Casts, bandages, crutches, X-ray and MRI rooms are the props children pick most; the youngest "mostly tap and drag things around". Lesson: props that do something when touched.
- **Doctor Kids (Bubadu)**: each illness gets its own custom mini-game; "tons of colourful plasters, bandages, braces". Lesson: variety and choice of colour.
- **Operation** (6+): a steady hand, a buzzer, silly ailment names. Lesson: a gentle precision test with a comic fail (the foot's channels already do this; the leg kick in the knee is the same idea).

On Zafar's three mechanics: **swipe** and **match** fit the leak test easily (the Kutchi says what to swipe, which to match). **Spot the difference** passes only when the difference is *said* in Kutchi ("the one with *trae amba*"); as a pure picture puzzle anyone wins. Used that way once, in C's level 4.

## The Kutchi available today

Recorded or confirmed: *paani*; *dho* (wash, draft), *dabai* (press), *rakh* (put), *kadh* (take out), *wij* (put in), *firai* (turn), *khobar* (wait), *nar* (look), *kapi chad*; *laal*, *lilo* (green); *hakro… panj*; *wadho/nindho*; *garam/thundo*; *dabo/jamni* (left/right); *andar/niche/mathe*; *aste thi*, *jaldi*, *thorok*, *wadhare*, *bas*, *barabar*; *pela / ne poi*; body: *kan*, *akh*, *gutan*, *hath* (hand), *pag* (foot); Cook's nouns (*ambo*, *limu*, *tameto*, *bakri*, *chakli*, *kutro*, *cup*, *murgi*, *dungri*…); *shabash*, *dhyan rakh*.
To record (already on the gap list): blue, yellow, white, pink; arm, elbow, finger, nose, head; cloth, plaster, bandage, tissue, ice pack; wipe, clean it, put on, up, down, times.

## Option A: the richer scrape (same injury)

**Steps.** 1 *Wash*: dirt patches of two sizes on the graze; drag the jug over each (the water clears only what it passes over, as P42 asks for the foot). The doctor names the water (*garam / thundo paani*) and the order (*pela wadho, ne poi nindho*). 2 *Dry*: the cloth; press and hold on the graze (*dabai*) until the shine goes, N presses said. 3 *Plaster*: a tray of four (big/small x red/green); pick the one said (*wadho laal*), drag it on, swipe to turn it along the graze (*firai*).
**Fun mechanic.** Water that only cleans where the finger goes; a plaster you turn to fit. Modest.
**Kutchi.** *paani, garam, thundo, dho, dabai, firai, wadho, nindho, laal, lilo*, numbers, *pela/ne poi*. Gaps: cloth, plaster, "dry it", blue, yellow.
**Leak test.** Water temperature, patch order, count, plaster size and colour are heard only; a non-speaker guesses 1 in 2 to 1 in 4 per step.
**Levels.** L1 wash any order, press N, one plaster by colour. L2 adds the order by size. L3 heard only. L4 two plasters, turned.
**Art.** None new (dirt, jugs, cloth, plaster sheets exist); a two-size plaster cut if the sheet lacks one.
**Build.** S-M. Honest floor; it stays three verbs on one wound.

## Option B: the bee sting on the hand (a swap, not the knee)

**Steps.** 1 *Find it*: the hand, palm up, a red bump on one finger; the doctor says which (*wadhi / nindhi* finger; from L2 *dabo / jamno hath* first, the side). 2 *Out*: tweezers (the ear's) drag the sting out along a short channel; a shake and "ow" if the edge is touched (the foot's steady row). 3 *Cool*: two packs, *thundo* and *garam*; press and hold the cold one until the bump shrinks (*dabai*; "*bas!*" when done). 4 *Cream*: rub N circles (*firai*, count). 5 A plaster in the colour said.
**Fun mechanic.** The swelling that visibly goes down under a held finger; the steady pull.
**Kutchi.** *hath, dabo/jamni, wadho/nindho, thundo/garam, dabai, firai, bas*, numbers, colours. Gaps: finger, bee, sting, cream, ice pack, rub.
**Leak test.** Which finger, which side, which pack, how many rubs, which colour: all heard.
**Levels.** L1 one bump, cold, plaster. L2 adds the side and the count. L3 heard only. L4 two bumps in order.
**Art.** M: a palm-up hand close-up with a bump (none exists), a bee (friendly, off-screen after), two packs, a cream tube. Tweezers exist.
**Build.** M. Risks: overlaps the foot (pull-out) and the ear (tweezers); a bee may frighten some four-year-olds.

## Option C: the sore arm, the cast and the stickers (recommended)

**Why.** "I fell off the swing, my arm hurts." / "Let's look inside, and put a cast on." Said as a *sore* arm, not "broken"; no blood, no bone shown outside the X-ray card.
**Steps.** 1 *X-ray*: a friendly blue card over the forearm with a cartoon bone in two pieces; swipe the loose piece until it clicks into line (Dr Panda's swipe; a skill row, no words). 2 *Cast*: rolls in four colours; pick the one said (*laal / lilo /* [blue] / [yellow]) and swipe along the arm from the hand up (*hath* → [elbow]); each swipe lays one band; N bands said (*char*). 3 *Stickers*: the doctor calls them out from Cook's pool and the child drags them onto the cast: "*Pela ba amba. Ne poi hakri bakri.*" (first two mangoes, then one goat). Wrong ones just sit there and show in the review (rule 12). 4 The girl holds the cast up and beams; her face circle keeps it in the send-off.
**Fun mechanic.** The click of the bone lining up, the cast growing band by band, and a decorated cast she shows off: match-and-decorate, the genre's favourite.
**Kutchi.** *hath*, colours (*laal, lilo*), numbers 1-5, *pela / ne poi*, *wadho / nindho* (big or small sticker from L2), and Cook's nouns with their plurals (*ambo/amba, bakri, chakli, kutro, limu, tameto, cup*): the richest reuse of recorded words in any heal game, and the plurals get a second home. Gaps for Mum: arm, elbow, cast, X-ray, sticker, blue, yellow.
**Leak test.** The colour, the band count and every sticker (which, how many, in what order) are heard only; the sheet holds eight stickers, so guessing fails. The X-ray step teaches nothing and tests nothing (a skill row, like the foot's steady hand).
**Levels** (Zafar's ladder, 13 Oct). L1: X-ray click, one colour, two stickers called one at a time. L2 adds the order and count ("*pela ba amba, ne poi hakri bakri*") on one card. L3 heard only. L4: *wadho / nindho* stickers, and the spot-the-difference end: two finished casts, the doctor describes hers ("*hakro laal, ba lila amba*"), tap it.
**Art.** S-M: the plain forearm exists (`closeups/child/forearm.webp`); a tileable cast band laid in code along the arm (the same method as the knee's crepe band, CLN-179); one X-ray card with the bone in two pieces (friendly, blue-white); a cast roll in four colours; stickers from Cook's icons until an art run gives a sticker sheet.
**Build.** M (a new game file on the heal API; the sticker step is the pharmacy's pick-by-word logic over a drag target).
