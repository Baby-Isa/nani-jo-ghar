# Other games: what children's apps teach us about our 16 new games

Research note for Zafar, 31 Oct 2026. It covers the "new" rows of Table A in `game-settings-map-2026-10-31.md`. It builds on `research-mechanics-2026-10-31.md` (the mechanics and the language apps) and doesn't repeat it. We take ideas, not copies: nothing here proposes rebuilding any app.

**How to read it.** Each source in Proof is marked **read** (I opened the page) or **summary** (search-engine summary only). Most of the evidence is reviews (Common Sense Media, library and parent sites), not studies. Many of the apps are 2012–2018 classics, so prices and details may have changed. "Leak" means a way to win without the heard word (non-negotiable 4). Engine names are ours: pack the bag, put it in its place, count, Snap, Find it, Cook's mechanics, the clinic's heal games, the window ride, conversations.

## Seven things almost every review says

1. **Children love things that react:** a guest sneezes at spicy food, a pet falls asleep happy, the person waiting for a parcel follows it with their eyes. That's the "toy" in the best apps (Toca Kitchen 2, Toca Pet Doctor, Toca House; read).
2. **A reaction that looks like disapproval feels like a verdict.** Common Sense flags Toca Tailor's characters looking disapproving at an outfit, and Toca House's characters grumbling (read). That breaks non-negotiable 7.
3. **Open sandboxes delight at first and then bore** children who want the app to "do something" (Sago Mini Farm, Toca Birthday Party; read). Reviewers say the learning comes only when an adult talks alongside (My PlayHome, Toca Tea Party; read). **Nani is that adult.** This is our edge: a dollhouse plus a voice that asks for things.
4. **Unclear instructions are the top complaint:** "not always clear what to tap next" (Toca Doctor, Dr. Panda's Hospital, Toca Pet Doctor; read). Our guided first round answers it.
5. **Small things and multi-step drags frustrate little fingers** (Toca Kitchen 2, Toca Life: Pets, Sago Mini Farm; read). Prefer tap-then-tap, with big targets.
6. **The hints that guide play are leaks for us:** shadow outlines showing where each plate goes (Toca Tea Party), a visible injury, a visibly dirty pet. Each scene needs a want or a fault that the picture leaves open, so the word settles it.
7. **Parents complain most about ads, paywalls and privacy,** not play (nearly every review). Our "nothing to buy" is worth saying in the store listing.

## 1. Someone else's birthday party

**Closest examples**
- **Toca Birthday Party** (read): choose a cake, seat the guests, serve, pour juice, pop confetti, open a present, then wash up in soapy water. Simple drag-and-drop toddler fun.
- **Toca Tea Party** (read): set the table, "get your guests", serve, eat, refill; guests spill and the child mops up (reviewers single out the spill and clean-up).
- **Toca Kitchen 2** (read): guests react to what they're fed (sneezing, steam from hot food).
- **Sago Mini Friends** (summary): visiting friends' houses; fun, though reviewers find little learning in it.

**Pitfalls**
- "Very little the app actually does" (Toca Birthday Party): once the cake is eaten, it's over.
- No prompts about manners, so the social side only happens with a parent.
- Tea Party's outlines show where every item goes, so the child just fills the gaps.

**How the heard word decides**
- **Gifts and cake to the right person:** the person (Grandad, the cousin) and the owner word ("Ali's present") decide. Parcels look alike apart from colour and size, so "the big red one, to Grandad" needs both words.
- **Serving by feeling:** the guest *says* "I'm thirsty" before the face shows anything. Water, a chair, a shawl and a fan are all on the tray, so the feeling word picks.
- **Snap the person named:** two or three guests of the same kind (two old men: Grandad and the neighbour), so the relationship word decides.
- **Musical statues:** the music stops and Nani calls a pose (sit, jump, turn). The child picks it from three, and every guest freezes in that pose.

**Where it leaks**
- A visible need (the sad child holding a string with no balloon) means the picture picks. Show it only after the child has tried, or only at level 1.
- A gift that suits its owner (the football for the boy) is matching. Use neutral boxes.
- Statues' poses in a fixed order is a pattern. Shuffle them.
- Introductions leak if the new person is the only stranger in the room.

**Delight to borrow:** blowing out the candles, by tapping or by blowing at the microphone. The number of candles is the age heard ("he's five today"), so the count engine rides on it. Then the present unwrap: a reveal with a squeal.

**Size:** mostly reskins: put it in its place (gift or cake to a person), count (candles), Snap (people), conversations (introductions). **Musical statues is a small new engine** (a pose picker). It also has an off-screen twin: the phone plays, stops, and Nani says "sit!" to the real child. That is unscored, and it's the strongest verb evidence we have (whole-body action; see the mechanics report). **The music is a question for Zafar** (a clapping or drum beat, or a hum, would do).

## 2. The town map

**Closest examples**
- **Toca Town** (read): six places (home, flat, restaurant, shop, police station, park); characters carry what they buy back home to the fridge; "more toy than app"; lights, candles and waking people are favourite touches.
- **Dr. Panda Town** (summary): 40+ places reached from a map icon; some behind a subscription.
- **Busytown app** (summary, Macworld): lovely pictures, but navigation was clumsy and the reviewer expected it to frustrate young children.
- **Toca Life: City** (summary): a digital dollhouse town, but reviewers say it lacks structure.

**Pitfalls**
- Too many places, or a fiddly route between them.
- Places a child can't tell apart without signs. Our mock-up prompt already tests that.
- Open towns with nothing asked of the child.

**How the heard word decides:** two words per move. **Who** ("Nana wants to go ...") picks the badge, and **where** picks the building. At the hard level, the want ("I want a book") stands in for the place.

**Where it leaks**
- **Elimination:** as people leave, fewer badges and places remain, so the last move is free. People should come back, or the round should end with several still waiting.
- **Pattern:** Nana always goes to the bakery. Rotate who wants what.
- **The inference level can be easier, not harder:** if the library's sign is a giant book, "I want a book" is plain picture matching. It's still language, but no harder than "the library". For real inference, use wants that aren't on the sign: "I feel poorly" for the clinic, "I want to play" for the park, "I have a letter" for the post office. Or keep the sign's picture different from the want.

**Delight to borrow:** the little figure walks the road, goes in, and comes back out carrying the thing (a book, a loaf). Even better, it turns up later in the evening book or at Nani's (Toca Town's carry-it-home idea).

**Size:** a reskin of put it in its place (person to place) with a walk animation. The new parts are the map art and the person row.

## 3. Hide and seek: find the cats

**Closest examples**
- **Monki Hide and Seek** (read, iGameMom): animals show, then walk off and hide; "Where is the kitten?"; sounds grow louder as you get near; hints like "behind the house"; hiding spots change after each find. The reviewer says it holds young children "for a long time".
- **Hide and Seek, farm vocabulary app** (read, iGameMom): count to ten with eyes closed, then "Can you find the cow?" and move things to look.
- **Fiete Hide and Seek** (summary): tap rocks and plants; a dog helper sniffs out the sailor.
- **Animal Hide and Seek, MyFirstApp** (summary): tap to reveal; hiding places change each level.

**Pitfalls**
- Fully hidden targets frustrate the youngest; few scenes means little replay.
- **The research:** in a touchscreen search study of children aged 4–25, 4-year-olds were "slow, inefficient and inaccurate". They found the target 84% of the time among 4 things and 66% among 32, and often gave up early when it wasn't there (read). So keep 4–6 places to look at level 1, and make every empty spot quick and fun.

**How the heard word decides:** someone says where they heard a meow ("I think he's in the cupboard"). The child opens that place. The cupboard, under the bed, behind the curtain and the box are all openable, so the place word picks.

**Where it leaks**
- **A tail sticking out** is visual search, not language. Either show nothing, or add decoy "tails" (a scarf end, a toy).
- **Opening everything** is elimination. Answers: the cats move after each find (as Monki does); only a first-try find earns the badge (the bulb rule, not a verdict); and each empty spot holds a small surprise (Ali's lost sock), so a miss is still fun.
- The "it glows" support is fine at level 1 only.

**Delight to borrow:** the count to ten with the eyes closed, then the reveal: the cat jumps out, or blinks sleepily, or isn't there and something silly is.

**Size:** a reskin of Find it's openables. Small. It is also the natural home for "Ask to find out" (the child asks where) once speaking is ready.

## 4. Pick it up and put it back (the doll's house)

**Closest examples**
- **My PlayHome** (read): a dollhouse with no set story; dolls eat, bathe, fry eggs and blow bubbles; easy controls. Learning comes "mainly when a parent adds conversation and vocabulary".
- **Pepi House** (summary): a family dollhouse over several floors; a parent quotes a 4-year-old who loves exploring the floors.
- **Toca Life: City and World** (summary): the digital dollhouse taken to its limit; open-ended, and some children find it aimless.
- **Toca Town** (read): carrying things between places is the core joy.

**Pitfalls**
- Small items are hard to pick up and place (Toca Life: Pets; read).
- No goal, and messes that stay until someone resets.
- **The research** (summary): in and on come before under, and context steers the answer (a cup goes "in"). Toddlers who learned "behind" and "next to" from pictures didn't carry the words over to new things.

**How the heard word decides**
- **Pick up:** "my bag is on the table in the kitchen", with two or three bags in different rooms and positions, so the room word and the position word decide.
- **Put back:** every spot is outlined (Zafar's rule), and the position word picks one.

**Where it leaks**
- **Natural places:** a book belongs on the shelf, a cup on the table. Use things that sit happily in several spots (a ball, a bag, a teddy: on, under or next to the chair).
- **Elimination:** if one bag is gone, the next is obvious. Put things back in a fresh room each round.
- **"This side, that side":** the viewpoint must be plain. Nani stands at one side of the room, and "this side" is hers.

**Delight to borrow:** the doll carries the thing up the stairs, the room's light comes on as it enters, and the bag sits in the doll's hand.

**Size:** a reskin of put it in its place (level 3 already has positions and owners) plus pack the bag's fetch. The new parts are the doll's-house cross-section and moving between rooms. Small to medium.

## 5. Clean-up day

**Closest examples**
- **Toca House** (read): 19 chores (mopping, ironing, dishes, mail, mowing, sorting). A trumpet and a rainbow when a task ends; the person waiting for a parcel follows it with their eyes.
- **Pepi Bath** (summary): washing clothes and using the sink; popping soap bubbles afterwards.
- **Toca Tea Party** (read): the spill and the mop-up are a favourite part.
- **Toca Town** (read): switching lights on and off is a favourite toy.

**Pitfalls**
- Common Sense says Toca House is "not quite as engaging as Toca's typical toys". Repeated sweeping and sorting gets tiresome; there's no story; difficulty doesn't build; and characters grumble when things go wrong (read).

**How the heard word decides:** **one room, many switchable things:** the tap, the light, the curtains, the drawer, the fan. Each has two states, and they start mixed (some on, some off). "Turn off the tap" then needs both the verb and the noun.

**Where it leaks**
- **One obvious job per thing** (dirt, so the broom) means the scene picks the move.
- **"Switch off the light" when it's the only thing on** is free.
- **"Clean everything"** is won by waiting and wiping it all.
- **The order card's picture** (Zafar's note) is fine as support, but from level 2 it must go, or the card picks.

**Delight to borrow:** the light goes off and the room goes dim, and a cat's eyes glow under the bed (a nod to hide and seek). The dripping tap stops with a plink, and the dust sparkles away.

**Size:** **a new small engine**, "do it to the thing": objects with states (on/off, open/closed, dirty/clean) and a tap or swipe to change them. It's the house version of "Nani says, do it". It borrows Cook's gesture mechanics (stir and pour show the swipe pattern).

## 6. The wake-up clock

**Closest examples**
- **Kids Clock Learning** (summary): move the hands and hear the time. The reviewer warns of a short shelf life: children move on once they can tell the time.
- **Kids Tell Time** (summary): six modes, gentle guidance when wrong.
- **Toca Town** (read): waking the characters up is one of the touches reviewers mention.
- **Research** (read: Cambridge Maths; summary: Durham thesis, Friedman and Laycock): digital time is read well before analogue, which takes years and is mostly in place by about 10. Time words, routines and the order of events matter as much as reading a clock, and the language of time can itself be a barrier.

**Pitfalls**
- Clock apps are drills with little fun evidence.
- **A fast clock is a timer,** and speed fights listening (mechanics report, finding 4).
- Waking someone "late" is a verdict.

**How the heard word decides:** from level 2, each sleeper says their time ("wake me at seven") and the bedside clock is blurred. The child has to know the number word to spot the wall clock reaching seven.

**Where it leaks**
- **Tapping every bed at every hour** is trial and error. Fix: a wrong wake gets a sleepy "not yet" and a roll-over (funny, no penalty), and only a first-tap wake earns the badge.
- **Waking in bed order** is a pattern. Shuffle who wakes when.
- **Missing the moment:** the clock should slow at each whole hour and wait there. The child must still choose *who*, so waiting alone wins nothing.

**Delight to borrow:** the alarm rattles, the sleeper sits bolt upright with wild hair, yawns and stretches; the curtains open and the sun comes up.

**Size:** a new small engine (a running clock plus tap a person). The digits come from the count work. Consider a level 1 that uses the parts of the day ("when the sun comes up") before numbers.

## 7. Hannah Mami's adoption centre

**Closest examples**
- **Toca Pet Doctor** (read): 15 animals; look for clues, treat with bandages and creams, untie a knotted worm, then feed. The animal falls asleep happy and well. There are no rules or points.
- **Sago Mini Pet Cafe** (read): count snacks onto a tray, feed by shape, mix colour smoothies; silly animations when a pet finishes. Built "without the pressure of time ticking by or trying to attain a high score".
- **Toca Life: Pets** (read): dunk a pet in mud, then wash it; most animals make a sound when picked up.
- **Nintendogs study** (read): 51 children aged 9–11 played for three weeks. Their empathy scores rose, and many treated the pets as real.

**Pitfalls**
- **Neglect and guilt:** virtual pets that sicken or die when ignored upset children (anecdotes; summary). Never let an animal suffer while the child is away.
- Potty humour (Toca Life: Pets).
- Small items are fiddly.

**How the heard word decides**
- **Several animals, several needs:** "the bee is hungry", "the ant is dirty", "the hamster is sleepy". The animal word and the need word together pick the animal and the care.
- **One animal, several cares** (feed, wash, brush, stroke): the verb decides.

**Where it leaks:** a visible need (a muddy ant) means the picture picks. Every animal shows the same "wants something" look until the child acts, or the mess appears only on the part named.

**Delight to borrow:** the tiny animal falls asleep content (Toca Pet Doctor's ending). The bee does a happy waggle dance, and the ant staggers off carrying a crumb bigger than itself.

**Size:** a reskin of the clinic's heal-game pattern (care actions on a body) plus put it in its place (food to the animal). New art; small to medium.

## 8. The orchard and vegetable patch

**Closest examples**
- **Sago Mini Farm** (read): tap a rain cloud and crops grow, including silly ones (cameras, diamonds) that are "good for a giggle". The farm is small, and children who want it to "do something" get bored.
- **My PlayHome** (read) and **Toca House** (summary): gardening and planting flowers are favourite small jobs.
- **The give-N counting research** (mechanics report): "give me three" is the standard test of a number word.

**Pitfalls**
- Pure counting drills.
- Tiny fruit that's hard to tap.
- Nothing to choose between.

**How the heard word decides:** "pick three mangoes", with more mangoes on the tree than asked, other fruit beside them, and **ripe and unripe** (a natural describing pair). "Three of the yellow ones" then needs the number and the colour.

**Where it leaks:** exactly three mangoes on the tree means pick them all. One kind of fruit per tree means the tree picks.

**Delight to borrow:** shake the tree and the fruit wobbles; each picked fruit bounces into the basket. Planting a seed and watering it, then watching it grow in a few seconds (Sago's rain cloud).

**Size:** a reskin of count, with Cook's pour for watering. Small.

## 9. The safari (Snap from the jeep)

**Closest examples**
- **Pokémon Snap** (read and summary): an on-rails photo ride where the joy is "catching moments", with a graded photo album. A premise nobody expected to be fun, but it was. It's criticised for little replay.
- **Ranger Rick Jr. Lions, Photo Safari** (summary): turn the tablet to look round a 360-degree scene.
- **Wild Earth: African Safari** (summary): photo missions; a children's technology award.
- **Ranger Rick's bird photography game** (summary): fast-flying birds, aimed at 7+.

**Pitfalls**
- Fast targets frustrate under-6s.
- Little replay once the album is full.

**How the heard word decides:** two elephants, one drinking and one sleeping: "the elephant that's drinking". Verbs are seen in motion, which suits over-3s (Roseberry, in the mechanics report). Counts and sizes too: "two giraffes", "the baby one".

**Where it leaks:** one of each animal means plain picture matching (fine at level 1). An animal that leaves before it's asked means speed wins, so nothing leaves before it's been asked (the window-ride rule).

**Delight to borrow:** the animal reacts to the shutter: the giraffe stares into the lens, the elephant sprays the jeep, the monkey pulls a face. Then the photos are developed and pasted into the album (Zafar's earlier Snap idea).

**Size:** a reskin of Snap plus the window ride. Small.

## 10. The boat (litter fishing)

**Closest examples**
- **Marine Missions** (summary): tap rubbish into a recycling bin, swipe away an oil spill. A teacher found it simple, without much detail or challenge.
- **Juno Lamb Saves the Ocean** (summary): toddler ocean clean-up at three levels; most items respond with a sound or an animation.
- **Toddler fishing apps and magnetic fishing toys** (summary): toddlers love collecting the fish; parents report the magnets are hard for small hands.

**Pitfalls**
- A thin genre with little review evidence.
- Fiddly rod control.
- A preachy message.
- "Catch everything" needs no language.

**How the heard word decides:** Nani names which bit of rubbish: "the shoe", "the red bottle", "two cans". There's lots of rubbish of mixed kinds in the water. The household nouns learned at home turn up in an odd place.

**Where it leaks:** "clear it all" is won by waiting. One of each item is fine at level 1 only. Fish versus rubbish is a picture choice, not language. A fish on the hook just swims off with a splash (no verdict).

**Delight to borrow:** hauling up something absurd: a boot with a crab inside, an old kettle. As the water clears, more fish come back to look.

**Size:** a reskin of pack the bag's fetch, with one new small control (drop the hook where you tap). Small. **It's thin as a game of its own;** it may be stronger as one stage of the boat day.

## 11. The beach's three zoom-in stalls

**Closest examples**
- **Dr. Panda Restaurant** (summary): take guests' orders, then cook in the kitchen; a parent reports long engagement.
- **Toca Kitchen 2** (read): guests react to food, and the food browns as it cooks. The multi-step fridge-to-pan-to-guest sequence frustrates the youngest.
- **Toca Life: Vacation** (summary): beach, sun and ice cream; free play.

**Pitfalls**
- Too many steps per dish.
- Every dish feels the same.

**How the heard word decides:** the customer's request picks **which stall** to zoom into (corn, cassava chips or coconut). The number or kind then picks within it ("two corn, with lemon").

**Where it leaks:** if each stall has one dish and one customer, the zoom picks the dish. Two or three customers wait at once, and the one who speaks is the order.

**Delight to borrow:** the corn kernels pop and blacken on the grill, the coconut top comes off with a thwack and the straw slurps, and the customer's happy face.

**Size:** Cook reskins (grill, fry, chop, count), as `days/beach.md` already says. Small.

## 12. A school day

**Closest examples**
- **Toca Life: School** (summary): five places (classroom, playground, cafeteria and more). Common Sense found the dialogue "weirdly silly" in ways young children may not follow, and failed it on privacy.
- **Sago Mini School** (summary): 300+ light-learning activities for ages 2–5. Common Sense warns there's no natural stopping point.
- **Khan Academy Kids** (summary): structured early learning; children love collecting the prizes.
- **Busy Shapes** (summary): calm shape-sorting puzzles that teachers like.

**Pitfalls**
- School games drift into drills or into aimless sandboxes.
- No stopping point.

**How the heard word decides**
- **The morning routine:** Ali does what Nani says (wash your face, put on your shoes, brush your teeth). There is one Ali with several actions, so the verb decides.
- **Packing the school bag:** pack the bag, with toys as decoys.
- **The blackboard:** several shapes, sizes and colours are already drawn, and the teacher says "colour the big circle" or "count the ducks".

**Where it leaks**
- **Putting the routine in order** is won by knowing the routine (a pattern), not the words. Use do-it, not ordering.
- **A board with one shape** is free.
- **Only school things in reach** means the bag packs itself.

**Delight to borrow:** the chalk circle comes alive (it becomes the sun and smiles). The teacher sticks a sticker on the child's work.

**Size:** pack the bag, plus do-it (shared with clean-up day), plus **a small new blackboard engine** (tap or trace on the board). Medium.

## 13. Big Ma's sewing

**Closest examples**
- **Toca Tailor** (read): choose the shape and length of each garment, pick fabric, add buttons, zips and pockets, then a fitting and a runway walk. Reviewers praise it as inclusive and easy, but the characters' disapproving faces may discourage children.
- **Toca Tailor Fairy Tales** (summary): the same for fairy-tale characters; preschool to age 7.
- **My PlayHome** (read): clothes and hats on dolls, and reviewers wish for more.

**Pitfalls**
- The customer "judging" the outfit.
- Too many features for small children.

**How the heard word decides**
- **Buying cloth:** "the green one, the long piece" (the X one, plus pay).
- **Making:** the customer asks for "long sleeves, red", and only the heard order counts (Cook's rule).
- **Mending:** the customer says *which* fault: "the pocket is torn". A tear, a missing button and a stain all show at once, so the word picks which to mend.
- **The fitting:** put on, take off.

**Where it leaks:** a single visible fault means the picture picks. A card showing the finished garment at level 2+ means matching.

**Delight to borrow:** the sewing machine whirring along a dotted line as the child drags (Cook's thread mechanic). Then the fitting: the person twirls in front of the mirror, always pleased. Modest cuts only.

**Size:** the rebuild of the parked Dress up mode, with Cook's thread and cut mechanics and the shops' pay. Medium.

## 14. A baking station (quantities)

**Closest examples**
- **Foodo Kitchen** (summary): preschool baking with no text and no spoken instructions, only visual cues.
- **Strawberry Shortcake Bake Shop** (summary): measure with spoons and drag into the bowl; marked down for ads and purchases.
- **Cake Doodle** (summary): sequencing and simple directions, ages 3–10.
- **Toca Kitchen 2** (read): food browns in the oven, which children enjoy.

**Pitfalls**
- Decorating swamps measuring.
- Ads.
- Sweets as rewards (non-negotiable 9): bake bread, naan or a birthday cake for the party, never treats as prizes.

**How the heard word decides:** "a little milk", "a lot of flour", "half a cup", "a bit more". The jug has no fill line, so the amount word decides when to stop.

**Where it leaks:** a fill line or a picture of the target amount means matching. "Pour until it stops" is waiting.

**Delight to borrow:** consequences, not verdicts: too much flour puffs a cloud, and the right dough rises in the oven window. Something baked here then appears at the party.

**Size:** Cook's pour, add, knead and fill-fold, plus a small "how full" state. A Cook station reskin; small.

## 15. A breakfast station

**Closest examples**
- **My PlayHome** (read): frying eggs on the stove is a favourite doll action.
- **Toca Kitchen 2** (read): fry, boil and juice, and the guests react.
- **Dr. Panda Restaurant** (summary): orders, then cooking.

**Pitfalls:** it's very close to Cook. The unique point (a meal tied to the time of day) is thin.

**How the heard word decides:** "a boiled egg for Nana, a fried one for Ali". The owner and the cooking verb both decide, and the eggs are all the same until cooked.

**Where it leaks:** the morning window picks *breakfast*. That's fine, as the sky sets only the kind of meal and the Kutchi picks the item (pack the bag's rule). A plate picture of the order at level 2+ means matching.

**Delight to borrow:** tap to crack the egg and the yolk slides out; toast pops up; the paratha puffs.

**Size:** a Cook station (fry and boil exist; crack is a tiny new mechanic). Small. **I'd fold it into Cook as a station** rather than treat it as its own game.

## 16. The clinic's extra treatments

**Closest examples**
- **Toca Doctor** (read): pull splinters (the patient says "ow!"), clean a knee and put a plaster on, set a bone, pop bugs. Children like the simple animations; reviewers find it repetitive and unclear about what to tap, and the blood may upset some.
- **Dr. Panda's Hospital** (read): tap a sad animal in the waiting room; shots, X-ray bone-setting, a magnet down the throat; a sticker per cure. Reviewers want more feedback.
- **Pepi Hospital** (summary): a multi-floor hospital; parents like it, but complain about paywalls and bugs.
- **Toca Pet Doctor** (read): the same pattern with animals; it ends with the patient asleep and well.

**Pitfalls**
- Repetition, gore, and not knowing the next step. Our clinic already answers these (comic not gory, one instruction at a time, a guided first round).

**How the heard word decides:** the patient says "my finger hurts", "my head", "my nose", and **no part shows a clue until the child taps it** (the diagnosis rule, extended). In the generic cut game, the patient has two or three small scrapes, and "clean the one on the elbow" picks one. "First clean it, then the plaster" carries the linking words.

**Where it leaks**
- A visible bandage-shaped wound means the picture picks the part.
- A tool that gives the action away (a tissue means blow) is matching. Put the tissue, the cream and the plaster on one tray.
- One scrape only is free.

**Delight to borrow:** the nose game's sneeze: a tickle, a build-up, then "atchoo!" blows things off the table. Every family laughs at that. Then the "ow ... careful ... phew" of the hand splinter.

**Size:** reskins of the heal games: the ear game becomes the nose, the knee and scrape games become the finger and the head, the foot's splinter becomes the hand. The generic cut is the knee game on any part. Small each.

## Cross-cutting lessons

1. **Every right move gets a reaction, and every miss gets a different funny one, never a frown.** The best apps are toys because things react. The worst moments in reviews are characters looking cross.
2. **Show an open need, and let the word close it.** Outlines, visible injuries and dirty pets guide play in other apps; in ours they leak. Every scene needs a want that several things could meet.
3. **Two words per move from level 2** (who and where, which and how many, the animal and the need). It's the simplest leak-proofing, and it's how real requests sound.
4. **Keep searches small for 4-year-olds:** 4–6 places at level 1, and fun empty spots, because young children give up early when the target isn't there.
5. **Tap then tap, not drag;** big targets; one step at a time. Multi-step drags are the most common frustration.
6. **Rotate what's hidden and who wants what,** so nothing is learned by pattern and replay stays fresh (Monki changes the hiding spots after every find).
7. **Carry things between games.** The book from the library, the cloth from the shop and the cake from the baking station turn up later (Toca Town's carry-it-home). That's cheap delight, and it gives the evening book something to show.
8. **No timers, and nothing leaves before it's been asked** (Sago's stated design; our ride rule). This matters most for the clock and the safari.
9. **Our voice is the adult the reviews keep asking for.** Sandboxes teach little without someone talking, and Nani does that talking.

## The five with the strongest outside evidence of fun (ages 3–8)

1. **Hide and seek:** a real-world favourite; several toddler apps; a reviewer says it holds young children "for a long time"; the reveal is built in.
2. **Doctor play (the clinic extras):** a whole genre (Toca Doctor, Dr. Panda's Hospital, Pepi Hospital, Toca Pet Doctor), all well liked by 3–6s, and our clinic already works.
3. **Pet care (the adoption centre):** Toca Pet Doctor, Sago Mini Pet Cafe, Toca Life: Pets, and children bond with virtual animals (Nintendogs study).
4. **The doll's house (pick it up and put it back):** My PlayHome, Pepi House and Toca Life are among the most loved preschool apps. We add the missing goal.
5. **The party (serving guests):** Toca Birthday Party, Tea Party and Kitchen 2 show that serving people who react is a lasting draw.

Close behind: the cooking stalls (Dr. Panda Restaurant, Toca Kitchen), which are Cook reskins anyway.

## Ours that look weak on the evidence

- **The wake-up clock:** clock apps are drills with a short shelf life; a fast clock is a timer; tapping every bed every hour is a leak. Keep it small, slow at the hour, and funny ("not yet", roll over). Zafar's wife's liking is worth a quick lab test before a full build.
- **The boat's litter fishing:** a thin genre, a simple teacher verdict, fiddly rods. Better as one stage of the boat day than a game of its own.
- **Clean-up day:** the nearest app (Toca House) is the least engaging of its family, because of repetitive sweeping and sorting. It works only if it's toy-like (lights, taps, curtains with states), not chores.
- **The breakfast station:** no unique point beyond Cook. Fold it into Cook.
- **Musical statues on screen:** no evidence for the screen version; the real fun is the child's own body. Keep it short, and add an unscored off-screen round. The music needs Zafar's call.
- **The school day's routine:** known routines leak by pattern, and school apps tend to be drills. The blackboard is the part worth designing.

## Flaws and limits

- **Mostly reviews, not studies.** The only studies here are on visual search, clock reading and a virtual-pet empathy trial (ages 9–11). Nothing tests language riding on these mechanics for under-8s.
- **Older apps.** Several Toca and Dr. Panda apps are from 2012–2016; some have since been retired or folded into larger apps.
- **Blocked pages:** The Horn Book (Fiete, 403), BridgingApps (403), and Common Sense Education's Marine Missions page (reviews withdrawn in 2026). Those points are summaries.
- **Not found:** no children's title called "Little Big City" turned up in a form worth citing. Lingokids, Endless and the language apps are in the mechanics report.
