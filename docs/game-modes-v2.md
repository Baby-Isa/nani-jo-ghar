# Nani jo Ghar: game modes v2 (syllabus first)

**Date:** 23 Sept 2026
**Status:** proposal. Supersedes the "Game modes" table in the Roadmap once Zafar confirms. Builds on `docs/game-modes-fun-analysis.md` (how hit games work; the checklist of what makes a mode fun).
**Approach (Zafar's call):** start from the **syllabus** (what language has to be taught), find a **proven, genuinely fun game** for each need, then fit the story around the games. It's easier to write a story around a good game than to find a good game for a story.

---

## 1. What the syllabus demands

| Stage | Language to teach | Kind of game that *forces* that language |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 Arrive and fetch** | Greetings, "I need X", "give me X", number + noun, yes/no; fruit, veg, spices, staples | Fulfilling requests; finding named things; counting out |
| **S2 Do as Nani says** | Imperatives, **position phrases**, this/that; rooms, household objects, utensils, clothes, **colours**, dishes | Following commands with verbs; placing things *where* told; picking by colour |
| **S3 Who's who** | Kinship, possessives, **who/what/where/how many**, "I like", **adjective agreement** | Serving the right person their preference; identifying people from descriptions; dressing to a description |
| **S4 How I feel** | Present and habitual, "it hurts", "I'm cold", weather; body, feelings, times of day, animals | Treating patients; reacting to the weather; routines by time of day; animals |
| **S5 What happened** | **Past tense**, yesterday/today, **first/then**; actions, materials, place words | Solving "who did it"; ordering events; following recipe sequences |
| **S6 Tell and plan** | **Future**, **comparatives**, "because", describing people, nature, travel | Packing with size and weight rules; spotting and describing; planning |
| **Running through everything** | Numbers (larger from Arc 2, with prices), respect language (formal "you", honorific kinship) | Money in every shop and upgrade; greeting each character correctly |

## 2. The eight games (each has a different core verb and a proven hit behind it)

| # | Game | Core verb | Proven hit it's built on | Syllabus | Story use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cook with Nani** | **Build** (wash, chop, stir, pour, roll, time the cook) + remember the sequence | *Good Pizza, Great Pizza*, Papa's Pizzeria, *Cooking Fever*, Simon | S1 nouns and numbers, S2 verbs, S3 likes and kinship, S5 first/then | Every arc: daal (Arc 1), wedding feast (2), chai in the rain (3), farm lunch (5) |
| 2 | **Find it** | **Search** a detailed scene | *June's Journey*, *Hidden City* | S1 nouns, S2 positions and colours, S4 animals, S5 past-tense clues | Bazaar list, the cat and the sweets, find the chicks, the lost ring |
| 3 | **Tidy up** | **Arrange** things to spoken rules | *A Little to the Left*, *Unpacking* | S2 positions, S3 colours and sizes, S6 comparatives | Dastarkhwan, shoe mountain, sweet box, mehndi, the family photo |
| 4 | **Dress up** | **Style** someone exactly as they ask | *Dress to Impress* (a Roblox hit), *Project Makeover* | S2 clothes and colours, S3 adjectives, S4 weather ("it's raining") | Eid morning, wedding outfits, a monsoon day |
| 5 | **Nani's clinic** | **Treat** patients (examine, choose a remedy, time management) | *Toca Doctor*, *My Hospital*, *Operate Now* | S4 body, feelings, "it hurts" | Monsoon: Nani has a cold; a village clinic |
| 6 | **Who did it?** | **Deduce** from clues | *Guess Who*, *Clue* | S3 describing people and kinship, S5 past tense | The lost ring, who ate the sweets, who's at the door |
| 7 | **Monsoon rush** | **React** quickly (catch, place, tap in time) | Catch and reaction arcade (*Fruit Ninja*-style) | S4 weather and rooms, S2 positions | The leak (buckets under the drips Nani calls out), animals into the shed |
| 8 | **Snap** | **Aim and capture** (pan a wide scene, take the photo of what's described) | *Pokémon Snap*, *Alba* | S6 describing, nature, travel | The journey to the village, the farm, old photos |

**Plus, across all of them:**
- **In-game upgrades** bought with coins (section 3).
- **Nani's word of the day** (a daily streak).
- The **quilt** for story progress.

**Greetings and respect language** aren't a separate game. **Every character interaction opens with a quick greeting choice** (in Cook, at the door, in the clinic): tap the right greeting or honorific. It's constant, low-cost practice, and it replaces the separate "At the door" mode.

**Why this isn't padding:**
- Each game exists because a syllabus stage *needs* its verb.
- Each verb is different: build, search, arrange, style, treat, deduce, react, capture.
- Each passes the test: you can't win without understanding the Kutchi.

**What's dropped from Roadmap v1:**
- **Shopping** as its own mode: buying becomes part of *Find it* (the bazaar list) and of Cook's ingredients.
- **At the door:** now greeting choices inside every game.
- **Ask around:** absorbed into *Who did it?*
- **Spot it:** split into *Snap* and *Monsoon rush*.

## 3. In-game upgrades (Zafar's idea: tycoon-style choices inside each game)

**Rules:**
- Coins go into one wallet, earned in any game.
- **Each game has its own shop** of upgrades that **change how it plays**.
- Space or slots are limited, so **every purchase is a choice**.
- **Upgrades only ever automate physical steps, never the listening.** You still have to understand the order.

**Cook with Nani, for example:**

| Upgrade | Effect | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Sharp knife | One swipe per cut instead of two | Cheap and early; everyone wants it |
| Chai machine | Pours and brews chai automatically | Takes a counter slot. Great if your customers order chai a lot |
| Second burner | Two pots cook at once | Expensive; pays off in the busy setting |
| Bigger pantry | More ingredients and recipes unlocked | Opens bigger orders (more coins, harder listening) |
| Tawa upgrade | Rotis cook faster | Only useful once you've unlocked roti |
| A cousin as helper | Auto-washes vegetables | Costs coins every day (a running wage), like a tycoon game |

A **counter with 4 slots** forces the classic decision: chai machine *or* second burner *or* spice rack. Different players build different kitchens.

**Other games:**
- **Find it:** a torch (for night scenes), a faster hint recharge, a bigger bag (longer lists, more coins).
- **Dress up:** new wardrobe sets.
- **Clinic:** better instruments that speed up examinations.

## 4. How the story fits

The arcs stay; each errand just uses the new games. Arc 1 as an example:

| Chapter | Errands (new game) |
|---|---|
| The guests are coming | Fruit from the bazaar (**Find it**), then fill the bowl · Daal for dinner (**Cook**) · Lay the dastarkhwan (**Tidy up**) |
| Knock knock | Greet each guest (greeting choice) and serve them (**Cook**: each guest likes it their way) · Shoe mountain (**Tidy up**) |
| The cat and the sweets | Find the sweets (**Find it**) · Who ate one? (**Who did it?**) · Repack the box (**Tidy up**, with counting) |
| The spill | New kurta: fabric by colour (**Find it**) · Dress the guest (**Dress up**) |
| Eid morning | Get dressed (**Dress up**) · Serve the Eid breakfast (**Cook**) |

**So Arc 1 needs:** Cook, Find it and Tidy up, with Dress up and Who did it? by the end of the arc.
- **Arc 3 (Monsoon)** adds Clinic and Monsoon rush.
- **Arc 5 (Village)** adds Snap.

**Build order follows the arcs.**

## 5. Feasibility with our tools (Phaser + Claude for code + ChatGPT for art + the family for voice)

| Game | Code difficulty | Art needed | Biggest risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cook with Nani | Medium: station mini-games, order generation, grading, upgrades | Ingredients in states (raw, chopped, cooked); utensils; stations | Ingredient states multiply the art. **Mitigation:** generic effects (split the sprite to show "chopped", tint plus steam to show "cooked") instead of drawing each state |
| Find it | Low | Dense, cluttered scenes (AI does these well) | Finding items fairly on small phone screens → zoom buttons, large tap areas |
| Tidy up | Low | Objects and surfaces | Designing puzzles with rules that are interesting, not just "put X there" |
| Dress up | Low | Clothing layers on one body per character | Clothing art volume (colours via tinting help a lot) |
| Clinic | Medium (time management like Cook, different verbs) | Patient poses, instruments | Keeping it gentle and funny, never scary |
| Who did it? | Medium: generated clues from character attributes | Existing characters | Recording enough clue sentences; generating them from templates keeps it manageable |
| Monsoon rush | Low–medium (arcade) | Few assets | Keeping it listening-led, not just reflexes |
| Snap | Medium (panning scene, moving subjects) | A wide panoramic scene and animals | Moving subjects need simple animation |

**Avoid entirely:** 3D, physics piles (triple-match), multiplayer, speech recognition, full-body character animation.

**The real bottlenecks aren't code:**
1. **Tuning for fun** (needs playtests).
2. **Family recording time.**
3. **Consistent art.**

**Laptops:** Phaser treats a mouse drag exactly like a finger swipe, so tap, drag and swipe all work on a laptop for free. **Design for phone and tablet first.** Avoid only pinch-zoom and two-finger gestures (use on-screen buttons instead). No extra work is planned for laptops.

## 7. Cook with Nani: stations first, recipes second (23 Sept 2026)

**Principle:** pick the stations that are proven fun and cheap to build, then choose recipes that use them. Every station is a **single-finger gesture** (it works with a mouse too) and ties to language (a count, a sequence or a named item). **Main reference:** *Cooking Mama*, where every cooking step is its own mini-game.

| Station | Gesture | Why it's fun | Language | Build |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pantry fetch** | Tap the named ingredient | The core listening moment | Nouns, numbers | Easy (exists) |
| **Chop** | Swipe across the item; it splits into pieces | *Fruit Ninja* satisfaction; counting | "Cut it into 4" (numbers) | Easy: split the sprite along the swipe |
| **Knead** | Press repeatedly, or drag back and forth | Squishy, tactile | "Knead" (verb) | Easy: squash-and-stretch tween |
| **Roll** (chapati) | Drag outwards from the centre until the circle fills the guide | Visible growth; aiming for "just right" | "Roll", "thin", "round" | Easy: scale the circle with drag distance |
| **Tawa flip / puff** | Watch the colour change; tap to flip at the right moment; the roti puffs up | Timing tension and a magical payoff | "Flip it", "it's ready" | Medium: a colour gradient plus a puff tween |
| **Tadka (spices in order)** | Drag spices into the hot oil **in the order Nani said**; it sizzles | Memory tension; sizzle sound; the Simon element | "First jeeru, then rai, then hing" (sequence) | Easy |
| **Stir / grind** | Circle drag the named number of times | Rhythmic, calming | Numbers; "stir" or "grind" | Easy |
| **Pour** | Hold to pour; release at the line | Precision tension | Measures: "half a cup", "to the line" | Easy |
| **Boil watch** | Tap before it boils over (the classic chai moment) | Real tension, funny failure (a harmless foam overflow) | "It's boiling!" | Easy |
| **Churn** (chaas, butter) | Drag back and forth quickly | Energetic; very Kutch | Verb, counting | Easy |
| **Plate / thali** | Place each part where told | Satisfying finish; positions | "Daal in the bowl, rotis on the left" | Easy (the Tidy-up mechanic) |

**Recipes these stations make:**
- **Chai:** pour water, boil watch, spoons of sugar (count), pour milk, pour into cups.
- **Chapati / rotli:** knead, roll, tawa flip and puff.
- **Daal:** wash, chop onion, tadka sequence, stir.
- **Kachumber / fruit chaat:** wash, chop (count), sprinkle, plate.
- **Chaas (buttermilk):** pour, churn, spice.

**Kutch specialities for later:**
- **Bajra rotlo:** pat between the palms (alternate taps), then clay tawa.
- **Khichdi and kadhi:** rinse, pour, tadka, stir.
- **Dabeli:** a famous Kutchi street food, perfect for a later bazaar food-stall chapter.

**Prototype set:** **chai, chapati and daal.** Together they cover pour, boil watch, count, knead, roll, flip, chop and the tadka sequence: the eight most distinct gestures. Drop the fruit plate from the prototype; it's only "chop".
