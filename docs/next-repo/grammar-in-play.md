# Grammar in play: how the patterns get into the games

For Zafar, 17 Nov 2026. The companion to `grammar-ladder.md`: that page says which patterns and in what order; this one says where a child meets them and what they do. It is what to design in now, not full designs. Kutchi is copied from `docs/language/grammar-notes.md` (section in brackets); everything else is English marked **Mum**.

## 1. The idea

Every game already starts and ends with people talking: someone arrives, greets you, asks for something, you make it, they thank you and go. That talk is the conversations module, and it is the natural home for most grammar: who is speaking, who it is for, yes or no, done or still to do. The cooking in the middle stays the fun. So the plan is one conversation shape (**greeting → order given → order checked → questions and options → thanks**) that every game uses, with a small number of grammar moves that plug into its steps. Because the shape runs through every game, a pattern climbs its ladder across the whole day, as a word does: heard at the door, deciding a cup in the kitchen, said at the clinic.

One correction to a thought in the voice note: **an order does not change with the gender of the person ordering.** "I want" follows the thing wanted (*khapeti* for chai, *khapeto* for water, §58). What does change with the person is "I will", "I can", "I eat", "gave" and "he / she does" (§32, §56, §61, §62). The he-form and she-form games below use those.

## 2. The ladder as levels

Each pattern has four rungs, kept per child, like a word's. A game's level (L1 to L4) caps the rung offered; it never forces one the child has not reached. Names follow the revised ladder (28 rows); these are the rows the conversation carries. The rest (positions, how much, counting) stay in the games' middles.

| Pattern | Heard (from L1) | Decides between two (L2) | Near miss, three choices (L3) | The child says it (L4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A child or an elder** (respect) | Family greet each other in front of the child | Same question from Nana and from Ali: the reply's ending fits who asked | A goodbye joins the two replies | The child opens the greeting; calls people over |
| **"To me" verbs** | Every order: *Muke chai khape* | Two people at the counter: the first word says whose cup | "Us" and "them" join | The child reads the order back as "you" |
| **No, don't, without** | "No sugar" rows, as today | Two orders alike but for the "no" | "No", "a little" and "with" | The polite no; reading back the "no" |
| **Questions**, and "or" | Nani asks; pictures answer | The question word picks the kind of answer | Who, what, how many together | The child takes the order |
| **Also, only** | "And one for Ma too" | "Too" or not: one cup or two | "Only", "too", neither | The child offers "you too?" |
| **Let's, let me, shall I** | Nani's "let me taste" | Who acts: the child or Nani | "Let's" joins: both | "Shall I help?" |
| **He, she, they, I** (verb endings) | Nani talks about Ali and the older cousin | The ending says which of the two | The elder (respect form) joins | "Say it as you": the child's own form |
| **He-words and she-words** | "Big", "small" with every dish | "The big" with no noun: the ending picks the thing | Number and gender mixed | The child asks for "the big" |
| **Feelings; can, need** | "How are you?" "I'm fine" | Who needs it; who can't | Want, need, have to | The child says how they are |
| **What happened** | "You made it!" at every serve | Come or still to come; who gave | Three people, three endings | The evening book: the child tells the day |
| **What will happen** | "We'll make chai" at every start | Come or still to come | With the past, above | "I'll help" in the child's own form |

Past and future are A2 in the ladder: their heard rung starts on day one and their deciding rungs wait.

## 3. The mechanics

For all of them: one graded grammar move a round at most; the choices share one picture and one voice, so only the pattern differs; it is modelled first where possible (Zafar, 14 Oct); a wrong pick gets the warm recast and another go, and anything placed can be taken back.

| # | Name | Pattern | Where it lives | How the grammar decides | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Who's asking? Who's called?** | A child or an elder | The greeting before the order; calling the next person | Nana and Ali ask the same question; two replies, same picture, differ only in the ending. Or Nani calls from the kitchen and the ending says whether Ali or Nana is wanted | *Tu ki aiye?* → *… Aai ki aayo?* (Nana) or *… Tu ki aiye?* (Ali). *Hida ach!* / *Hida acho!* (§21) |
| 2 | **Will you make me…?** | Questions, respect | The order given. Heard from L1 as the order's opening line. It decides when the roles swap: Nana cooks, or the child takes the order | The child's two asks differ only in the "you"; who is in front of them decides. The answer fills the card | *Aai muke chai banai dinda?* / *Tu muke …* (§27). *Anke kuro khapeto?* / *Toke kuro khapeto?* (§23) |
| 3 | **Whose cup?** | "To me" verbs | The order given: two people at the counter, one speaks | The dish never changes. Only the first word says who it is for: me, her, us. The face label goes on that cup (two for "us") | *Muke chai khapeti* / *Inke chai khapeti* / *Panke chai khapeti* (§58) |
| 4 | **The full no** | No, don't, without | The order given, in the person's own words | Two people order the same dish; one sentence has the "no". Once the pattern is past support its row on the card looks like a "want" row, so it must be heard | *Muke khun nati khape* (§11). "Don't put onion in my daar": **Mum** (parts: §12, §65) |
| 5 | **One for Ma too** | Also, only | The order given, last line | The same order ends with or without "too": make one, or one more. Later "only" against "too" | *Ma lai pan hakro banai* (§8). "Only for Ma": **Mum** (*kali*, §43) |
| 6 | **Say it back** | "To me" verbs, no, this and that (said) | The order checked: a new step between the order and the cooking | The child confirms with one of two read-backs, alike but for the pattern, turned from "me" to "you". The person nods or says it again. A mistake is mended here, not found at the serve | Nana: "I don't want sugar." Child: "You don't want sugar?" **Mum** (*anke*, §23, with §11). "Not this, that": *hi na, hu* (§13) |
| 7 | **Nani's three questions** | Questions | A pause in the cooking; the shops; the clinic | The question word decides whether the answer is a person, a thing or a number; the three bubbles are one of each | *Ker ai?* / *Hi kuro ai?* / *Kitla?* (§23) |
| 8 | **This or that?** | "Or"; the polite no | Nani's treat after the serve; the host at a party | Nani offers two things. The child picks or says one and gets exactly that, so a mis-heard pick shows itself kindly. Any polite answer is right: practice, never counted as proof | "Do you want chai, or milk?" **Mum** (*ke*, §9; *Toke chai khapeti?*, §58) → *Na, muke na khape* (§30) |
| 9 | **Let me, you, let's** | Let's, let me, shall I | The taste at the serve; any shared job | Who acts. "Let me taste": hand Nani the spoon. "Taste it": the child does. "Let's": both. Nothing else in the scene says which | *Muke chakhan lai de* (§27); *Kha!* (§27). "Let's eat": **Mum** |
| 10 | **He or she? Hand it over** | He, she (verb endings); later the past | The serve; the thanks; the picnic; the party | Ali and the older cousin (a girl) wait. Nani, in one voice, says a line where "he" and "she" are the same word; only the ending tells. The child hands the plate to that one. Past version: a mango arrives, Nani says who gave it, the child thanks that one | *E roj maani khaito* / *khaiti* (§62). *E … ambo dini* (§56); "gave it to you": **Mum** |
| 11 | **Say it as you** | "I" forms; the future | Nani's "will you help me cook?" at the start of the day | Two replies that differ only in the ending, a boy's and a girl's. The right one is the child's own. Both play in one voice | *Ha, aau randhan lai madad kar dos* / *… kar dis* (§32) |
| 12 | **The big what?** | He-words and she-words | The serve; picky shopping; the fruit stall | The person names no thing, only "the big". A big cup and a big maani are both there; the endings pick | *Muke wadhi khapeti* → the maani; *wadho khan* (§40, §43, §58). She-form of "take the big": **Mum** |
| 13 | **Who's come, who's coming?** | What happened against what will happen | The door; laying the tray | No "now" or "later" in the line. The verb alone says who is here and who is on the way: pour for those here, keep a cup aside for the rest | *E achi vyo* / *e achdo*; for an elder *achi vya / achda* (§21) |
| 14 | **We'll make it, you made it** | Future and past (heard only) | The first and last line of every round | Nothing to decide: the same verb opens the round in the future and closes it in the past, heard hundreds of times before it is ever tested | "We'll make chai." … "You made chai!" **Mum**. Near today: *Panke randhnu khapdo*; *Shabash, beta* (§33) |
| 15 | **Who needs it?** | Feelings; can, need | The clinic: the greeting and the doctor | Two patients, one medicine: "you", "I" or "she" needs it. Later a real answer to "how are you?" picks what helps, and "I can't" gets the helping hand | *Toke dawa ji jaruri* / *Inke …* (§58); *Aau nati winni saka* (§61). Hungry, cold, "she can't": **Mum** |

**Cut on the second pass, and why**

- **Dragging a written he-sentence and she-sentence to the right person:** it needs reading. Mechanic 10 keeps the idea by ear.
- **Two voices, one male and one female:** the voice gives the answer away before the grammar does. One voice says both.
- **Orders that change with the customer's gender:** they do not (section 1).
- **A graded greeting, order and thanks every round:** a toll before the fun. One graded move; the rest play at a rung the child owns.
- **"You made it" as a test at the serve:** the forms are not known yet, and the serve is a reward. Heard only (14).
- **Elders in mechanic 10 at first:** the respect form hides he and she (§21), so it would be a guess. Later, as the near miss.

## 5. Decide now, design later

The new engine should allow these from day one; each is cheap now and costly later. Yes to all, recommended, except …

1. **The order and the serve are conversation scenes**, run by the conversations module, not drawn by each game. Each has named slots: greeting, ask, check, question, options, thanks, goodbye. A game fills a slot or leaves it empty.
2. **A "check" slot between the order and the cooking** (mechanic 6), empty at first.
3. **An order is data about people, not only about food:** who spoke, who it is for (me, you, him or her, us, them), each person's gender, age band (elder or child) and number, and a "no" on any part.
4. **The player's own gender reaches the language engine**, for "I will" and "I can".
5. **A grammar ladder beside the word ladder:** a rung per pattern per child, shared across every game, moved only by a move the pattern decided.
6. **The order card can hold back a pattern as it holds back a word:** shown plainly while supported, then only heard. Without this a reader wins "the full no" from the card.
7. **A scene can have two or three people in it**, each able to be handed a thing, called, or thanked.
8. **Choices in one voice and one picture** is a rule of the module, checked by the leak bot; always picking one side wins half at most.
9. **"No" as a full sentence from L2**, replacing the bare "no sugar" row there (it changes H23; your newest word wins). L1 keeps today's row.
10. **The child's model voice for mechanic 11 is one voice for both forms**, an exception to "Zafar for a boy, Mum for a girl".

Later: which mechanic goes in which game first, the art, the reactions, the speaking rungs.

## 6. Recordings this needs

For the one recording sheet, in this order. All **Mum to confirm**.

1. "I don't want sugar / milk / onion" as whole sentences, and "Don't put onion in my daar."
2. "You don't want sugar?" to a child and to an elder; "You want two?" (the read-backs).
3. "He wants chai" and "she wants chai" said about someone standing there; "Make chai for her."
4. "Do you want chai, or milk?"; "This one, or that one?"
5. "Will you make me …?" with each dish not yet recorded; the same from the child to an elder.
6. "We'll make chai" / "We'll make maani"; "You made chai!"; "Who made this?"
7. "He eats", "she eats", then the same for drink, make, cut, bring, want to go (the ladder's ten verbs), for he, she, they and an elder.
8. "She gave it to you"; "He gave it to you"; "Who gave you this?"
9. "Take the big one" for a she-thing; "I want the small one" for both.
10. "Nana has come"; "Ali will come"; "They will come."
11. "She can't walk"; "He needs medicine"; "I'm hungry / cold / tired".
12. "Let's eat"; "Shall I help?"; "Only for Ma"; "You too?"
13. Both "I'll help" forms in Mum's one voice, for mechanic 11.
