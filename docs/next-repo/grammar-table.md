# The grammar table: 28 patterns across the 24 modes

For Zafar, 17 Nov 2026. Step 2 of the first night. It joins `grammar-ladder.md` (the 28 patterns), `grammar-in-play.md` (the mechanics and the four rungs) and `games-map-review.md` (the 24 modes). It is a plan, not a design: nothing built changes because of this page. No Kutchi is written here; the forms are in the ladder.

## How to read it

- **Primary** means the pattern decides the move: two choices alike but for the pattern. **Secondary or heard** means it is spoken and used, but something else decides.
- **The number after a mode is the highest rung that mode reaches:** 1 heard, 2 decides between two, 3 near miss among three, 4 the child says it. Rung 4 waits for "You be Nani" everywhere.
- **The letter is the status of that placement:** **a** actual (the built game does it today, by the modes table), **p** planned (designed, or agreed by Zafar on 16-17 Nov), **s** suggested (new on this page).
- **"Actual" is generous.** The ladder's own check says most built orders are won on the nouns or read off the card. An "a" here means the pattern is in the built game as its deciding thing, not that it has passed the leak test.
- **The shared order and serve scenes** count for the mode whose round they sit in, and are marked "(order)" or "(serve)".
- **Rows 25 and 26 are one move:** "done" against "not yet", two-way only. They are placed together and counted once in a mode's load.
- **Rows 22 and 23 are never primary,** by the ladder's own rule (heard only). They are the one planned exception to "primary in two modes".

## Against the two targets

**Every pattern not waiting on Mum is primary in at least two modes: met for 21 of 21.** Two rows (22, 23) are heard only on purpose. Five rows (12, 13, 15, 27, 28) wait on Mum and have a placeholder home only.

**No mode over about four: met.** The fullest are at four. Talk back holds four moves, one of which is the tense pair.

Honest weight of the "two modes": for rows 2, 5, 9, 17, 20, 21 and 24, **both** homes are suggestions; nothing is built or designed yet.

**How the agreed decisions show here**

- **Chai** (Pour to order): its middle keeps what it has (milk or none, half or full). Its only new grammar is "Whose cup?" in the shared order and serve, and the two heard bookends. The sugar count is a number word, so one-and-many is listed as heard there, not primary.
- **Eggs** (the pan): Nani speaks for everyone, so "wants" about someone else, "for Nana" and the owner word decide who gets which egg. Fried and boiled are two items on the word ladder, not a grammar row.
- **Tenses:** heard at rung 1 in every cooking round. They decide, two-way, only at the door, the table and tray, Masi's call and Work out who.
- **The pantry** carries the one-and-many endings; **the serve** carries "me against her".

## The gaps, plainly

1. **Five patterns have no game and cannot have one yet** (12, 13, 15, 27, 28). Three of them (there is and "I have", feelings, right now) are early patterns a child needs first.
2. **Seven patterns stand on suggestions only** (2, 5, 9, 17, 20, 21, 24). The target is met on paper, not in any game.
3. **Almost nothing truly decides today.** Built orders are won on the nouns or read from the card. Until the card can hold back a pattern (grammar-in-play, decision 6), every "a" in the table is unproven by the leak test.
4. **Nothing reaches rung 4.** Speaking waits for "You be Nani". Only Talk back and Describe it have a rung 4 at all, and Describe it is an idea, not a game.
5. **Tenses decide in few places and none are built.** The door, the table, Masi's call and Work out who are all planned. The bookends themselves ("we'll make", "you made") are not recorded.
6. **Row 14 rests on one verb.** Only "eats" is known for he, she, they and I. Both its homes are stuck until Mum gives the other verbs.
7. **Two thin modes.** The clock and the window ride have one primary each, both suggested. They sit below the brief's band of two to four and would need a "starter" mark or a second pattern.
8. **Three modes exist only as ideas or designs,** and carry real weight here: Remember it (row 5), Describe it (rows 2, 10, 24), Work out who (rows 8, 25). If they slip, row 5 falls to one home.
9. **Crowded rows.** Commands, he- and she-words and whose are each primary in five modes; one-and-many, positions, questions, linking and how much in four. That is where counting and size already crowd the word map. When sheets are written, the first to step back to secondary are: commands in Treat it, whose in Count, how much in Count.
10. **The order scene is busy.** Whose cup, the full no, "one for Ma too" and the read-back all sit in it. The rule of one graded move a round holds only if the runner picks one; that is a rule to build, not yet built.
11. **Respect has no cooking home.** Customers differ by age, but the child chooses the form only in Talk back and Who gets it.
12. **The table's "actual" marks come from the old modes table,** which used the first 15-row ladder. Rows 2, 5, 9, 17, 20, 21 and 24 were never tagged on any game, so their "heard" cells are my reading, to be checked as each sheet is written.

## Questions for Mum, ranked by what they unblock

1. **The ten everyday verbs for he, she, they, I and an elder; and whether "is eating now" differs from "eats every day".** Unblocks row 14 (two homes), row 15, half of row 28, and the doing-word levels in Snap and the farm photos. The biggest single ask.
2. **"We'll make chai" and "You made chai!"; "Nana has come", "Ali will come", "They will come".** Unblocks rows 25 and 26: the bookends heard in every cooking round, then the door and the table.
3. **"He wants chai", "she wants chai" about someone standing there; "make it for her", "for him".** Unblocks eggs (the third game) and the serve's "me against her"; settles whether him and her can ever decide.
4. **"There is", "there isn't", "I have", "have you got?"** Unblocks row 12: the shops, the pantry, Pack the bag, What's missing.
5. **Hungry, cold, tired, "it hurts", "I know", "I don't know"; "she can't".** Unblocks row 13 and the second home of row 16: the whole clinic conversation.
6. **Later, today, tomorrow, always, never, o'clock.** Unblocks most of row 21: getting ready, the clock, the evening book.
7. **"I don't want sugar" as a whole sentence; "You don't want sugar?" to a child and to an elder.** Unblocks the full no and the read-back (rows 3, 4, 9) in every order.
8. **Because, so, if, when; "the one with milk", "the girl who is eating".** Unblocks rows 27 and 28. Late patterns, so lower, but nothing can be designed without them.
9. **"Take the big one" for a she-thing; "bigger than".** Finishes rows 10 and 24: Snap, sewing, the shops.
10. **"Let's eat", "shall I help?", "only for Ma", "you too?", very, again.** Finishes rows 17 and 20.
11. **The elder form of the command verbs; "was" for she-things.** Finishes rows 9 and 5.
