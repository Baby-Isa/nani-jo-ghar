# Review of the six mode designs (25 Sept 2026)

**What this is.** An independent, critical read of `docs/find-it-design.md` and the five designs in `docs/modes/` (Tidy up, Who did it?, Dress up, Monsoon rush, Snap), for Zafar to decide before build agents launch. The clinic is being redesigned and is out of scope except where it overlaps. Judged against the brief, the Cook Kutchi audit, the Roadmap, and the repo as it stands (`js/cook/*` is the engine, `js/find/*` the first slice on it; there is **no** `js/shared/`, no `data/relations.json`, and `data/scenes/` holds only `bazaar.json` and `kitchen.json`).

**The short version.** All six designs are good, disciplined about leaks, and structurally alike: one engine, rows as data, a hint ladder, a leak bot. The problems sit between them, not inside them: (1) only three slices are a real Kutchi test today, (2) four modes depend on a shared relations file nobody owns yet, and (3) Arc 3 Ch1 and the "daily minute" are claimed by nearly every mode.

## Find it

**How it plays.** Nani's list appears as rows on the order ladder; the player pans a stall or room and taps the thing meant; right taps arc into the basket, wrong ones wiggle with a recast; Done hands the basket over, and the shopkeeper sometimes packs one wrong thing for the player to catch. A round: *bo santra, hikdo limu, no kelo*; the stall holds oranges, lemons, peaches and bananas; tap two oranges and one lemon, leave the bananas, press Done; the shopkeeper's bag holds a peach instead of the lemon; tap the peach and hand it back.

**Verdict and conditions.** Go, with seven open questions rather than conditions. The design itself says M2 and M3 can't be audited until Round 1 Q3 (positions) and Q11 (colours) come back.

**Critique.**
- *Fun.* For a 5-year-old, tap-the-thing is the right verb and the openables, cats and torch are the best juice in the set. For an 11-year-old the first slice is thin: M1 is the Shopping errand rebuilt. The mode earns its keep at M4 (Simba's mischief) and the torch.
- *Forces Kutchi.* M1 is well guarded (no labels, look-alike groups, Done, over-collecting graded). Check in the live slice: the handoff notes a "small number beside each row" that may be the target, not the tally (a High leak if so).
- *Distinct.* M1 = Cook's pantry fetch on a bigger stage; M2 = Tidy up's relations, found instead of placed; M3 = the same "adjective + noun" decision as Dress up D1, Snap M4 and Who did it "is" clues. Find it's real identity is M4 and M8, which come last and wait on words.
- *Build risk.* Panning scenes, occluders cut from backgrounds and openables are a new art discipline; 375 px phones are marginal. The scene schema (anchors, spots, openables) exists in neither scene file yet.
- *Missing.* An owner for `data/relations.json`; Tidy up says "whoever builds first owns it", which is how the Wave 2 merge cost two hours.

**Recommendation.** Build as designed. The next slice should be **M3 with size rows** (*vadho / nindho* are drafted, so "the big one / the small one" is a Kutchi test now), not M2. Adopt Wave 5A's sidebar first. Make the Find it agent the owner of the relations file and the scene `spots` schema, since it already reads scene data.

**Decisions.** Q1 (does the fruit bowl move onto this engine) blocks the shell work, not the mode. Q5 (panning scenes or one screen with zoom) blocks the sitting-room art. Q2 (sharp-eye star) and Q3 (ear leniency) can be defaulted.

## Tidy up

**How it plays.** A board (a surface with uniform spots) and a tray of items; Nani's rows are rules ("cup in front of Nana", "red plate in the middle"); tap an item, tap a spot; nothing is refused; press Done and Nani checks rule by rule, recasting the first wrong one for the player to fix. A round of *Put the shopping away*: the tray holds *santra, limu, kelo, dungri* and a spare; rows say *santra* in the bowl, *dungri* on the second shelf, *bo limu* in the bowl; place them, press Done, Nani hops each item as she says its row.

**Verdict and conditions.** Go with changes: (1) the relation file and checker built once for Find it and Tidy up; (2) position, left/right, colour and tableware words before M1 is audited; (3) Zafar confirms the dastarkhwan camera (the design wants the kept high angle, the art bible says top-down).

**Critique.**
- *Fun.* The rule-graded layout (grade rules, not slots; several layouts are right) is the best single design idea across the six. For a 5-year-old, level 1's live check and tap-tap are right, but "several layouts satisfy the rules" is an adult's pleasure; Layla needs 2–3 rows and one obvious surface. For an 11-year-old M10 rule riddles and the mehndi canvas are strong, and the doc's own Zayn note ("it's just putting away") applies to M2, the mechanic proposed first.
- *Forces Kutchi.* Guards G1–G8 and the five solver checks (solvable, ≥3 options, convention fails, no forced row, flat priors) are the most rigorous anti-leak spec of the six. But what is real today: in M2 only the **noun** has Kutchi; "top shelf / on / in" are English. M3 shoes and M4 sweet box are **0% Kutchi** today (shoes, colours, sweets, corner, top row are placeholders; only counts are real). OVERVIEW lists M2 as a working Kutchi test; it is half of one.
- *Distinct.* Arrange is a real verb. Two overlaps: M2 versus Find it's end-of-round recall (both re-test the basket after shopping), and M1's "which cup goes to Nana" versus Cook's Chai tray. M8 pens duplicate Monsoon's M3.
- *Build risk.* Solver and spot graph are cheap. The risk is the first set's size: engine + four boards + lab + bot + 30 edit-in-place images + three new scenes (doorway T, sweet box T, sitting room H). Edit-in-place tableware on the high-angle cloth is flagged by the design itself.
- *Missing.* How the board reads at 915×375 with items + 3 spots, a ≤22% tray and a sidebar.

**Recommendation.** Build with changes: first slice is **engine + M2 only**, on the existing kitchen shelves and existing item art, with M1 dastarkhwan second. Hold M3 and M4 until sweets, shoes and colours have Kutchi. Drop M8 (pens) from Tidy up entirely. Do not start until the relations file exists.

**Decisions.** Dastarkhwan camera (blocks the art brief, not the greybox). Unmentioned items free (#13), check timing (#12), craft icon: default them (yes, live at L1 only, broom). Mehndi ownership (#7) and sweets (#6) can wait.

## Who did it?

**How it plays.** Suspects stand behind the sofa; Nani gives one spoken clue at a time; the player taps everyone who still fits and presses Done; the rest sit down; when one is left, point and the caught reaction plays. A level-1 round: three sweets missing, Simba, Zazu and Kasuku in the line-up; "*It had* **hardar** *on its paws*"; the player taps Zazu with the magnifier, sees yellow paws, taps Zazu; "*the one with the* **kelo**"; Simba is holding a banana; tap Simba; the third clue names Kasuku; three caught moments, case book.

**Verdict and conditions.** Go with changes: (1) W1 built with Kutchi-real clue types first (traces, held items); (2) describing words, yes/no and kinship before W2 and W5 count; (3) settle overlaps (W8 with Snap, W9 with Tidy up, Footprints).

**Critique.**
- *Fun.* Guess Who plus the cats' caught moments is a proven shape and the reactions carry it. For a 5-year-old, L1 is honest: one clue picks one suspect, so it is "pass me" with people, and the design says so. Real deduction starts at L2; "Prove it" at L3 is for 8+; the doc's own worry ("feels like a quiz") is right for L2 without pace. The 15% **no-op clue** at L2 (press Done, change nothing) will confuse a 5–7-year-old; start it at L3.
- *Forces Kutchi.* The balanced line-up, exact-set commits, lucky-guess rule and solver are excellent, and the Node leak bot (no browser) is the best test plan of the six. The phase-1 honesty is right: the frame is English, the noun decides, and hardar / atto / tameto exist in `data/cook.json`.
- *Distinct.* Combining clues is unique. W2 (Who's at the door) is Find it's rejected M10 reskinned as a photo wall; its L1 (one feature, 6 frames) is a which-one task, and only L3 kinship is new. W4 Look closer is a peek, close to Find it's openables.
- *Build risk.* Lowest of the six: phase 0 needs no art and no browser; suspects are base + overlays + tints, an upper-body version of Dress up's paper doll. Build the overlay-at-anchor system **once** and share it with Dress up (glasses, caps and scarves are on both lists).
- *Missing.* Busy mode appears only in loop 3; nothing says what the patience ring drains on. W2 needs a doorway scene that Tidy up's shoe mountain also needs, at a different camera (E versus T): two scene files, named so they don't collide.

**Recommendation.** Build with changes: phases 0–1 only (`case.js`, the Node bot, the greybox line-up with traces and held items, L1–2); no-op clues from L3; W5 into the lab with a grey ear until yes/no exist; W2 after the door scene and kinship words. Smallest slice: task 1 (pure logic) alone is a day's work and proves the mode.

**Decisions.** Culprit pool (#1) is data, not blocking; Kasuku silent suspect: default yes. #18 Prove it: keep, L3 only. #5 (a new Ch2 errand) can wait until phase 3.

## Dress up

**How it plays.** A client asks in Kutchi for pieces by garment and colour; the player taps them from Big Ma's rail onto a full-body figure, does one hands task (iron, stitch), presses Done, and the client checks each piece in the mirror. A round: Nana says "*Muke* [white] [kurta] *khape. Ne* [green] [topi]"; the rail shows kurtas in white, green and blue and caps in white, green and red; tap the white kurta and the green cap; iron a crease; Done; the mirror names each piece.

**Verdict and conditions.** Go with changes: family colour, clothes and weather words before the ear star is judged; a two-garment alignment test before any art; first set only (D1 + D5, D3 then D2, D4).

**Critique.**
- *Fun.* The mirror reveal, the ironing (Layla's favourite in the loops) and D2/D8/the lookbook give this mode the strongest ownership hook for an 11-year-old of any design. For a 5-year-old, D1 L1 is fine but calm; binding at L2 only is right.
- *Forces Kutchi.* The only mode with **no Kutchi-real slice at all**: clothes, colours, patterns and weather are all placeholders, and size words are excluded because the body shows them. The per-round **blind-odds budget** (compute the best blind strategy, add rows or decoys until ≤5%) is the best generator idea in the set and belongs in the shared engine.
- *Distinct.* Composing a person is distinct in feel, but D1's language decision (colour + noun from a rack sorted by type) is Find it's M3 with no search. D4 weather is the unique part (S4): four states plus colour rows to make the budget.
- *Build risk.* The largest art risk in the project: full-body characters exist nowhere yet (the art bible is upper body behind counters); ~125 images, 45 garments aligned to three bases, modesty rules, and Eid morning needs four full-body clients before the first story errand. New engine: doll renderer, tint, pattern masks, mirror mask, weather reveal.
- *Missing.* What the first story errand costs in art before it can ship; what happens to the roster if the family says the clothes are wrong (#15).

**Recommendation.** Build with changes, and **later than third**: do task 1 (generator, grader, rack, bot) now if an agent is spare, since it is pure logic and has no file overlap, but no greybox art or story until Round 1 Q9 and Q11 come back from the visit. Keep D3 in Arc 1 Ch4 minimal (one motif on one part). Cut D2 from the first set (Arc 2). Smallest slice: logic + greybox D1 L1 + iron.

**Decisions.** #15 (what the family wears; *topi* or *kofia*) blocks the garment list and therefore all art. #14 (weather heard only; dress the player; persistent outfits): default yes / later / no. #13 free choices: yes, ungraded.

## Monsoon rush

**How it plays.** Nani calls a place or thing in Kutchi; every candidate shows the same countdown (all the stains swell); the player acts before the reveal; the drop falls on the beat and plinks into the bucket or splashes. Only answers before the reveal count for the ear. A kitchen-slice round: six pots on the island (*dudh, paani, dai, khun, loon, atto*, a whole look-alike group); "*Hedo! Dudh!*"; tap the milk pot, a lid drops, the drip plinks off it; "*Khun! Ne loon!*" a double; the tempo steps up every four waves; the storm passes, word review.

**Verdict and conditions.** Go with changes: (1) kitchen slice first; (2) family words before rooms, weather and animals count; (3) one courtyard scene file shared with Find it.

**Critique.**
- *Fun.* Anticipation on a beat, escalating tempo, near-misses and the cats: the best Zayn hook, and Drizzle (no timer, the drop waits) makes it playable at 5 with a parent calling. Adult density (12–18 words a minute from L2) is three times Cook's. "Every drop saved" answers Maryam's "babyish flooding" well.
- *Forces Kutchi.* The strongest argument of the six: the shared countdown plus the "heard first" rule means the world cannot tell you before Nani does, and one-answer-per-target with buckets only during a live call closes camping and spam. The kitchen slice is 100% real Kutchi today with **no new art** and no new words: the cheapest honest proof in the plan. The caveat: this slice is Cook's "pass me" under a beat; the mode's identity (rooms, positions, weather) is all placeholders.
- *Distinct.* React is distinct. But M3 Into the shed duplicates Tidy up's M8 (same scene, animals, words), and M2 Unpeg, Dress up D4 and Tidy up's washing sort all claim Arc 3 Ch1.
- *Build risk.* Audio-clock scheduling with a per-clip `keyAt`, a virtual clock for headless tests, phone audio latency, and the house cross-section as one big art item. The 80% ear threshold is a new rule (see decisions).
- *Missing.* How Busy misses feed word stages: "two misses drop a stage" could demote a known word in one fast storm; misses under time pressure should count less than in Drizzle.

**Recommendation.** Build as designed and **move it up the order**: its first three tasks touch no shared file (put the `monsoon` block in a sidecar, not in `kitchen.json`), need no art and no words, and produce a real-Kutchi mode in days. Smallest slice: their tasks 1–3.

**Decisions.** #11 ear rule: not blocking (data knob; default 80%, revisit after a playtest). #16 rooms: blocks L2 data and the cross-section art only. Goats in the kitchen and Big Ma's song (#2): wait. The menu-words tick: default a list and let Zafar edit it.

## Snap

**How it plays.** The player pans a wide living scene under a fixed viewfinder, waits for the moment asked for, and shoots; nothing says at the shutter whether it's right; at the end Nani asks for each row again in a new order and the player hands her a print. A farm round: rows *trae aamo*, [goat] [eating], [hen] on the charpai; zoom on a branch until exactly three mangoes are in frame, shoot; wait for the grazing goat, shoot; hand in: Nani says *trae aamo*, tap the print with three.

**Verdict and conditions.** Go with changes: S4/S6 words before M4–M6; seed the album from Arc 1 beat photos; build after Find it and reuse its schema, courtyard and bot; confirm chapter splits.

**Critique.**
- *Fun.* The hand-in, photobombs, the rail and Then and now are lovely and the album is a real collection. For a 5-year-old it is three controls (pan, zoom, shutter) plus moving subjects; the mitigations (tap-to-centre, long holds, pan the world not the frame) are right but untested. For 8–11 it is the richest replay design here.
- *Forces Kutchi.* Strong on paper (hand-in, main-subject rule, film = rows + 2, states asked uniformly). But only M2 "exactly N" is real today, and fruit counts are already tested by Cook and Find it; this would be the third count mechanic.
- *Distinct.* Moving subjects and framing rules (exactly N, together, without) are genuinely new. M1 and M3 are Find it with motion, as the design admits.
- *Build risk.* The heaviest engine of the six: state machines on path graphs, herds, a geometric evaluator with occlusion and parallax, a rail, `snapshotArea` prints, an HTML album, ~130 images. Performance on an iPad or phone (2–4 screen panoramas, dozens of animated sprites, per-shot snapshots) is unmeasured. It sits in Arc 5, which the design itself lists as a risk ("may never be reached").
- *Missing.* Nothing important in the design; what's missing is a reason to build it now.

**Recommendation.** Rethink the timing, not the design. Do not spawn an agent. The one cheap thing worth doing early is the **album screen** seeded from beat photos (HTML, tiny, sits in the shell), which the design itself proposes. Revisit Snap when Find it's courtyard, the animal words and Arc 3 exist.

**Decisions.** #4 (young Nani, camera or phone, vehicle, Kasuku) and #8/#9 (footprints, sky) block nothing now.

### Shared engine pieces to build once

1. **Stabilise Cook's shared modules.** Every design says "load `js/cook/{lang,ui,order,core,zone}.js`, never copy". Wave 5A is rewriting `ui.js`, `flow.js` and `order.js` right now (intro card, "?" help, word review, sidebar). Five agents coding against an API changing under them is the one clash that hits all of them. **Wave 5A must merge before any mode agent starts**, and the intro card, ladder row, help-cost and word-review calls should be a frozen interface (a one-page `docs/shared-api.md` would cost an hour and save days).
2. **Relations and scene spots** (`data/relations.json`, `js/shared/rel.js`, `anchors`/`spots`/`occluders`/`safe` keys in scene JSON). Needed by Find it M2, Tidy up, Monsoon L2+, Snap. Owner: the Find it agent, as a one-day pre-task, with Tidy up's rule types as the spec.
3. **Star sets and ear rules as data** (`star_sets.<mode>`, `earPass`, `minTested`, taught-rows exclusion), and the `menu_words` flag on words.
4. **Attribute decoy rules and blind odds** (from Dress up 8.4 and Find it's look-alike groups) as one module.
5. **Overlay-at-anchor sprites** (Who did it suspects, Dress up doll): one system, upper body first.
6. **The audio manifest** `dur` and `keyAt` fields (Monsoon only; small, but it edits `build/build_audio_manifest.py`).

**Must "one app, one save" come first?** Not for the first slices: Find it already proves a mode can live on its own page using Cook's save and progress, and every design plans the same. It **does** have to come before story integration (beats, map places, one wallet, story replays) and before any art run, and the longer it waits the more mode pages carry their own save keys to migrate. The right shape: one agent does the shell (owning `index.html`, `js/shell.js`, `js/storage.js`, `js/progress.js`, `js/cook/core.js`, `cook.html`) **in parallel** with mode agents doing pure-logic and greybox phases that don't touch those files; then a one-day integration.

### Build order

OVERVIEW proposes Tidy up → Who did it → Dress up → Monsoon / clinic → Snap, on story grounds. Ranked by what is a real Kutchi test today, which is the project's own first rule, it reads differently: Monsoon kitchen slice (all real, no art) > Who did it traces (the noun decides, no art) > Find it M3-size (drafts) > Tidy up M2 (the noun half) > Snap M2 (counts, already tested elsewhere) > Dress up (nothing). Proposed order:

1. Now, in parallel: merge Wave 5A; the shell agent ("one app, one save"); the Find it agent builds the relations file and M3-size.
2. In parallel with 1: **Monsoon kitchen slice** and **Who did it phases 0–1** (both pure logic first, disjoint files, real Kutchi).
3. **Tidy up M2 then M1**, once the relations file has landed.
4. **Dress up** logic now if an agent is free; greybox and art only after the family visit returns colours and clothes.
5. Snap: Arc 5; album screen early via the shell.

Arc 1 still needs Tidy up (Ch1–3), Who did it (Ch2–3) and Dress up (Ch4–5); this order delays Arc 1's last two chapters by a few weeks in exchange for two real-Kutchi modes and a shared engine that never needs re-merging.
