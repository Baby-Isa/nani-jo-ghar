# Nani jo Ghar — Questions for Mum (Round 4)

28 Sept 2026 · Round 3 went brilliantly: the re-takes, the conversations, the first story, one-and-more-than-one and the first grammar sentences are all done, thank you! This round puts **the cooking game first**, then **the grammar sentences**, then everything else.

*Everything below is in English. It's not meant to be done in one go. Parts 1 to 3 are the heart of it (about 25 minutes); then Section C carries on the grammar (about 20 minutes). After that, stop whenever you like.*

## How to answer

- **Record one long voice file** on the phone, in a quiet room. One sitting or several, it doesn't matter.
- **Before each part or section, say its name out loud** ("Part 2", "Section C"…), and before a numbered line say its number ("N5"). That's how the recording gets lined up with this sheet.
- **For Parts 1 to 4: say each line three times**, with a gap of about two seconds after each, the way you'd say it to a grandchild. The gaps let each one be cut out on its own.
- **For the sentences in Section C:** say each one once, naturally, with a short gap after it.
- **Not sure? Say "skip".** **More than one way to say it? Say them all.** **If the family just uses the English word**, say so: that's a real answer.
- The right-hand columns are for Zafar to fill in afterwards.

**Rough times:** Part 1 about 5 minutes · Part 2 about 10 · Part 3 about 10 · Section C about 20 · Part 4 about 10 · Section I about 20–30 (a chat) · then E, G, F, H, J.

### I36. Which dishes are really ours?

These are the dishes we'd like in the game, a mix of Kutch cooking and the East African food we grew up with. For each: do you really make it, what's it called at home, and what would you add, remove or change? Which are more Kenya or Tanzania, and which are more Kutch? And what's missing that you'd expect in your kitchen?

Chai · maani / rotli · daal · chaat bowl (chana bateta) · samosa · mishkaki with chips · chips mayai · mogo · makai (corn on the cob) · mandazi · dahi puri / sev puri · dabeli · falooda · jalebi · sheer khurma · biryani / pilau

For the ones you really make, name five to ten things that go in each, and say if it belongs to an occasion (Eid morning, everyday dinner, guests, a rainy day).

Answer notes:

### I37. How you make the first six

For each, talk through what goes in and in what order, what you do at each step, and how you know it's ready. This becomes the recipe in the game.

1. **Chai:** water, tea, boil, milk, sugar? Masala, elchi, ginger? When do they go in?
2. **Maani / rotli:** flour, water, oil or ghee? How thin? When do you flip it? Ghee on top?
3. **Daal:** which daal? What goes in the tadka and in what order (jeeru, rai, hing, garlic, chilli…)? Onion, tomato?
4. **Chaat bowl:** every topping, in the usual order.
5. **Samosa:** which filling? How do you fold it?
6. **Mishkaki:** which meat, what else goes on the skewer, the marinade, the chips on the side with chilli and lemon?

Answer notes:

### I38. Who likes what

In the game everyone has their own taste and the child has to listen. Tell us the real ones, silly ones included: who takes three sugars, who has no milk, who won't touch chilli, who always wants extra onion, who hates what.

Answer notes:

### I39. A real chai order, in one breath

Finally, say two or three complete orders the way they'd be said at home: "chai with two sugars, no milk, half a cup", "two maani and daal, no onion", "one veg skewer and one mixed, with chips". These are the sentences the game will copy.
