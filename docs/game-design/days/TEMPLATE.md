# A trip: the template, and what good looks like

Every trip file follows this shape (Zafar, 31 Oct: "so the agents know what a good trip looks like"). Part A is the template, Part B the quality bar, Part C the farm cast in it as the worked example. It replaces the old section list in `README.md` §6. The principles in `README.md` §1 still hold (a day is data, a stage is an engine plus settings, every day ends at the book).

**A trip is designed language first.** Pick what the child should hear and say, then the story that needs those words, then the games where the words decide the move. A fun game that the words don't drive is cut, however good.

---

## Part A: the template

### 0. One line

What, where, the weather, the host, which visit (first, second ...), status, sources (newest wins).

### 1. The language goal

What this trip teaches. Every row gets an ID (O1, O2 ...) so the stages below can point at it.

| ID | Kind | Target | New this trip | Comes back from earlier days |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| O1 | Main topic (1-2 per trip) | the topic, and its core words in English | the words first met here | words already met that are reused |
| O2 | Supporting topic (1-2) | | | |
| O3 | Verbs (3-6, from the top verb tiers) | each verb and the form used (command, "wants to", past in the book) | | |
| O4 | Small words: one grammar point (positions, numbers, question words, describing words, owners) | | | |
| O5 | Frames heard (listening) | whole sentences the child hears again and again | | |
| O6 | Frames said (speaking) | the closed sets the child says: 2-3 options that sound different | | |

Then three numbers: **new words** (each one costs Mum's recording time; keep it small, see Part B), **words that come back**, and **speaking turns**. Tag each word ✓ recorded or "to record", never invented. The word targets come from the language-targets research (`build/reports/`, 31 Oct).

### 2. The story

Five beats, in the child's world, a few lines each. The story is why the words are needed.

1. **The reason to go:** a need or an invitation, said by Nani at the door. Someone needs help, something is missing, a visit is promised.
2. **Getting there:** the bag and the ride; something to watch for on the way.
3. **Helping:** two or three jobs for the host, each a game; the child is useful, never tested.
4. **The surprise:** one joke, twist or reveal that the child remembers and retells (the chick under the hen).
5. **Home:** the thing for Ali, what goes to the pantry or the shelf, the evening book.

Name the one feeling the trip leaves (proud, giggly, cosy) and how the end shows it.

### 3. The day's list

| # | Stage | Engine (built / designed / reskin / new) | Level | Objectives (O-IDs) | What the Kutchi decides | Skill (L, S) | The fun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Engine:** reuse a built engine where you can. Mark a reskin, and say what is new in it. A brand-new engine is marked **new**, at most one per trip.
- **Objectives:** every game stage names at least one O-ID. The door, the ride and the book can carry objectives too.
- **What the Kutchi decides:** the leak test in one line. Every thing on screen could be right, and only the heard word picks; or fewer are asked than shown, with learned decoys. If nothing is decided by the word, it isn't a game stage.
- **Skill:** L = listening, S = speaking, L+S = both.
- **The fun:** what makes this stage worth playing: a toy-like action, a funny reaction, a reveal, making something.

### 4. Conversation moments

At least two per trip, and at least one where the child speaks. For each one:

| Moment | Who | It plays out as | The child does | Closed set | Frame | O-IDs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Speaking always follows `docs/game-design/speaking.md`: a picture says what, a heard frame says how, the steps are watch, then the handover, then alone, and the set is closed. A frame is said by the child only after it has been heard in this trip or an earlier one.

### 5. Fun and novelty

- **The new thing:** one mechanic, twist or toy that no other trip has. Say why it fits this place.
- **The joke:** one funny moment, the same each visit so the child waits for it.
- **The collectible:** what goes in the book (photos, finds, a stamp).
- **Rhythm:** no two of the same engine back to back; a calm stage after a busy one; the length in stages.

### 6. Coverage check

| O-ID | Stages that teach it | Times heard in the day | Times said | Met again (which later day) |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Every objective is taught in at least two stages; every new word is heard at least three times in the day, in at least two stages; every speaking turn has been heard first. Any objective that fails is cut or given another stage.

### 7. People, the thing for Ali, what comes home

Who is there (real people only; likeness agreed first), what Ali gets, and which later day uses what comes home.

### 8. Words it needs

The full list from §1, ✓ recorded or "to record", with Mum's frames where known. Gaps go to Mum's next sheet (`docs/language/recording-sheet.md`); missing Kutchi is grey English while testing (non-negotiable 2).

### 9. Art it needs

Done, and to make (ChatGPT runs Zafar supervises, non-negotiable 8).

### 10. Later visits

The same trip harder (more things, positions, counts), and the next objectives it can carry (for example, past tense in the book, or the child gives the orders).

### 11. Zafar's answers and open questions

Dated, newest first.

## Part B: what good looks like

**A good trip:**
1. Teaches one or two clear topics, and you can say which in a sentence ("animals and their food, counting to five").
2. Has a story a four-year-old could retell: why we went, what we did, the funny bit, what we brought home.
3. Lets the heard word decide every move, and passes the leak test at every stage.
4. Has the child speak at least once, in a closed set, after hearing the frame.
5. Brings back words from earlier days, not only new ones.
6. Has one thing no other trip has.
7. Reuses engines, with at most one new engine.
8. Never tests or tells off: progress, not verdicts; no waiting for speech; undo until the step moves on (non-negotiable 7).
9. Keeps the family's rules: halal food; modest clothes; no religious markers from other faiths; no sweets, lollies or biscuits as rewards (mithai at a celebration is fine) (non-negotiable 9).
10. Has no written English for the child and no spoken English outside story mode (non-negotiable 3).

**Common faults:**
- **The word is a toll, not the move:** a fun game with a word question stuck in front of it. The research says this teaches less and gets skipped (`build/reports/research-mechanics-2026-10-31.md`, finding 1).
- **Too many new words:** a trip that needs 60 new recordings won't get made. Proposed: about 15-25 new words a first visit (to confirm with Zafar).
- **Open speaking:** "say what you see" can't be recognised. Always 2-3 options.
- **The same engine three times in a row,** or a reskin with nothing new in it.
- **Timers and speed** against listening; anything a child can lose by being slow.
- **English instructions,** or a game that can't be understood by watching first.
- **Invented Kutchi** in the doc. English only until Mum records it.

New words about 18; words coming back about 12 (✓ = its own family clip, checked against the data on 31 Oct); speaking turns two (one agreed, one proposed).

### 2. The story

1. **The reason to go:** Hannah Mami's hens are laying and the animals need feeding; she has asked for a helper. Nani at the door: it's sunny, today we go to the farm. **(Proposed:** the need is the template's addition; the agreed door line is "Today I want to go to the farm".)
2. **Getting there:** pack the hat and water for the sun; the car leaves town for the farm road, naming what passes.
3. **Helping:** feed the animals, collect the eggs, take the photos, help at the corn stall.
4. **The surprise:** lift a hen for her eggs and a chick is underneath.
5. **Home:** the animals go home, one egg for Ali, the rest to the pantry; the evening book with today's photos.

The feeling: useful and proud, a big helper on the farm.

**What the check shows (the template working):** O3 and O5 are thin, below three hearings in two stages. O6 (verbs) has no agreed stage at all, and the child says nothing after meeting Hannah Mami. Put to Zafar, not decided.
