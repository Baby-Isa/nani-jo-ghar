# Gemini's "written Kutchi" excerpts, checked against the family's words

> **For a friendly overview, read `kutchi-findings-summary-2026-10-05.md` first.** This file is the detail behind it, for building the game.

5 Oct 2026. Zafar asked Gemini for excerpts of written Kutchi, with translations and vocabulary (`Gemini_Kutchi_Excerps_1_to_5.docx`, uploaded 5 Oct; not kept in the repo). Claude respelled each one into our conventions and checked it against `grammar-notes.md` and `lexicon.md`.

**Status: AI-generated, unverified, not evidence** (rule G1; two AIs agreeing proves nothing). **None of this is the family's Kutchi.** The respelled column only makes Gemini's text comparable with ours, and nothing from it goes into the game. Each useful word becomes a yes/no question for Mum (`../mum-questions/Mum yes-no list (2026-10-05).md`).

**Respelling used:** a word-initial *v* becomes *w* (rule G4: *vadi* → *wadi*); the "went" helper *vayo / vayi / vaya* is written as the family writes it, *vyo / vai / vya* (§19); *chay* → *chai*; *mukhe* → *muke*; long vowels are doubled only where Gemini already marks them. Gemini's grammar and word choice are left as they are. **"Our word"** gives the family's word where the lexicon or grammar notes have one (§ = `grammar-notes.md`).

**Overall verdict.** Excerpt 1 and the market talk in Excerpt 2 look like a real rural Kutch register, with some Gujarati mixed in. Excerpts 3–5 are a formal, Sanskrit-heavy Gujarati/Hindi register in Kutchi clothing (*sanskruti, parampara, dharohar, paryavaran, maatrubhasha, chintan, vaidyakshastra*), which no grandmother uses with a five-year-old. There are clear signs of machine invention: Devanagari slips into the Roman text twice (*s संभाल*, *परिस्थिति*); "I" is spelt three ways (*Ma*, *Aao*, *Moo*); *kal* is "yesterday" in Excerpt 1 and "tomorrow" in Excerpts 2–3; *dhoriyo* means "ran" in Excerpt 1 but "Hurry up!" in Excerpt 5; and two excerpts are both labelled "Excerpt 3".
